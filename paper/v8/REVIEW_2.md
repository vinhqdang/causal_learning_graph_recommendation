# Referee report (blind read of main.pdf, 57 pp. incl. appendices; figures 1-3 inspected)

## 1. What I understood
- Problem: recommenders are trained on policy logs but should rank videos that are shown at random (KuaiRand random insertions); logged clicks mislead (non-examination, policy-chosen audiences).
- Idea: two "log-only" signals. LEAF = per-user lifelong, exposure-conditional lift/familiarity on tag/author/music of out-of-pool videos. DAX = click = examination(user, scenario) x attraction(item), item attractiveness by EM with tag shrinkage.
- Method: LEAF-LGN = LightGCN + 6 LEAF channels + popularity + policy-channel positives (BPR). DAX score is standardised per candidate list and added with weight gamma chosen on validation.
- Evaluation: KuaiRand-27K (and 1K), temporal cut-off, random log used only for validation/test, 5 user-level splits, paired and Nadeau-Bengio tests with Holm, ablations, permutation controls, training seeds.
- Finding: LEAF-LGN+DAX 0.4854 nDCG@10 vs 0.4634 for the authors' unpublished "QP family"; DAX adds ~+11 (x1e-3) to six backbones; shuffles remove the gains. I understood this, but only after the third read (see 5).

## 2. Motivation
Good running example (cooking clip) and a real problem. Gaps: (a) never says who needs "ranking randomly inserted items" (cold-start exploration? new-item promotion?) beyond one sentence in the intro; (b) "random insertion = direct test" is asserted, not argued: random items are a pool of ~7.6k videos in one scenario, labels are clicks, not satisfaction; (c) the claim "we found no method that..." rests on a self-defined task, so the gap is partly constructed by the protocol (random log forbidden for training).

## 3. Reproducibility of LEAF and DAX
Mostly implementable (Eqs. 1-15, Alg. 1-2 are a strength), but:
- Sec. 3.1: scenario set T, number of scenarios, which is t=1, definition of "click", "impression" and "pool" are never given; "first tag" only (44 tag values) is stated in App. D.2, not in 3.1.
- Eq. 2/4: "standard deviation over non-zero entries" over which cells (users x pool items? users x keys)? Appendix D.2 says "all user and pool-item cells": contradicts "non-zero entries" ambiguity in Eq. 4. Author/music channels for keys never seen: zero, but not stated for lift when n=0 vs ell=0 legitimately.
- Eq. 5: LightGCN readout ("mean of layer-zero and propagated" only in App. D.2), initialisation, popularity q_i standardisation, how w "policy channel" interacts with negatives (Sec. 3.3 negatives: "never shown" vs "never clicked" when w=0) are ambiguous.
- Eq. 13: sum over r in D of p_E / sum kappa, written as a single global ratio times r_u/rbar; per user or global? Reads as global, then c_u is just r_u rescaled; clarify. The min(1, .) vs clip [1e-3,1] in B.
- Eq. 9-10: theta update divides by sum kappa (Poisson approximation) while the E-step uses Bernoulli; App. B admits this is "not the MAP". Convergence is not tested (40 its fixed). Initial values 0.7/0.6 unjustified.
- Eq. 14: mu_alpha, sigma_alpha over I; "user-group" variant (DAX with per-group alpha, "strata") is only in D.1/B and is chosen in all five splits (Table C.11, "user groups 80"), so it is part of the main method but absent from Sec. 3.4. Same for the content-regression centre.
- Sec. 3.5: "DAX variant of every other backbone" - for DIN/SIM/EASE what is s^backbone's scale? Fine via std, but the epoch selection coupling (Alg. 2) is intricate.
- Not defined in main text: QP, DR-BPR-LGN-1/p2/p, r-AdjNorm exponent, DIN/SIM architecture (hidden sizes only in Table D.31), candidate list construction (43.6 per user; how sampled?).

## 4. Self-containedness
Not self-contained in vocabulary: "QP family" (never expanded, "authors' own earlier method, not published": the headline baseline is unreproducible outside App. A and unverifiable), "LEAF development experiment", "LEAF-tied", "policy channel", "re-trained LightGCN", "independent tuning", "Table D.xx/C.xx" cross-references, "kept (%)", "main family / primary family / sensitivity family". "Configurations" vs "evaluations" counts. Code "will be released upon publication". Main-text tables 1-10 are printed after the references (pp. 45-49), forcing constant page-flipping. The footer says "Elsevier preprint"; "Appendix Appendix D.1" typos (several).

## 5. Abstract and introduction
Abstract is dense but readable until the last third; it names "graph, debiased and lifelong deep baselines" yet reports only one comparison, and "strongest debiased graph baseline" is the unpublished QP family (a reader will assume a published method). Intro "Main findings" paragraph is overloaded: 0.4854, 0.4634, 0.4733, nine baselines, Holm, "two borderline", six backbones, permutations, 1K. The statistics belong in Sec. 5. The "Evaluation design" paragraph (five splits, two tests, multiple-comparison correction) precedes the reader knowing what the task is. Contributions list four bullets; the first (headroom is item-level) is a diagnostic using test labels, not a contribution of the method.

## 6. Results
- Claims findable via the question-style subsection leads (good). But the main table (Table 3/4) is far from text; Fig. 1 is clear and legible; Fig. 2 has overlapping x tick labels ("without kappa_t", "without lifelong prior") and a y-axis in a different base than Table 7 text; Fig. 3 left y-axis (0.4835-0.4870) magnifies noise.
- Inconsistencies: users evaluated 22,661 (5.1) vs 22,612 (Table 2/4) vs 22,847 (5.1, second computation); per-split test users 18,954 (4.3) vs 15,809-15,913 with a click (Table 2). LightGCN is 0.4576 (Table 3/4) and 0.4597 (Table 6), explained only in a note. LEAF-LGN 0.4736 vs "independent tuning" 0.4728 vs ablation base 0.4706: three LEAF-LGN baselines. Table 7 "Mean" for full DAX is 0.4854 but gain +15.10 over a 0.4706 base (=14.8; rounding or different base? stated only in the note). Table 7 "Gain" of LEAF 0.4854 equals main experiment although "reduced" grid (320 vs 3,840 configs) - claimed exact reproduction needs explanation. Table 9 spread "+13.65 +- 1.48" mixes seed 0 (full search) with reduced seeds. "NB p 0.000" in Tables 4, 6, 10. Table D.33 caption speaks of a family size in "column header" that is not there. Sec. 5.3 says DIN+DAX "NB p 0.043" and Holm 0.0456 in text, 4.56e-2 in tables, "0.0456" threshold arguments: fine but the headline "ahead of all nine on both tests" is carried by two p=0.0456 values from a 5-split test that the authors call approximate.
- Per-user p values (1e-125 ... 1e-190) are not meaningful (users share training; same users across splits); they should be removed from the main tables.
- Budgets: 3,840 vs 20 vs 4 configurations; DIN/SIM tuned over 4 settings, dimension 16, 12 epochs, versus LEAF-LGN 12 trainings. DAX-equipped QP is the only fairly-matched baseline, and there the split-level gain (+12.04) is the real claim. The "LEAF backbone alone is not clearly better than QP" (5.4) undermines the LEAF half of the title; LEAF controls are only run on a different model (LEAF-tied, closed form), never on LEAF-LGN (admitted in 5.10).
- DAX alone is never shown as a ranker; no analysis of how alpha differs from click rate except one Spearman (0.419 vs 0.433); the 5.1 oracle text ("DAX closes a quarter of the gap") uses a blend that "is not the method".
- Within-tag permutation keeps 41.6% of the gain, but the paper's "item-level" conclusion rests on that.

## 7. Novelty and positioning
Seen by a reviewer: a LightGCN with six user-key features (feature engineering, close to SIM-style category lookups and to target-aware features), plus an examination x attraction click factorisation (the position-based/cascade click-model family, ExpoMF, occupancy) with Beta shrinkage, evaluated on one dataset with a self-defined protocol. Section 2.7 honestly says "combination". The ecological analogy is decoration; the DAX likelihood is a product model with a known scale non-identifiability (3.6) that is then handled by validation-tuned priors at grid edges (nu=80 lower edge, lambda=100 upper edge, App. C). Related work misses: unbiased/propensity-based learning from MNAR with doubly-robust (the QP baseline is DR-style but unpublished), counterfactual/off-policy evaluation, cold-start item models, uplift of KuaiRand-based studies, and published strong sequence baselines (TWIN excluded). No second dataset with randomness; 1K is a subset. Title ("Examination before attraction... randomised exposure") is not about LEAF and is long.

## 9. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISION
The idea of separating examination from attraction in a feed without positions and of transferring lifelong, exposure-conditional user affinities through content keys is reasonable, the protocol (random log only for selection/test) is careful, and the controls are more honest than most. But the headline comparison rests on an unpublished baseline of the authors' own, search budgets differing by two orders of magnitude, split-level tests that the authors themselves call approximate and borderline for two of nine comparisons, and one platform whose second dataset is a subset of the first. The LEAF half is validated only on a different backbone, and the paper's structure (tables after the references, many internal experiment names, numerically overloaded abstract and intro, inconsistent counts) makes it hard to read and verify. With a published strong baseline, clearer exposition, a consistent set of numbers, and LEAF controls on the actual model, this could become a solid TORS-length contribution; at present it is not ready.
