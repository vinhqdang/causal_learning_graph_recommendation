# Referee report 3 on paper/v8 (blind read of the sources and of main.pdf, 66 pp.; figures 1-3 inspected)

Scope of this round: I re-read main.tex, sections/*.tex, tables/*.tex and the PDF text, compared the text with every table, and checked REVIEW_1.md and REVIEW_2.md point by point. Line references are to the .tex files (s3 = sections/s3_method.tex, etc.). The one placeholder comment for the QP training seeds (s5_results.tex l.161) is expected and is not counted against the paper.

## 1. Claimed contribution (4 sentences)

The paper studies ranking of videos that a platform inserts into a feed uniformly at random (KuaiRand), when training may use only the policy log from before the random period and the random log is reserved for validation and test. It proposes LEAF, per-user lift and familiarity statistics on tag/author/music keys computed from the user's out-of-pool history and fed as six channels into LightGCN (LEAF-LGN), and DAX, an occupancy/click-model style likelihood (click = examination(user x scenario) x attraction(item)) fitted by EM with tag shrinkage and added to any backbone through a validation-tuned weight. On KuaiRand-27K, LEAF-LGN+DAX reaches nDCG@10 0.4854 against 0.4634 for the authors' own unpublished propensity-weighted LightGCN ("QP family"), is above all 13 comparators on a per-user paired test, and is above 11 of 13 at split level after Holm correction (two comparisons, QP family and DIN+DAX, sit at 0.0456). DAX adds roughly +11 to +13 (x1e-3) to six backbones, permuting attractiveness across items removes the gain, and shuffling users' lifelong tables removes the LEAF gain, with a diagnostic claiming that the remaining headroom is mostly item-level.

## 2. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISION (clearly improved, not yet acceptable)

The paper is much better than the version REVIEW_2 saw. The structure is now readable (tables sit in the text, the three logs are defined and drawn, the DAX variants that are actually used are in Section 3, counts and baseline values are reconciled in 4.4, the Holm families are listed, 13 comparators including four published debiased estimators and SIM+DAX are reported, the DIN/SIM rows are real, the headroom section exists, and the wording on borderline p values is honest in Sections 4-5 and 7). The protocol (random log only for selection and test, cut-off, leakage checks) is careful and is the paper's main strength.

I still cannot recommend acceptance, for four reasons.

1. The central scientific claim of the title, "examination before attraction", is not isolated. The ablations that remove the examination parts (scenario factor kappa_t: +1.51, p 0.076; lifelong prior on theta_u: +1.78, p 0.263; Table `tab:ablate`) are not significant at split level, and there is no control of the form "same tag prior, theta = kappa = 1" (a shrunk, tag-pooled click rate). A pooled tag-mean alone already gives +4.31 of the +9.45 full-DAX gain in the (appendix, fixed-score) preliminary analysis (Table `tab:controls-real`). It is therefore not shown that the examination model, rather than a shrunk item click rate with a user-activity correction, produces the +12.
2. The strongest baselines are not independent of the proposal and the strongest comparison is borderline. The abstract and the introduction use the authors' unpublished QP family (0.4634) as the reference, whereas the closest comparators are DIN+DAX (0.4796, +5.73 [1.64], split-level Holm 0.0456) and QP+DAX (+12.04). The published debiased estimators are weak (0.3765-0.4579), tuned over four configurations, and tell the reader little. DIN/SIM use dimension 16, 12 epochs and 4 trainings against 64, 40 and 12 for LEAF-LGN.
3. Inference is fragile: five splits that share one training run, an approximate Nadeau-Bengio test that needs a mean/SD ratio above 4.42, families defined after the fact, and no training-seed replication of any baseline. The baseline re-train shift visible in Table `tab:methods` (QP 0.4634 vs 0.4652 on the same configurations, 1.8e-3) is a third of the DIN+DAX margin.
4. The LEAF half is only partly validated (controls compare a 197-configuration reference with 12-configuration variants, unadjusted), and the headroom narrative ("mostly item-level") is not supported by what is computed and is in tension with the LEAF result.

These are fixable with additional controls and reanalysis; the required experiments are cheap relative to the work already done. With them, and with a tighter, more self-contained text, this would be a solid contribution for TORS or IP&M.

## 4. New major issues

N1. Significance language is inconsistent and the abstract hides it.
- s4 l.159-160 defines "significant only when Holm p < 0.05" and in the next sentence says that 0.0456 "is called borderline, not significant". By the stated rule 0.0456 is significant. s5 l.48 then says "significant against eleven ... borderline against the other two", and s8 l.8 says LEAF-LGN+DAX "ranks above each of the thirteen". The reader needs one rule ("we treat adjusted p in [0.04, 0.05) as inconclusive because the test is approximate" is fine, but must be stated as the rule and applied in the abstract).
- The abstract (l.7-9) does not mention that the two nearest comparators (the QP family and DIN+DAX) are inconclusive at split level, nor that the DAX increment on QP is unsupported at split level (Table `tab:backbone`, p 0.105; 1K p_Holm 0.160). "DAX improves every backbone tested" is therefore an over-statement unless qualified ("positive per user on all six, supported at split level on five").
- Reference baseline: the number in the abstract (0.4634) is the authors' unpublished baseline. The honest strongest-comparator statement is "+5.7 [1.6] over DIN+DAX, +12.0 over QP+DAX, +11.96 over the same backbone without DAX".

N2. The "examination before attraction" mechanism is not isolated (title claim).
- Table `tab:ablate`: removing kappa_t gives p 0.076, removing the lifelong theta prior p 0.263, removing the tag prior p 0.116. The sentence "All three priors contribute" (s5 l.111) and "Each removal lowers the score" (l.122) are descriptive of point estimates only. kappa_t is not a prior either.
- Missing controls that decide the claim: (a) theta = kappa = 1 with the same tag prior and the same selection (a Beta-shrunk tag-pooled CTR); (b) theta only, kappa only; (c) a user-activity-normalised CTR (impressions weighted by 1/user click rate). Without them, "+12 from DAX" may be a shrunk item-click-rate or popularity effect. The additive-logit variant (+6.09, p 0.015) differs from DAX in more than the product structure (different prior, different fit), so it does not test examination either.
- s5 l.28-29 argues that because DAX's item-level Spearman with the uniform rate (0.419) is below that of pre-cut click counts (0.433), "the DAX gain is not simply popularity". This is a non sequitur; a partial correlation or an incremental test over LEAF-LGN's popularity term (q_i is already in the backbone) is needed. In addition the tag-mean-alone result (+4.31 of +9.45, appendix, fixed scores) and the within-tag permutation (41.6% kept) indicate that a large part of DAX is a tag-level click prior.
- DAX is item-level (plus six user groups) and the user-group variant is on in all five splits (App. C). The method as run is therefore partly a user-group-by-item click table; this should be said in the abstract's description.

N3. Interpretation of theta_u as "examination" is unvalidated.
- The centre of theta_u is the user's lifelong click rate (Eq. `life-centre`), so a user who rarely clicks because content rarely matches is treated as a low examiner. Examination and taste are conflated by construction, and there is no independent signal (play time, long view, scroll speed) showing that theta_u or kappa_t track attention. The only validations are simulation checks (App. D.5) with known parameters generated from the same model.
- The product likelihood has an unresolved scale non-identifiability (s3.6); the logit that enters the ranker depends on it (via nu, lambda). It is acknowledged, but the 40-iteration, no-convergence-test fit with an arbitrary start is then the estimator; one robustness check over initialisation or iteration count would help.

N4. LEAF is not cleanly isolated, and the "exposure-conditional" idea is not tested.
- Table `tab:leafctl-lgn`: reference 197 configurations vs 12/12/4 for the variants (authors note it favours the reference); the reference (0.4728) is not the main LEAF-LGN (0.4736); "without lifelong channels" has NB p 0.018 and the shuffled variant 0.002 although its difference is smaller (+10.65 vs +11.46), both unadjusted and with no Holm family.
- The controls were not run on LEAF-LGN+DAX, so what the paper reports as "lifelong gain" is measured with a different, weaker model.
- The motivation (s1 l.14-18, s3 "Idea") says counting how many cooking videos a user was shown measures the policy, not preference. But `familiarity` = log(1 + shown count) is a LEAF channel, and in Table `tab:leafctl` "familiarity only" (0.4585) is +7.3 above "without lifelong channels" (0.4512) and only 10.26 below full. A raw (not exposure-conditioned) click-count channel versus lift is never compared. The key novelty claim of LEAF is therefore untested.
- Contribution 2 asks that LEAF-LGN be taken as a gain over graph recommenders, yet LEAF-LGN alone against the QP family is +9.63 with split-level p 0.063 (Holm 0.189 in the dev family) and, against LightGCN, borderline (R1 #13 values not reported). With DAX on both sides the margin over DIN is +5.7 (borderline). The text (s5 l.85-88) says so; the title, abstract and contributions do not.
- Policy channel: in four of five splits the variant without it gives identical test scores (s5 l.193), i.e. w = 0 is selected. A component of the model (s3.3, App. D.2 grid over w) that is not used should be removed or moved to the appendix rather than stay in the method.

N5. Baselines, scale and fairness of the comparison.
- No trivial reference is reported (random order, pre-cut popularity, smoothed item CTR, and a "DAX alone" row). With ~44 candidates per user (median 22) and nDCG@10 only for users with at least one click, readers cannot judge whether 0.4854 vs 0.4576 (LightGCN) is large, nor how much the oracle (0.5068) or the off-protocol +36.62 (App. D.5) means. This is needed for the headroom story and for the DAX stand-alone question.
- IPS-MF, DR-JL, MRDR reach 0.3765-0.3826, about 8 points below plain LightGCN with four configurations, and NAVIP equals LightGCN (0.4579 vs 0.4576). Such results usually signal that a propensity from item popularity, 64 dimensions and four grid points is a poor fit rather than that the estimators are weak; as published comparators they carry little evidential weight. A popularity baseline would show whether they are even above popularity.
- DIN/SIM use d = 16, 12 epochs, patience 2, four trainings vs 64/40/12 for LEAF-LGN. The comparison that matters for LEAF is DIN+DAX at 0.4796; its tuning budget (1,280 configurations, 4 backbone trainings) is deliberately smaller. A matched-capacity DIN/SIM (d = 64, same epochs) is required before concluding that "LEAF-LGN+DAX beats lifelong deep models".
- Baselines are tuned by the authors with grids chosen by the authors; QP is theirs, and QP+DAX uses 10 QP trainings preselected without DAX.

N6. Statistical inference.
- All five splits reuse one training run; the Nadeau-Bengio correction is for resampled training sets (s4 l.129-130, honest). The needed ratio d/s_d >= 4.42 makes the test depend on the split-to-split coefficient of variation; with a shared training trajectory its null is unknown. The simplest remedy is a user-cluster bootstrap (resampling users, re-running selection) plus training seeds.
- Training-seed variance of the baselines is not measured. Indirect evidence says it is not negligible: QP re-trained on its selected configurations 0.4652 vs 0.4634 (Table `tab:methods`, 1.8e-3), LEAF-LGN+DAX seed spread +/-0.6e-3 but with a reduced backbone grid, DIN+DAX margin +5.73. The column "SD over five splits" in Table `tab:methods` is split variation only; it should not be read as uncertainty of the method (LEAF-LGN SD 0.0002).
- Families are defined after the fact (main family "defined after the primary family ... extended afterwards", s4 l.140-143), the paper then asserts that "every comparison significant in the thirteen-member family was also significant in the nine-member family"; the nine-member family appears nowhere else and the sentence is an artefact of the revision history. Pre-specification of the primary family and of the ablation criteria (s4 l.154-158) has no verifiable record (no registration, commit identifier, or date).
- Ablation families: s4 l.146-147 define two ablation Holm families (7 gains, 6 differences) but Table `tab:ablate` and s5 l.125-126 report unadjusted p; either report the adjustment or delete the definition. The permutation rows (960 configurations = 3 seeds x 320) seem to pool the seeds into one selection; this is unclear.

N7. Headroom diagnosis (Section 5.1) does not support its conclusion.
- The oracle uses other test users' labels (leave-one-user-out) and shows only that item-level uniform rates are predictive. The statement that "the residual headroom therefore lies mainly at the item level" rests on one personalisation model (rank-64 residual fit on in-pool uniform data) failing to improve 0.5850 to 0.5851 on 19,474 users, which can fail for sparsity reasons (about 44 labels per user), and which uses a different user set and a different value (0.5850) that is not reconciled with 0.5068/22,661.
- It is in tension with Section 5.10 and the LEAF controls, where user-level information (lifelong tables) gives +11 to +19; the conclusion's first paragraph nevertheless says the graph recommender "leaves its remaining error mostly in item-level appeal".
- "The top quintile of the uniform click rate carries 0.83 of the residual gain of the item-level correction" cannot be decoded from the text.
- The oracle gap is measured against LEAF-LGN (0.4735), but the method reaches 0.4854 (closing about 36% of the gap, not the "quarter" of the 0.5-blend that is "not the method"). Report the method.

N8. Generality and the test target.
- One platform, one cut-off, one scenario (random insertions almost entirely in scenario 1), click label only; 1K users are a subset of 27K (admitted). The classic-dataset argument (s4 l.56-62) contains an unreported experiment ("a training-free prior ... reaches nDCG@20 0.6367 on 1,410 users on KuaiRec"), which should be shown or removed.
- The test population is users with at least one click among candidates; the effect of this conditioning (a selection on the outcome that differs by method only through ranking, so likely harmless) is not discussed.

## 5. Minor issues

Abstract and introduction
- Abstract l.1-3 and l.6: "content keys", "pool", "inserted items", "backbone", "graph, debiased (tuned and published) and lifelong deep baselines" are undefined or vague; LEAF and DAX are used before their expansion (given in the intro only). "Debiased (tuned and published)" reads as if the strongest tuned baseline were published; say "our own propensity-weighted LightGCN".
- Abstract: two accuracy numbers only (good), but no effect size on the strongest comparator and no mention of borderline/per-split caveats (N1). The final "The evidence comes from one platform." is the only limitation.
- Intro l.28-30 "We found no method that ..." and s2 l.53, l.86, l.119-120 "As far as our searches went": unverifiable; give the search protocol in a sentence or soften. TODO_citations.md still lists 17 sources characterised from titles only (richardson2007, craswell2008, chapelle2009, wang2016/2018, kim2022navip "title only", chen2021autodebias, and others): verify them before submission, since Table `tab:positioning` rests on them.
- Intro l.58 "one tuned by us and four published estimators": the four estimators include IPS-MF (Schnabel 2016, MF with IPS) which is not a "debiased graph recommender"; the abstract says "graph, debiased".
- Contribution 1 is a diagnostic using test labels (N7) and should not be listed as a contribution; contribution 4 (evaluation protocol) is methodology rather than novelty (R1 #18).
- Intro l.34-37 and s5 l.3-6: sentences about the multiple-testing machinery ("tested twice ... corrected for multiple comparisons") belong in Section 4.

Method (s3) and appendix consistency
- Symbol overload: r is the impression index, the base rate r_u, the r-AdjNorm exponent, and rbar; c is a click count (c^k_uv), a tag centre (c_g) and a user centre (c-tilde); w is the policy weight (s3.3), the item weights w_i in Eq. `content-centre` and the negative weight w_uj in App. A; C_u is both the candidate list and C_u^life (lifelong clicks); g is the channel weight vector and g(i) the tag group; "strata" and "user groups" name one thing (App. B vs s3.4).
- s3 Eq. `m-theta` has no pseudo-count for lambda = 0 while App. B uses (S+1)/(K+2) and Beta(2,2) for lambda = 0.
- s3.4 says nu_g is "selected on the validation users"; App. B, C and D.2 fix it at 80 ("the value selected in every split" cannot be a selection from one value).
- s3 "Two variants of the main experiment" (l. ~"Content-regression centre") refers to "the main experiment" before it exists in the reader's mind.
- s3.3 last sentence: "Its contribution is tested in Section 5.10, where it gives no measurable gain on LEAF-LGN" appears inside the method description of the policy channel; the method should state the final model, and drop w if it is never selected.

## 6. The three changes that would most raise acceptance odds

1. Isolate the mechanism and calibrate the scale (N2, N3, N5). Add, under the same selection protocol and Holm family: random, popularity, smoothed item CTR and DAX-alone rows; LEAF-LGN plus a Beta-shrunk tag-pooled CTR (theta = kappa = 1), theta-only and kappa-only variants, and a user-activity-normalised CTR; a partial-correlation or incremental test replacing the "not simply popularity" argument; and, if available, one independent attention signal (play time/long view) correlated with theta_u. Then either show that the examination factorisation matters (significantly) or retitle and reword the claim as "item-level shrinkage with user and scenario adjustment".
2. Make the headline comparison robust and honest (N1, N5, N6). Re-seed (at least three training seeds, full grids) the three comparators that matter, QP+DAX, DIN+DAX and the LEAF-LGN backbone, add a matched-capacity DIN/SIM (d = 64, same epochs), use a user-cluster bootstrap in addition to Nadeau-Bengio, adopt one stated significance rule, and report the abstract result against DIN+DAX (and against QP+DAX), not against the unpublished QP; state in the abstract that two comparisons are inconclusive at split level and the DAX increment on QP is unsupported at split level. Remove or justify the post-hoc family history.
3. Isolate LEAF and prune the narrative (N4, N7, self-containedness). Rerun the LEAF controls on LEAF-LGN+DAX with budgets matched to the reference and Holm-adjusted; add raw click-count vs exposure-conditional lift, and LightGCN with the same six channels as plain features, to test the "exposure-conditional" claim; drop the unused policy channel from the method; delete or rebuild Section 5.1 (use the method's score and a sparsity-aware personalisation test, and remove the "mostly item-level" conclusion if not supported); remove references to the unpublished "LEAF development experiment" and "LEAF-tied" from the main text; replace the name "QP family"; move code-test material out of App. D.5; and cut the main text toward 25 pages with one baseline-values table.
