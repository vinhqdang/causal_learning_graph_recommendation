Tables~\ref{tab:acc} and~\ref{tab:sig} report accuracy and the user-level tests.

\paragraph{The walk correction does not change accuracy.} On every dataset DRUP
and the uncorrected DR adjacency with the same grid are statistically
indistinguishable (Coat $+0.0014$, KuaiRec $-0.0001$ with $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.45$), and
on Yahoo!\,R3 they coincide exactly, because validation selects the IPS end
of the control-variate family ($\lambda=0$) on every split and IPS edges have no
three-hop repeated-walk bias on unexposed candidates (Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}).
The IPS adjacency and IPS with the correction give identical rankings on all
three datasets for the same reason. At five hops on KuaiRec, where the
correction is active for both edge types, the corrected operator is lower by
$0.0003$ nDCG@20 ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.04$), a negligible but consistent difference in
line with the ranking agreement of Section~\ref{sec:mc}. Five hops themselves do
not add accuracy over three (0.6376 against 0.6378).

\paragraph{Propagation over the DR graph is what helps on KuaiRec.} On KuaiRec,
whose test matrix is fully observed and whose candidate sets have 3{,}314 items,
training-free propagation over the DR graph reaches nDCG@20 of 0.638 and is
significantly better than every trained model (the best, LightGCN with a
pointwise loss, reaches 0.628; $+0.010$, 95\% CI $[0.007,0.013]$), every operator on
the logged graph (GF-CF 0.614, IPS adjacency 0.613, linear LightGCN 0.604) and the
low-rank imputation used alone (0.619). GF-CF applied to the same DR graph
performs as well as DRUP (0.637), which confirms that the gain comes from the
DR graph and not from a particular filter. The selected DR configurations use
the low-rank imputation on all five splits.

\paragraph{Yahoo!\,R3 and Coat.} On Yahoo!\,R3 the log is 2\% dense and the
imputation alone is below popularity (0.516 against 0.533), so almost every edge
of a DR graph would be imputed from a poor model; validation therefore selects
IPS edges ($\lambda=0$) with cross-fitted $W$-degrees on every split. With these
settings DRUP (0.657) is on par with linear propagation over the logged graph
(0.659) and GF-CF (0.657), and it is significantly behind the three graph models
trained with BPR: r-AdjNorm (0.671; $-0.015$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.004$), NAVIP and LightGCN
(0.669; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}\le0.04$). With degrees taken from the imputation instead, DRUP
drops to 0.62--0.63 on this dataset, so the degree source matters here. On Coat
(290 users, 15 candidates per user) no difference between DRUP and a trained or
training-free baseline survives the Holm correction except GF-CF on the DR
graph, which is better by 0.013 ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.03$); the best models are again the BPR
graph models (r-AdjNorm 0.575, LightGCN 0.570), but their advantage over DRUP is
not significant at the user level.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Two changes of the baselines matter. First, pairwise
training is decisive on Yahoo!\,R3: pointwise MF variants stay near 0.51, below
popularity, whereas BPR-MF reaches 0.646 and BPR-trained LightGCN 0.669. On
KuaiRec the ordering reverses: pointwise LightGCN (0.628) and MF (0.623) beat
their BPR versions (0.609, 0.591). Second, per-split selection of configuration
and epoch benefits the trained models on the small Coat data, where re-training
the split-0 selection with other seeds lowers LightGCN from 0.581 to
$0.547\pm0.018$; on Yahoo!\,R3 and KuaiRec the seed standard deviation is below 0.005.
Across the three datasets, the best trained model is thus a BPR graph model on
Coat and Yahoo!\,R3 and a pointwise one on KuaiRec, while DR propagation without
training is the most accurate method on the largest and most complete test set.
