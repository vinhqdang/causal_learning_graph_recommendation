Tables~\ref{tab:acc} and~\ref{tab:sig} report accuracy and the user-level
tests; AUC and Recall are in the Supplementary Material and lead to the same
conclusions.

\paragraph{The walk correction does not change accuracy.} On every dataset DRUP
and the uncorrected DR adjacency with the same grid are not significantly
different (Coat $+0.0014$, KuaiRec $-0.0001$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1$). Only KuaiRec and, marginally,
Coat can test equivalence: within $\pm0.005$ nDCG the two operators are equivalent on KuaiRec
($p_{\mathrm{TOST}}<10^{-3}$, also at $\pm0.002$) and marginally so on Coat ($p_{\mathrm{TOST}}=0.034$, but 0.38 at
$\pm0.002$; Table~\ref{tab:nbtost}). The margin is a convention, about twice the split-to-split standard deviation of the
methods on KuaiRec (0.002--0.004). On Yahoo!\,R3 and KuaiRand the two operators coincide exactly, so
there is no test to run, because
validation selects the IPS end of the control-variate family ($\lambda=0$) on
every split and IPS edges have no three-hop repeated-walk bias on unexposed
candidates (Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}); on KuaiRand the selected clip is the smallest in the grid, 0.02, on all splits. The IPS adjacency and IPS with the
correction give identical rankings on all datasets for the same reason. At five
hops, where the correction is active for both edge types, the corrected
operator is lower by 0.0003 nDCG@20 on KuaiRec ($p=0.011$ before and
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.16$ after the adjustment) and by 0.008 nDCG@5 on Coat
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1$). The same holds under the sample split (Coat $-0.003$,
Yahoo!\,R3 $-0.001$, KuaiRec $-0.0001$; all $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1$). Five hops do not add
accuracy over three (KuaiRec 0.6376 against 0.6378).

\paragraph{The sample split.} DRUP-split, the only variant covered by the
guarantees, costs little on the larger and denser logs and much on the sparse
one. On KuaiRec it reaches $0.6321\pm0.0035$ over five draws of the split-off pairs
(0.627 to 0.636), 0.006 below the cross-fitted DRUP. For the first draw (0.6337) the
gap is 0.0035 ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.003$), and that draw remains
significantly above pointwise LightGCN ($+0.007$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}<10^{-3}$; Wilcoxon
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.48$) and on par with DR-JL and MRDR ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1$). 
On Coat its accuracy depends on the draw ($0.544\pm0.011$, from 0.527 to 0.558):
with 24 logged ratings per user, which fifth of them is spent on the nuisances
matters, and the first draw is not significantly different from the
cross-fitted DRUP ($+0.009$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1$). On Yahoo!\,R3 it loses 0.062
($0.595\pm0.004$; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}<10^{-3}$ for the first draw), because a fifth of the
few logged exposures goes to the nuisances and the imputation that replaces
them is poor. Section~\ref{sec:semisynth} shows the corresponding cost for
score values: the imputed pairs bias the utility estimates upwards.

\paragraph{Propagation over the DR graph is what helps on KuaiRec.} On KuaiRec,
whose test matrix is fully observed and whose candidate sets have 3{,}314 items,
training-free propagation over the DR graph reaches nDCG@20 of 0.638 and is
ahead of every trained model at the user level, but by little over the best ones: the jointly
learned doubly robust models DR-JL and MRDR (0.633; $+0.004$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.003$). With the
Nadeau--Bengio test over splits, adjusted over the same family, neither margin is significant
($p_{\mathrm{NB,Holm}}=1.0$ and $0.34$), and both are smaller than the seed spread of 0.009 and 0.015 of
these models. The margin over pointwise LightGCN (0.627; $+0.010$) is significant under both tests
($p_{\mathrm{NB,Holm}}=0.03$). The next ranks are

LightGCN 0.604) and than the imputation used alone (0.619). GF-CF on the same DR

\paragraph{KuaiRand.} KuaiRand is the only dataset whose log records platform
exposure. Its log is 0.7\% dense, and as on Yahoo!\,R3 validation selects IPS
edges ($\lambda=0$) on every split, so DRUP, DR adjacency and IPS adjacency
coincide (nDCG@10 0.4492). They are not significantly different from GF-CF
(0.4502) or BSPM (0.4498) on the logged graph, from iALS (0.4528) or from
BPR-trained MF (0.4475; all $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1$), and they are significantly
behind the BPR graph models: r-AdjNorm (0.4590; $-0.010$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}<10^{-3}$), LightGCN
(0.4569; $-0.008$) and NAVIP (0.4565; $-0.008$; both $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}<10^{-3}$). They
are significantly ahead of popularity (0.4204), of the pointwise models (LightGCN
0.406, MF 0.371, DR-JL 0.376), of MACR (0.431) and of SimGCL (0.420). The sample split
costs 0.009 ($0.4400\pm0.0007$ over five draws; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}<10^{-3}$), with
DRUP-split and DR adjacency under the split again indistinguishable
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1$). Only eleven baselines were trained on KuaiRand.

\paragraph{Coat and Yahoo!\,R3.} On Coat (290 users, 15 candidates per user) the
most accurate method is iALS (0.581), followed by BSPM on the logged graph,
DR-LightGCN and the BPR graph models (0.566--0.573); DRUP reaches 0.547. With the Holm
adjustment over all 124 comparisons, DRUP is significantly better than the
pointwise MF, IPS-MF, DR-JL and MRDR and not significantly different from any
other method, including iALS ($-0.037$, $p=0.033$ before adjustment) and GF-CF on
the DR graph ($-0.014$, $p=0.001$ before and $0.11$ after adjustment). On
Yahoo!\,R3 the log is 2\% dense and the imputation alone is below popularity
(0.516 against 0.533), so almost every edge of a DR graph would be imputed from
a poor model; validation therefore selects IPS edges ($\lambda=0$) with
cross-fitted $W$-degrees on every split, which lie outside
Assumption~\ref{as:fixed}. DRUP (0.657) is then on par with linear propagation
over the logged graph (0.659), GF-CF (0.655) and iALS (0.663), and significantly
behind the three graph models trained with BPR: r-AdjNorm (0.674; $-0.019$,
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}<10^{-3}$), LightGCN (0.671; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.021$) and NAVIP (0.670;
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.029$). SimGCL (0.635) is below DRUP on this dataset
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}<10^{-3}$).

\paragraph{Tuning budgets.} The training-free operators search many more
configurations than the trained models (Table~\ref{tab:configs}), because each
configuration costs seconds rather than a training run. Table~\ref{tab:budget}
(Appendix~\ref{app:details}) gives the expected test score of DRUP when its
configuration is selected from as many random configurations, or as many
validation evaluations, as the most accurate trained model used. On Coat and
Yahoo!\,R3 the larger grid does not help DRUP: with the budget of iALS (24
configurations) it reaches 0.549 on Coat, and with that of r-AdjNorm (72) 0.657
on Yahoo!\,R3, still below both. On KuaiRec the budget matters in
configurations and not in evaluations. Counting a trained configuration with
all its epochs as one, DRUP with the six configurations of DR-JL reaches only
0.618 against 0.633; counting every validation evaluation, as each epoch of a
trained model is one, DRUP with the 103 evaluations of DR-JL reaches 0.636.
On four CPU cores, fitting the nuisances takes about 50 minutes (14 for the
cross-fitted propensity model and about 35 for the cross-fitted imputations of
the grid, shared by all DR-type operators), after which a DRUP configuration
costs about 3\,s (21 minutes for all 384), against 130--210\,s for training one
LightGCN configuration.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Three observations concern the baselines. First, the loss
matters more than the architecture, and in opposite directions on the
logs with few exposures per user and on KuaiRec: pairwise training is decisive on
Yahoo!\,R3 (pointwise MF variants stay near 0.51, below popularity, whereas BPR-MF
reaches 0.646 and BPR-trained LightGCN 0.671) and on KuaiRand (BPR-MF 0.448
against 0.371 pointwise, LightGCN 0.457 against 0.406), whereas on KuaiRec the pointwise
models beat their BPR versions (LightGCN 0.627 against 0.609, MF 0.622 against
0.596). Second, iALS is the strongest trained model on Coat and competitive on
Yahoo!\,R3, but weak on KuaiRec (0.613), and the jointly learned DR models are
the strongest trained models on KuaiRec but weak on the two small logs.
No single trained baseline is strong on all datasets. Third, re-training the
split-0 selection with other seeds shows how much of a trained model's score
is seed luck. On Yahoo!\,R3 the seeds lie within 0.013 of each other (0.007 for DR-JL and
for the BPR graph models) and for the LightGCN variants on KuaiRec within 0.007; on Coat they spread by up to 0.05 for the
graph models (r-AdjNorm 0.521--0.566), and on KuaiRec by 0.009 and 0.015 for
DR-JL and MRDR, which learn their imputation jointly. The margin of DRUP over
DR-JL and MRDR on KuaiRec (0.004) is therefore smaller than their seed
variation, and differences on Coat should be read with this in mind; iALS and
the training-free operators are deterministic given the log.
