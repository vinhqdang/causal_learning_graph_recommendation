\subsection{Monte-Carlo checks}\label{sec:mc}

Table~\ref{tab:mc} uses a synthetic MNAR model with known propensities: 30 users,
40 items, low-rank preferences, and exposures driven by user activity, item
popularity and the outcome itself (median propensity 0.06, a third of the
pairs below 0.05). We draw 20{,}000 exposure matrices for three hops and 4{,}000
for five hops.

Over all entries, the uncorrected IPS and DR operators are biased by 2.3 and 1.8
times the mean target at three hops, and by 15 and 8 times at five hops. With
the correction the residual bias (0.02--0.06) is at the level of the
Monte-Carlo error. On unexposed candidates the picture is the one predicted by
Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}: the uncorrected IPS operator is unbiased at three
hops (0.029, identical to IPS with the correction) but not at five hops (4.7),
and the uncorrected DR operator is biased at both depths (1.1 and 5.1). The
exposure elasticity is $+1.78$ for the logged graph, $-0.19$ for DR adjacency and
$+0.005$ for DRUP. The $K$-hop engine reproduces the closed-form three-hop
operator to $7\times10^{-15}$ and brute-force enumeration of all five-hop walks to
$10^{-13}$. On a dense matrix of KuaiRec's size (7{,}176 users, 10{,}728 items,
1{,}411 scored users, four CPU cores) the corrected three-hop operator takes 1.9\,s
against 1.4\,s for the plug-in one, and the corrected five-hop operator 85\,s
against 2.9\,s: the $K$-hop correction is exact but not free.

Unbiasedness does not buy ranking accuracy in this model. The rank agreement
of a single realisation with the target is higher for the uncorrected DR
operator (Kendall $\tau=0.20$) than for DRUP (0.12), and at five hops 0.11 against
0.09. The reason is visible in Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}: on unexposed candidates
the bias is $C_{ui}\hat Y_{ui}(R_u^{(i)}+K_i)$, which adds a positive multiple of the
imputation to the score, and here the imputation is informative. The corrected
operator removes this term and, with it, some useful signal. For IPS edges,
whose bias does not involve the imputation, the correction leaves the rank
agreement unchanged (0.061 against 0.059 at five hops).

\paragraph{Theory against protocol.} Table~\ref{tab:protocol} measures how far the
guarantees carry over to the way nuisances are fitted in practice. With
nuisances from an independent log, the corrected operator's bias is at the
Monte-Carlo error (0.01--0.10) while the uncorrected operators are biased by
0.09--2.2 times the mean target. With the cross-fitted protocol of our main
experiments this advantage disappears: the corrected operator is biased by
0.17--0.80 and the uncorrected one by 0.11--0.61, because the nuisances, and in
particular the degree weights, depend on the exposures of the walk edges, and
this dependence partly offsets the repeated-walk excess of the uncorrected
operator. The dependence bias shrinks as users have more exposures (from 0.80
at 11 to 0.18 at 60 per user) but does not vanish. Degrees from edge estimates
make the uncorrected operators much worse (up to 4.1). The sample split
restores the guarantee: relative to the target of
Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased} with $\hat Y$ on the imputed pairs, the bias of
DRUP-split is 0.007--0.065, and relative to the full-exposure target it is
0.07--0.18 with $q=0.2$, against 0.07--1.09 for the uncorrected operator under the same
split; $q=0.5$ imputes too many pairs (0.13--0.42). The data-dependent
constants matter as well: with independent nuisances and DR edges, the full
score is biased by 0.18--0.35 when $c_1,c_3$ are computed from the scores and by
0.02--0.11 when they come from the imputed graph. On
unexposed candidates, the conditional target of Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}
differs from the full-exposure target by 12\% (three hops) and 6\% (five hops)
of the mean target in this model, and it agrees with it in within-user order
only up to Kendall $\tau=0.63$ and 0.77: this is the part of $F^*$ that no
estimator can recover on the candidates actually ranked. Results with
estimated propensities, which are misspecified in this model and bias every
operator, are in Table~\ref{tab:protocolfull} of Appendix~\ref{app:more}.

Stress tests outside the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased} are in
Appendix~\ref{app:more} (Table~\ref{tab:stress}). With fixed-size slates, which
make exposures within a user negatively dependent, IPS picks up a first-order
bias (0.22) whereas DRUP stays at 0.024, as Proposition~\ref{prop:dep} predicts;
with misspecified propensities DRUP is exactly unbiased when the imputation is
exact and biased by 0.16 when it is noisy, against 0.57 for IPS and 1.9 for the
uncorrected DR operator. The bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:var} were never violated
but are loose by a factor of 70 to 8{,}000 (Table~\ref{tab:bounds}).
