Table~\ref{tab:interv} tests the prediction of Theorem~\ref{thm:fair} with a known
intervention on exposure. The operators use the cross-fitted protocol, which
the theorem does not cover (Remark~\ref{rem:xfit}), so this is an empirical
check. The logged graph behaves as the theorem predicts for correlational
propagation: with frozen nuisances $\eta=+1.00$ to $+1.03$ on all three datasets, and
thinned items drop by 0.07--0.24 in mean within-user rank. On KuaiRec and Coat,
DRUP is invariant to within a few hundredths ($\eta=-0.012$ and $-0.008$) and stays
so when its nuisances are re-fitted on the thinned log, with the known or with
re-estimated propensities where they are estimated ($|\eta|\le0.022$). IPS adjacency is far from invariant
($\eta=+0.14$ on KuaiRec, $+0.90$ on Coat). The uncorrected DR adjacency is as
invariant as DRUP on these data; its over-correction ($\eta=-0.19$ in
Table~\ref{tab:mc}) scales with $(1-p)/p\cdot\varepsilon^2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}), and
the selected clips and imputations keep it small here.

Yahoo!\,R3 is the exception, and Table~\ref{tab:tau} explains it. There,
validation selects IPS edges ($\lambda=0$) with a clip of 0.1--0.2, which binds for
82\% of the logged pairs at $\tau=0.2$. A clipped IPS edge is $O_eY_e/\tau$, proportional
to the exposure itself, so DRUP inherits the elasticity of the logged graph
($\eta=+0.96$). On Coat the clip binds just as often (77\%), but with DR edges
($\lambda=1$) and an accurate imputation the clipping bias is small
(Theorem~\ref{thm:var}(c)) and invariance survives. Lowering the clip on Yahoo!\,R3
reduces the elasticity to $+0.18\pm0.11$ at $\tau=5\cdot10^{-4}$, where almost no
propensity is clipped, at a cost of 0.044 nDCG@5; the remaining elasticity and
its large spread over the thinning draws come from heavy-tailed
inverse-propensity weights on a log that is 2\% dense. Exposure invariance is
therefore a property of the estimator under its assumptions, and whether a
tuned configuration has it must be checked; the thinning audit is a simple way
to do so.

Table~\ref{tab:fair} shows the distributional side. On KuaiRec, the
exposure-conditional bias drops from 0.83 (logged graph) and 0.80 (IPS) to 0.33
for DRUP and DR adjacency: given their true quality, items are ranked much less
by how often they were shown. At the same time DRUP's top-20 lists are
concentrated on 1.2\% of the candidate items, against 15\% for the logged graph,
because the estimated full-exposure scores are themselves concentrated on few items; on Coat its Gini
coefficient is 0.60 against 0.34. Exposure invariance and exposure concentration
are different properties, and DRUP improves the first at the expense of the
second. The user-group gaps do not differ between operators. They are clearly
non-zero on Coat (men 0.11 above women) and on Yahoo!\,R3 (inactive users 0.046
below active ones), under every operator, and not on KuaiRec.
