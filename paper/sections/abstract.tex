Graph recommenders propagate preferences along walks of a logged graph whose
edges exist only where an item was exposed. Debiasing re-weights edges by
inverse propensities or completes them with doubly robust (DR) estimates, but
such corrections are biased for multi-hop propagation: a walk that traverses an
edge several times multiplies its estimate with itself. We give the exact bias,
whose worst case grows as the inverse of the propensity clip, and remove it with
DRUP (Doubly Robust, walk-Unbiased Propagation), which replaces each repeated
edge by its idempotent counterpart; Möbius inversion extends the correction to
any odd number of hops at the asymptotic cost of the uncorrected operator. The
guarantees (edge-wise double robustness, second-order robustness to within-user
exposure dependence, exposure invariance in expectation) require nuisances that
do not depend on the log. In simulation they hold for nuisances from an
independent log or a sample split, not for nuisances cross-fitted on the same
log, which is what most pipelines, including our main experiments, do. The
contribution concerns score values. On a semi-synthetic KuaiRec log with known
exposure, the correction removes the 18--29\% overestimate of full-exposure
utility of uncorrected operators; on Coat, Yahoo!\,R3, KuaiRand and KuaiRec it
does not change accuracy or rankings against 26 tuned baselines, also under
exposure caps. The most accurate method differs by dataset, and the sample split
costs accuracy on sparse logs. Real-data thinning interventions confirm exposure
invariance where the selected configuration uses DR edges, not where it uses
clipped IPS edges.
