Graph recommenders propagate preferences along walks of a logged graph whose
edges exist only where an item was exposed. Debiasing re-weights edges by
inverse propensities or completes them with doubly robust (DR) estimates. For
multi-hop propagation this is biased: a walk that traverses an edge several
times multiplies its estimate with itself. We give the exact bias and remove it
with DRUP (Doubly Robust, walk-Unbiased Propagation), which replaces each
repeated edge by its idempotent counterpart; Möbius inversion extends the
correction to any odd number of hops. The guarantees require nuisances that do not depend on the log.
In simulation, cross-fitting suffices only if the degree weights are fixed a priori;
with degrees taken from the log, as in our experiments, the correction no longer reduces bias. The correction
changes no ranking: on Coat, Yahoo!\,R3, KuaiRand and KuaiRec its accuracy equals
that of uncorrected DR propagation, and the most accurate method differs by
dataset. It matters for score values, but only under oracle conditions: on a
semi-synthetic KuaiRec log it removes an 18--29\% overestimate of an
allocation's utility when propensities are true and weights fixed; with
estimated propensities all operators overstate it by 100--540\%. The
sample split that satisfies the assumptions costs up to 0.06 nDCG on sparse logs.
A real-data thinning audit of exposure invariance has limited power: its negative
control fails where invariance holds, and the selected configurations are not
invariant on Yahoo!\,R3 and KuaiRand. Private release is useful only on the largest log.
