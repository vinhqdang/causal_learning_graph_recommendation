Graph recommenders propagate preferences along walks of a logged graph whose
edges exist only where an item was exposed. Debiasing re-weights edges by
inverse propensities or completes them with doubly robust (DR) estimates. For
multi-hop propagation this is biased: a walk that traverses an edge several
times multiplies its estimate with itself. We give the exact bias and remove it
with DRUP (Doubly Robust, walk-Unbiased Propagation), which replaces each
repeated edge by its idempotent counterpart; Möbius inversion extends the
correction to any odd number of hops. The guarantees require nuisances that do
not depend on the log. In simulation, cross-fitting suffices only if the degree
weights are fixed a priori; with degrees taken from the log, as in our
experiments, the correction no longer reduces bias. We find no detectable change
in accuracy: the corrected and uncorrected DR operators are equivalent within
0.005 nDCG on KuaiRec, marginally on Coat, and identical on Yahoo!\,R3 and KuaiRand,
and the most accurate method differs by dataset. The correction matters for score values, but only under
oracle conditions: on a semi-synthetic KuaiRec log it removes an 18--29\%
overestimate of an allocation's utility when propensities are true and weights
fixed, while with estimated propensities all operators overstate it by
100--540\%. The sample split that satisfies the assumptions costs up to 0.06 nDCG
on sparse logs. A real-data thinning audit of exposure invariance has limited
power, and the selected configurations are not invariant on Yahoo!\,R3 and
KuaiRand. Private release is useful only on the largest log.
