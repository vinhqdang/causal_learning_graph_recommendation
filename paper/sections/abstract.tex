Graph recommenders propagate preferences along walks of a logged graph whose
edges exist only where an item was exposed. Debiasing re-weights
edges by inverse propensities or completes them with doubly robust (DR)
estimates. Such corrections are biased for multi-hop propagation: a walk that
traverses an edge several times multiplies its estimate with itself. We give the exact bias, whose worst case
grows as the inverse of the propensity clip, and remove it with DRUP (Doubly
Robust, walk-Unbiased Propagation), which replaces each repeated edge by its
idempotent counterpart; at three hops the correction has the asymptotic cost
of the uncorrected operator, and Möbius inversion extends it exactly to any odd
number of hops. With
nuisances that do not depend on the log, DRUP is edge-wise doubly robust for
full-exposure propagation, second-order robust to within-user exposure
dependence, and exposure-invariant in expectation; as a linear
operator it also admits exact attributions, certificates against injected
profiles and private release. In simulation these guarantees hold
for nuisances from an independent log or a sample split (for a target with
imputed split-off pairs), not for nuisances cross-fitted on the same log. The contribution concerns
scores: on Coat, Yahoo!\,R3 and KuaiRec, against 22 tuned methods, the
correction leaves accuracy and rankings unchanged, also under exposure caps. Training-free DR propagation is the most accurate method on
KuaiRec, while graph models trained with pairwise losses are better on
Yahoo!\,R3. Real-data interventions confirm exposure invariance on Coat and
KuaiRec, but not at the selected configuration on the sparse Yahoo!\,R3 log.
