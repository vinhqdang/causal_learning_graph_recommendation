# v4 design: an evaluation study of exposure-bias corrections for graph recommenders

Status: design document, 2026-11-04. No file outside `paper/v4/` has been changed. Every number below
is copied from a file in this repository, and its source is given. Two kinds of number are **not**
from stored files, and each is marked where it appears:
- "probe": computed for this design from stored files with a throw-away script. These are indicative
  and must be regenerated by the scripts in Section 3.4 before they appear in the paper.
- "TODO": the number does not exist yet. A new run or analysis must produce it.

Rules carried over from `paper/v2/OUTLINE.md` Section 0: no "state of the art", "novel framework" or
"first"; "significant" only when p_Holm < 0.05; paired per-user differences are given as x1e-3 with
the 95% CI half-width in brackets, copied from the files and not computed by subtracting table means;
and the manuscript never says how it or the code was produced.

---

## 0. What changed and why this paper

History in this repository:
1. **IPM.** The DRUP theory paper was rejected.
2. **Information Sciences.** The QP-LightGCN method paper (`paper/v2/`) was desk-rejected for
   novelty (`paper/v2/SUBMISSION_NOTES.md`).
3. **DRGP.** The method attempt (`paper/v3/DESIGN.md`, results in `results/v5/final_v6_stage1.txt`)
   failed its Stage 1 gates:
   - Coat: DRGP final 0.5992 against iALS + additive prior 0.5984 (+3.08e-3 [12.20], p_Holm 1.0)
     and QP-LightGCN 0.6015 (-0.13e-3 [15.11]).
   - KuaiRec: DRGP final 0.6318 against DRUP 0.6378 (-5.58e-3 [1.90], p_Holm < 1e-4).

Three method papers' worth of experiments therefore point the same way. Under a clean protocol,
the gains on these benchmarks come from a few cheap ingredients, and the effects are of the same size
as the variation the evaluation itself introduces. That body of evidence is the contribution. The
paper reports it as an evaluation study. It does not propose a method.

### 1.2 Thesis

> On four benchmarks with unbiased test data (Coat, Yahoo! R3, KuaiRand-Pure, KuaiRec), once uniform
> data are kept out of training and model selection is done per split on validation data only,
> exposure-bias corrections built into graph recommenders change ranking accuracy by a few
> thousandths of nDCG. Their effects are at most about +0.004 over the equally tuned LightGCN
> backbone on three datasets. That is smaller than three things: the seed-to-seed spread of a
> single model, the inflation caused by selecting on test data, and the effect of two simple
> ingredients:
> 1. A fixed, inverse-propensity-fitted additive imputation (in effect, a debiased item-quality
>    score) added to the score of any learner.
> 2. On the fully observed KuaiRec, a training-free doubly robust propagation of the log, which no
>    trained model beats.
>
> Making the propagation exactly unbiased (walk correction) does not change ranking accuracy on real
> data, although it removes the bias on oracle logs.

### 1.3 Contributions (3 + 1)

**C1. A leakage-controlled benchmark with exact statistical statements, and its sensitivity.**
- About 25 methods on four unbiased-test benchmarks under one protocol:
  - uniform data only for validation and test;
  - per-split selection of (configuration, epoch) on validation;
  - per-user paired tests, Holm within dataset, Nadeau-Bengio-corrected tests as a robustness
    check;
  - seeds 1-3 for the leading methods;
  - configuration counts reported per method.
- The new part is the **quantified sensitivity of conclusions to evaluation choices**. On the
  same stored results, the paper measures:
  - (i) selection on test instead of validation;
  - (ii) grid size, via expected maximum validation performance as a function of the budget;
  - (iii) seed;
  - (iv) the early-stopping rule;
  - (v) the Holm family.
- It also states, for each pairwise conclusion, whether it survives each choice.
- Probe on Coat (stored banks, nDCG@5, validation-selected → per-split test oracle):

  | Method | Validation-selected | Per-split test oracle | Inflation |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | iALS | 0.5803 | 0.5909 | +0.011 |
  | LightGCN | 0.5683 | 0.5925 | +0.024 |
  | r-AdjNorm | 0.5722 | 0.5926 | +0.020 |
  | NAVIP | 0.5667 | 0.5922 | +0.026 |
  | DR-LightGCN | 0.5786 | 0.6011 | +0.023 |
  | AdvInfoNCE | 0.5220 | 0.5505 | +0.029 |
  | DR-JL | 0.4889 | 0.5170 | +0.028 |
  | LightGCN-p | 0.6025 | 0.6203 | +0.018 |

  Test-selection inflation is of the same size as the method differences being claimed (0.003 to
  0.03). The values are a probe only; they must be regenerated by A1.

- SimGCL, MACR and the AdvInfoNCE re-implementation are below LightGCN everywhere.
- By contrast, adding the fixed additive IPS prior as a score offset gives:
  - LightGCN on Coat: +31.98e-3 [20.65], p = 0.0025 (probe; LightGCN-p vs LightGCN).
  - iALS on Coat: +16.8e-3 [18.0], p = 0.068 (probe; iALS + additive prior vs iALS), which is not
    significant.
  - LightGCN on KuaiRec: 0.6085 → 0.6288 (`s6_results.tex` line 105).
- Training-free DR propagation is the best method on KuaiRec. Ensemble vs DR-JL: +4.20e-3 [2.19],
  p_Holm 0.0005 (`results/v4/ensemble_summary.txt`). DRUP vs DR-JL: +0.0044 (`paper/tables/nbtost.tex`).

**C3. A pre-specified family of refinements, all reported, with negative controls.**
- The family has seven variants that add machinery on top of the prior:
  1. Q-posterior negative weighting;
  2. Q weighting with the offset (QP-LightGCN);
  3. Q from the imputation only, or from popularity;
  4. a low-rank instead of an additive imputation;
  5. a validated ensemble with DRUP;
  6. a leave-edge-out DR-fitted graph prior (DRGP) in iALS and LightGCN;
  7. Q-weighted iALS.
- None moves Coat by more than +3.1e-3 over the simple prior in the same learner. All of these
  comparisons are non-significant.
- On KuaiRec, none moves above DR-JL/MRDR or DRUP.
- Negative controls: a shuffled Q returns to the LightGCN level (Coat 0.5689 vs 0.5683; Yahoo 0.6689
  vs 0.6708). A shuffled or popularity prior offset is the new control (X1).
- This is reported as a file-drawer-free account. Every variant that was run is listed with its
  configuration count, including those that were proposed as methods in earlier submissions.

**C4 (secondary). Exact unbiasedness of multi-hop debiased propagation is not the bottleneck for
ranking.**
- The walk correction removes the repeated-edge bias of DR propagation. On a synthetic MNAR model,
  relative |bias| falls from 1.756 to 0.019 at K = 3 and from 8.350 to 0.039 at K = 5
  (`paper/tables/mc.tex`). It also stays small under slate dependence (`mc_stress.tex`).
- It improves nDCG on a semi-synthetic KuaiRec log only with true propensities and fixed degree
  weights (0.097 → 0.107, `semisynth.tex`).
- With realistic (imputation-based) degrees it does not help: 0.377 vs 0.370.
- On real data at matched configurations it is neutral or harmful
  (`results/v3/matched_config_real.txt`).
- The lesson for estimator design is that bias in the operator is dominated by ranking-irrelevant
  components.

### 1.4 What is NOT claimed

- No new method. QP-LightGCN, DRGP and DRUP appear as members of the evaluated family, not as
  contributions. The word "propose" applies only to the protocol and sensitivity analysis.
- Not that debiasing is useless in general. The scope is four public benchmarks, top-K nDCG at the
  per-dataset K used here (Coat/Yahoo @5, KuaiRand @10, KuaiRec @20), offline, implicit-feedback
  learners trained on the biased log.
- Not equivalence on Coat. Coat is underpowered (minimum detectable effect about 0.017 to 0.029,
  Section 2.3). Coat statements are "no detectable difference; differences larger than ±X are
  excluded" with X taken from the TOST of A2. They are never "equal".
- Not that AdvInfoNCE, NAVIP, r-AdjNorm, SimGCL or MACR "do not work". The claim is that **our
  implementations, tuned with the stated budgets under this protocol**, do not beat the backbone.
  AdvInfoNCE is a simplified re-implementation (`s5_setup.tex`, Section "AdvInfoNCE
  re-implementation").
- Not that DR propagation is generally superior. It is best on KuaiRec, the only fully observed
  test matrix with about 3,300 candidates per user. On Yahoo/KuaiRand it reduces to IPS adjacency
  (0.6567 / 0.4492) and loses to trained models.
- No claim about why the prior helps beyond what X1 shows. The current reading ("a debiased
  item-quality score that uses exposed negatives which BPR discards") is a hypothesis until X1 is
  run.
  - Note for the writer: the additive imputation is mu + b_u + b_i (`drup/estimation.py`
    `baseline_imputation`). Within a user its ranking is the ranking of b_i, a shrunken
    IPS-weighted item mean of the logged outcomes.

## 2. Why this is publishable, and what reviewers will say

### 2.1 Novelty framed as findings

1. **Setting not covered by the existing "progress" critiques.**
   - Dacrema et al. (`dacrema2019progress`), Rendle et al. (`rendle2019baselines`) and Anelli et al.
     (`anelli2023myth`) re-evaluate accuracy on random splits of biased logs.
   - "Debias Can be Unreliable" (arXiv 2409.04810, 2024) questions the *evaluation metric* for
     debiasing on randomly exposed tests, using KuaiRec. It does not re-tune graph debiasers or
     quantify selection effects.
   - Our study re-evaluates **debiasing for graph recommenders on unbiased tests**. That setting is
     where the claimed gains of NAVIP, AdvDrop, PPD, DPAA and DR-GNN are made
     (`paper/v2/lit_search.md` Section 2.1). It also quantifies how evaluation choices flip
     conclusions.
2. **Protocol heterogeneity is documented.** `lit_search.md` Section 3 tabulates how 14 papers use
   uniform data:
   - AutoDebias, CausE, ConAlign and KDCRec train on it.
   - CDR and PPD select on it.
   - DCE and BISER avoid it entirely.

   We show that within one protocol, the selection-on-test inflation (C1 probe: +0.011 to +0.029 on
   Coat) is as large as the gains these papers report. This is the core empirical message. It is
   exact and checkable from the released banks.
3. **Exact negative statements.** Every "no effect" claim carries:
   - a paired difference with CI;
   - a minimum detectable effect;
   - for the central comparisons, a TOST with a margin declared before the analysis (Section 2.3).

   This turns a "negative result" into a bounded-effect result.
4. **Theory/practice gap (C4).** An estimator that is exactly unbiased for the multi-hop preference
   graph, with proofs (old `paper/sections/method_theory.tex`, Theorems `thm:unbiased`, `thm:khop`,
   `thm:lower`), gives no ranking benefit. This is a clean, citable negative result for the graph
   debiasing line.
5. **Released artefacts.** Per-(configuration, epoch, split) validation and test banks for about
   25 methods, which allow any reader to redo the selection analysis. This matches the April 2026
   ACM TORS policy on reproducibility materials and tuning documentation (Section 5).

### 2.3 Statistical statements to pre-register in the paper (Section 4.4 of the manuscript)

- **Primary comparisons**, declared before A2 is run. Holm over the primary family per dataset:
  - (P1) Best graph debiaser vs LightGCN, per dataset.
  - (P2) Simple prior (iALS + prior, LightGCN + prior) vs its backbone, on Coat and KuaiRec. On Yahoo
    and KuaiRand, LightGCN + prior only (after X3).
  - (P3) Best refinement vs simple prior in the same learner, on Coat and KuaiRec.
  - (P4) DR propagation vs best trained model on KuaiRec.
- **TOST equivalence margins** (one-sided α = 0.05 each), applied to P3 and to the walk correction
  (DRUP vs DR):
  - ±0.010 nDCG@5 on Coat and Yahoo;
  - ±0.003 nDCG@10 on KuaiRand;
  - ±0.003 nDCG@20 on KuaiRec.
  - Rationale: half of the smallest gain that the cited graph-debiasing papers report on these
    datasets (TODO-L2 to confirm). If L2 gives other values, fix the margins before running A2, and
    record the date in the repository.
- **MDE.** MDE at 80% power ≈ 1.43 × CI half-width (paired t, two-sided α = 0.05). From the stored
  half-widths:
  - Coat: 12.2e-3 to 27.6e-3 → MDE ≈ 0.017 to 0.039.
  - Yahoo: 5.2e-3 to 10e-3 → 0.007 to 0.014.
  - KuaiRand: 1.0e-3 to 4.0e-3 → 0.0015 to 0.006.
  - KuaiRec: 1.3e-3 to 6.6e-3 → 0.002 to 0.009.

  A2 computes them exactly per comparison.
- **Robustness columns:** Nadeau-Bengio p; seeds 1-3 p; matched-budget difference at k = the
  smaller grid.
