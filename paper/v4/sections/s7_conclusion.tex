\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We re-evaluated exposure-bias corrections for graph recommenders on four benchmarks with uniformly exposed test data, under one protocol in which uniform data serve for validation and testing, and, through Coat's shipped propensities, for one nuisance that we check. The study asked how much evaluation choices move conclusions, how much graph-specific debiasing adds over an equally tuned backbone, which ingredient carries the gains that remain, and whether exact unbiasedness of the propagation matters for ranking.

Three findings stand out. First, evaluation choices move the conclusions by amounts of the same order as the differences being compared. Selecting on test data inflates Coat scores by up to $0.030$ nDCG@5 and reverses the order of iALS and LightGCN, and a wider iALS grid raises iALS by $+12.92$ ($\times10^{-3}$). Second, graph-specific debiasers add at most $+4.10$ ($\times10^{-3}$) nDCG over LightGCN on Coat, Yahoo! R3 and KuaiRand, and none of these gains survives the Nadeau-Bengio correction. On KuaiRec the gains are larger, but a pointwise loss without any debiasing gives more. Third, an additive prior, fitted once on the exposed log and added to the score, carries most of the gain on Coat and KuaiRec, while the weights carry it on Yahoo! R3 and KuaiRand. The inverse-propensity weighting of the prior is not shown to matter, and the exact unbiasedness of the walk correction does not change accuracy on the real logs.

The study does not propose a method. It does not show that debiasing is ineffective in general, because its scope is four public benchmarks, one cut-off per dataset and offline evaluation of implicit-feedback learners trained on the biased log. It does not establish equivalence on Coat, where the study is underpowered, and it does not identify the mechanism by which the prior helps. For evaluation, the practical requirements are to select per split on validation data, to match budgets, to include the simple baselines, and to report minimum detectable effects and corrected tests with every claim that two methods are equal or that one is better.
