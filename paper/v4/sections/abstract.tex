Exposure-bias corrections for graph recommenders are usually compared on benchmarks with uniformly exposed test data, but their protocols, tuning budgets and selection rules differ. We re-evaluate these corrections on Coat, Yahoo! R3, KuaiRand-Pure and KuaiRec under one protocol. Uniform data are used for validation and testing, with one propensity exception on Coat that we check for the prior rows only. Configurations are selected per split on validation data, and the selected configurations of the leading methods are retrained with three further seeds. Selecting on test data inflates Coat scores by up to $0.030$ nDCG@5 and, under the per-split test oracle, reverses the order of iALS and LightGCN. Against an equally tuned LightGCN backbone, third-party graph debiasers give point-estimate gains of at most $+4.10$ ($\times10^{-3}$) nDCG on Coat, Yahoo! R3 and KuaiRand-Pure ($+5.26$ in the seed 1 to 3 re-runs). The Coat 95\% half-widths of these debiasers reach $\pm30$, and none of the gains survives a correction for overlapping splits. Our own QP-LightGCN family adds $+16.77$ on Yahoo! R3 and $+3.93$ on KuaiRand-Pure over LightGCN, and it is not significant after that correction. An additive prior fitted on the exposed log and added as a score offset gives the largest point-estimate gains over LightGCN on Coat and KuaiRec, and none on Yahoo! R3 or KuaiRand-Pure. Removing its inverse-propensity weights does not reduce the gain, a shuffled item term removes the KuaiRec gain, and popularity does not reproduce it. The Coat gain is not robust to the correction for overlapping splits. Making the multi-hop propagation exactly unbiased does not improve ranking on Coat, Yahoo! R3 or KuaiRec; DR and DRUP were not run on KuaiRand-Pure. We propose no new method. We do not claim that debiasing is ineffective in general, and we do not claim equivalence on Coat, which is underpowered.
