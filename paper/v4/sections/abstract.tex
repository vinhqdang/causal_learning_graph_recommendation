Exposure-bias corrections for graph recommenders are usually compared on benchmarks with uniformly exposed test data, but their protocols, tuning budgets and selection rules differ. We re-evaluate these corrections on Coat, Yahoo! R3, KuaiRand and KuaiRec under one protocol. Uniform data are used for validation and testing, apart from one checked propensity exception, configurations are selected per split on validation data, and the selected configurations of the leading methods are retrained with three further seeds. Selecting on test data inflates Coat scores by up to $0.030$ nDCG@5 and reverses the order of iALS and LightGCN. Against an equally tuned LightGCN backbone, graph-specific debiasers add at most $+4.10$ ($\times10^{-3}$) nDCG on Coat, Yahoo! R3 and KuaiRand, and none of these gains survives a correction for overlapping splits. An additive prior fitted on the exposed log and added as a score offset carries most of the gain on Coat and KuaiRec. Exact unbiasedness of the propagation does not improve ranking on real logs. We propose no new method. We do not claim that debiasing is ineffective in general, and we do not claim equivalence on Coat, which is underpowered.
