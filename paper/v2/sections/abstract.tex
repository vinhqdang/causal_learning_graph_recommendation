Graph recommenders learn from logs of items chosen only if shown, so most sampled negatives are unexposed. We propose a weighting and an offset built on the probability Q that an unlogged pair is positive, computed in closed form from an inverse-propensity imputation and an exposure propensity. Negatives are down-weighted by 1 - Q, and the standardised imputation logit is added to the score as a validated offset. Uniform data are used only for validation and testing, except the shipped Coat propensities, estimated from a uniform sample. With Holm-corrected per-user tests, the method has a higher mean than every graph baseline on all four datasets, including a re-implemented AdvInfoNCE. All differences are significant except against LightGCN-pt on KuaiRec ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.4409$). It is the best method we ran on Yahoo! R3 (nDCG@5 0.6866 vs 0.6742) and KuaiRand. On KuaiRec it is significantly lower than DR-JL and MRDR in the main run ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0002$ for both), but not across seeds. On Coat it is above iALS in the main run ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0469$), but not across seeds. Training-free propagation remains best on KuaiRec (0.6378). A shuffled-Q control removes the gain. On Coat, with unmatched grids (108 vs 54 configurations), the offset alone recovers most of it.
