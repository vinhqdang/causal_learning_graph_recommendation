Graph recommenders trained on logs treat most sampled negatives as unexposed. We propose a weighting and an offset built on $Q$, the probability that an unlogged pair is positive, computed in closed form from an inverse-propensity imputation and an exposure propensity. Negatives are down-weighted by $1-Q$, and the standardised imputation logit is added to the score as an offset. Uniform data serve only for validation and testing, except Coat's propensities, estimated from uniform data. With Holm-corrected per-user tests, the method has a higher mean than every trained graph baseline in the Holm family on all four datasets, including a re-implemented AdvInfoNCE. The differences are significant except against LightGCN-pt on KuaiRec ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.4409$). DR-LightGCN was tested only on Coat and KuaiRec. It is the best we ran on Yahoo! R3 and KuaiRand, with a larger tuning budget than the baselines. On KuaiRec it is below DR-JL and MRDR in the main run ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0002$), not across seeds, and training-free DRUP and the DR adjacency (0.6378) are higher. On Coat it is above iALS in the main run ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0469$), but not across seeds. A shuffled-$Q$ control returns to the LightGCN level. On Coat (unmatched grids) and KuaiRec, the offset alone recovers most of the gain.
