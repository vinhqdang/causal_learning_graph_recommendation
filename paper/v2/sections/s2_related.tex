\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Graph collaborative filtering}

LightGCN \citep{he2020lightgcn} removes the feature transformations and non-linearities
of earlier graph convolutional recommenders \citep{wang2019ngcf}, which reduces
propagation to a weighted sum of powers of a normalised adjacency matrix. Later
variants approximate infinitely many propagation layers \citep{mao2021ultragcn} or add
contrastive views of the graph \citep{wu2021sgl,yu2022simgcl}. Closed-form and
linear models are strong competitors. EASE \citep{steck2019ease} learns an item--item
matrix in closed form, GF-CF \citep{shen2021gfcf} casts collaborative filtering as
graph signal filtering, and BSPM \citep{choi2023bspm} uses a related diffusion view.
Implicit matrix factorisation with weighted least squares \citep{hu2008ials} remains a
competitive baseline, and carefully tuned simple methods often match published graph
models \citep{anelli2023myth}. A survey of graph-based recommenders is given by
\citet{wu2022gnnsurvey}. These models fit or propagate over the logged graph and do not
model which pairs were exposed. The present method keeps the propagation of LightGCN and
changes the training signal.

\subsection{Debiasing under missing-not-at-random feedback}

Ratings and implicit feedback are missing not at random
\citep{marlin2009collaborative}, and surveys organise the resulting biases and the
causal methods that address them \citep{chen2023bias,gao2024causalsurvey}. Propensity
methods re-weight the loss by inverse propensities or self-normalised estimators
\citep{schnabel2016,swaminathan2015snips,saito2020unbiased}. Steck \citep{steck2010mnar}
studied how training and evaluation should account for missingness when a uniformly
sampled subset is available. Doubly robust learning adds an imputation model
\citep{wang2019drjl}, and later variants reduce its bias or variance
\citep{guo2021mrdr,dai2022gdr,li2023stabledr}. An information-theoretic counterfactual
formulation is given by \citet{wang2020cvib}. Some debiasing methods use a small uniform
sample for training, as in AutoDebias \citep{chen2021autodebias} and CausE
\citep{bonner2018cause}. Causal approaches to popularity bias use counterfactual
reasoning \citep{wei2021macr}, confounder adjustment \citep{zhang2021pda} or
disentangled representations \citep{zheng2021dice}, and later work studies popularity
debiasing through the balance of user and item perspectives \citep{boratto2021connecting},
controllable trade-offs \citep{okamura2024flexibly} and invariant learning
\citep{bai2025invariant}. These methods correct the loss or the output of a model. The
present method applies propensity and DR estimation to the nuisances that define which
pairs act as negatives.

\subsection{False negatives and negative weighting in implicit feedback}

This is the closest group of methods. ExpoMF \citep{liang2016exposure} treats exposure
as a latent variable and infers an exposure posterior for unclicked items within a
matrix factorisation model. Rel-MF \citep{saito2020unbiased} re-weights observations
by propensity, and unbiased pairwise learning \citep{saito2020ictir} applies the same
idea to pairwise objectives. Sampling-based methods treat false negatives directly.
SRNS \citep{ding2020srns} identifies likely false negatives in the sampling step, and
debiased contrastive learning \citep{chuang2020dcl} corrects the sampling of negatives
that are in fact positives. In contrastive collaborative filtering, the bias-aware
margins of the BC loss \citep{zhang2022bc} and the adversarial re-weighting of negatives
in AdvInfoNCE \citep{zhang2023advinfonce} change how hard negatives are weighted.
The present weight $1-Q$ has the posterior form of ExpoMF but is computed in closed form
from the nuisances of a DR estimator. It is applied to a graph model with a pairwise
loss and is combined with a prior offset. The original formulations of ExpoMF, SRNS and
AdvInfoNCE do not use a propensity and an imputation of this kind.

\subsection{Debiasing inside graph models}

NAVIP \citep{kim2022navip} weights neighbour aggregation by inverse propensities inside
a graph neural recommender. r-AdjNorm \citep{zhao2022radjnorm} tunes the normalisation
of the adjacency matrix to control popularity amplification, and graph convolutions are
analysed as amplifiers of popularity bias by \citet{chen2024gcnpop}. APDA
\citep{zhou2023apda}, CAGED \citep{que2025caged} and DPAA \citep{dpaa2026} learn
aggregation weights or debiased message passing. These methods change which adjacency
is propagated or how neighbours are aggregated. The present method leaves the
propagation of LightGCN unchanged. It changes which pairs count as negatives in the
loss and adds a prior to the score.

\subsection{Score offsets and priors}

Adding a fixed offset to a score is a familiar device. In long-tail classification,
logit adjustment adds a class prior to the logits \citep{menon2021logit}, and in
generalised linear models an offset is a known term of the linear predictor. The offset
used here is different in one respect: it is a debiased estimate of population-level
preference, obtained from an inverse-propensity-weighted imputation, and not a
class prior.

\subsection{Evaluation protocols and the gap}

Progress claims in top-$N$ recommendation depend on the strength of the baselines and on
the evaluation protocol \citep{dacrema2019progress,rendle2019baselines,anelli2023myth}.
Per-user comparisons of models must account for the overlap between evaluation splits,
since standard t-tests treat the splits as independent \citep{nadeau2003}. Sampled
evaluation metrics can also be inconsistent with their exact counterparts
\citep{krichene2020sampled}. Section~\ref{sec:setup} describes the protocol used here.

Three lines of work meet in this paper but have rarely been combined. Doubly robust and
propensity methods (IPS, DR-JL, MRDR) are pointwise and are usually applied to matrix
factorisation. Graph recommenders that address exposure or popularity bias (NAVIP,
r-AdjNorm, APDA, CAGED) change the adjacency or the aggregation rather than the training
signal. False-negative-aware sampling (ExpoMF, SRNS, AdvInfoNCE) does not use the
propensity and imputation that DR estimation provides. We are not aware of a previous
study that uses the DR nuisances to weight the negatives of a graph recommender, and few
evaluate graph debiasing on Coat, Yahoo!\,R3, KuaiRand and KuaiRec together with
uniform data held out from training. Some debiasing methods train on uniformly collected data
\citep{chen2021autodebias,bonner2018cause}, and several studies report that tuned simple
baselines are competitive with published recommenders
\citep{dacrema2019progress,rendle2019baselines,anelli2023myth}.
