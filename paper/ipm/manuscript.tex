\title{Walk-unbiased doubly robust graph propagation for recommendation from
exposure-biased logs}

% Anonymised for double-anonymised review: author details are given on the
% separate title page (title_page.tex).

\begin{abstract}
Graph recommenders propagate preferences along multi-hop walks of a logged
graph whose edges exist only where an item was exposed. Propagation-level debiasing re-weights these edges by inverse propensities
or completes them with doubly robust (DR) estimates. We show that such
corrections are biased for multi-hop propagation: a walk that traverses an edge
several times multiplies that edge's estimate with itself. We give the exact bias, whose worst case grows as the
inverse of the propensity clip, and remove it with DRUP (Doubly Robust,
walk-Unbiased Propagation), which replaces every repeated edge by its idempotent
unbiased counterpart. Möbius inversion over walk-index coincidences makes the correction exact for any odd number of hops at the cost of
the uncorrected operator. With fixed nuisances, DRUP is edge-wise doubly robust
for propagation over the full-exposure graph, its bias under within-user
exposure dependence is of second order in the imputation error, and its expected
scores do not respond to interventions on exposure. As a linear operator it also admits exact
attributions, certificates against injected profiles and a jointly private
release, within a stated scope. On Coat, Yahoo!\,R3 and KuaiRec,
against 22 tuned methods, the correction leaves top-$K$ accuracy unchanged. Training-free propagation over the DR graph is the most accurate
method on KuaiRec (fully observed test matrix) while graph models trained
with pairwise losses are better on Yahoo!\,R3. Real-data interventions confirm
exposure invariance on Coat and KuaiRec; on the sparse Yahoo!\,R3 log it requires
small clips that cost accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================

Graph-based recommender systems represent users and items as nodes and
logged interactions as edges, and they score a user--item pair by aggregating
signals along multi-hop walks~\citep{he2020lightgcn,shen2021gfcf}. The logged
graph, however, is not the preference graph. An edge exists only if the
platform \emph{exposed} the item and the user then responded, so the graph
records the exposure policy as much as the user's taste. A recent survey of
causal learning in graph-based recommendation~\citep{yu2026survey} traces
several open problems of the field, among them fairness, interpretability and
out-of-distribution behaviour, to message passing over this observational
graph, and it lists theoretical guarantees and privacy as open directions.

Two families of corrections exist. \emph{Loss-level} debiasing re-weights the
training objective by inverse propensities or doubly robust (DR)
terms~\citep{schnabel2016,wang2019drjl,saito2020unbiased}, but it leaves the
propagation operator of a graph model untouched. \emph{Propagation-level}
debiasing changes the propagated graph itself, for example by weighting each
edge by its inverse propensity~\citep{kim2022navip} or by learning
popularity-aware aggregation weights~\citep{zhou2023apda,dpaa2026}. This paper
starts from a simple observation about the second family. An inverse-propensity
edge estimate $W_e=O_eY_e/p_e$ is unbiased for the potential outcome $Y_e$,
but a walk that traverses $e$ twice contributes $W_e^2$, and
$\E[W_e^2]=Y_e/p_e\neq Y_e$. The same happens for DR edge estimates. Walks that
repeat an edge are part of every multi-hop operator: the back-and-forth step
$u\to j\to u$ of a three-hop walk is one. Their excess is largest where the
propensity is smallest, that is, on the rarely exposed edges that debiasing is
meant to recover.

We make this observation precise and remove the resulting bias exactly. Two
points qualify its practical reach, and we state them up front. First, for
ranking the \emph{unexposed} candidates of a user with inverse-propensity
edges, the repeated walks through the candidate vanish at three hops (the
candidate's own estimate is zero), and the bias only appears from five hops
on; for DR edges it is present at three hops. Second, on the three public
benchmarks we use, removing the bias does not change top-$K$ accuracy. The
value of the correction is therefore that the propagated scores become
unbiased estimates of a well-defined counterfactual quantity, with guarantees
that can be checked, rather than an accuracy gain.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Diagnosis.} We give the exact conditional bias of inverse-propensity
and DR graph propagation on unexposed candidates. It is unbounded as
propensities vanish, and under a propensity clip $\tau$ its worst case is
$\Theta(1/\tau)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}).
\item \emph{Estimator.} DRUP (Doubly Robust, walk-Unbiased Propagation) uses
$Y_e^2=Y_e$ for binary outcomes and replaces every repeated edge by its
idempotent unbiased counterpart. The three-hop operator has the cost of one
item Gram matrix and factorises into a public item operator and per-user
corrections. Möbius inversion over the coincidence patterns of walk indices
makes the correction exact for any odd number of hops
(Theorem~\ref{thm:khop}).
\item \emph{Guarantees.} With fixed nuisances DRUP is edge-wise doubly robust
for propagation over the full-exposure graph (Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased}); on
unexposed candidates it is unbiased up to the imputation error at the
candidate itself, which no estimator can remove
(Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}); its bias under exposure dependence within a
user's history is of second order in the imputation error
(Proposition~\ref{prop:dep}); we bound its variance and clipping bias
(Theorem~\ref{thm:var}); and its expected scores do not respond to
interventions on an item's exposure (Theorem~\ref{thm:fair}).
\item \emph{Structural properties.} As a linear operator with fixed nuisances,
DRUP also admits exact attributions and optimal counterfactual explanations,
closed-form certificates against injected profiles, a jointly private release
of its item operator and an exposure-capped allocation. These properties
follow from linearity and hold for any linear propagation operator, including
the uncorrected ones; we state them with their scope (frozen or public
nuisances) and report where they are vacuous.
\item \emph{Evaluation.} On Coat~\citep{schnabel2016},
Yahoo!\,R3~\citep{marlin2009collaborative} and KuaiRec~\citep{gao2022kuairec},
whose test data are random or fully observed, we compare 23 methods under
one protocol: cross-fitted nuisances, per-split validation for every method,
and user-level significance tests with Holm correction. We check each
guarantee by Monte Carlo, including stress tests outside its assumptions, and
by real-data interventions on exposure.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
%======================================================================

We review six lines of work that DRUP touches. Table~\ref{tab:related}
summarises how DRUP relates to representative methods.

\paragraph{Graph-based recommendation}
Graph neural recommenders learn user and item representations by propagating
embeddings over the user--item graph. NGCF~\citep{wang2019ngcf} stacks
non-linear propagation layers, and LightGCN~\citep{he2020lightgcn} shows that
removing feature transformations and non-linearities improves accuracy, which
reduces propagation to a weighted sum of powers of a normalised adjacency.
UltraGCN~\citep{mao2021ultragcn} approximates infinite-layer propagation with a
constraint loss, and SGL~\citep{wu2021sgl} and SimGCL~\citep{yu2022simgcl} add
contrastive views. Closed-form models are strong competitors of trained
propagation: EASE~\citep{steck2019ease} learns an item--item matrix with a
zero diagonal in closed form, GF-CF~\citep{shen2021gfcf} and
BSPM~\citep{choi2023bspm} interpret collaborative filtering as graph signal
filtering, and graph convolutions can trade accuracy for
diversity~\citep{isufi2021accuracy}. Reproducibility studies show that carefully
tuned simple baselines often match graph models~\citep{anelli2023myth}. All these
models propagate over the \emph{logged} graph. DRUP keeps the linear
propagation form of LightGCN and of closed-form filters but propagates over an
unbiased estimate of the full-exposure graph, and we compare it with EASE and
GF-CF on both graphs.

\paragraph{Bias and debiasing in recommendation}
Recommendation logs are missing not at random: users see what the system
exposed and rate what they chose to rate~\citep{marlin2009collaborative}.
Surveys organise the resulting biases and their remedies~\citep{chen2023bias}
and cast debiasing as causal inference~\citep{gao2024causalsurvey}.
Propensity-based methods re-weight the loss by inverse exposure
probabilities~\citep{schnabel2016}, possibly self-normalised to reduce
variance~\citep{swaminathan2015snips}; \citet{saito2020unbiased} extend them
to implicit feedback, and \citet{liang2016exposure} model exposure as a
latent variable. Doubly robust learning combines inverse propensities with
an imputation model~\citep{wang2019drjl}, and later variants reduce its bias
and variance~\citep{guo2021mrdr,dai2022gdr,li2023stabledr} or learn
information-theoretic representations~\citep{wang2020cvib}. A small uniformly
logged sample can supervise the debiasing itself~\citep{chen2021autodebias}.
Causal approaches to popularity bias remove its direct effect by
counterfactual inference~\citep{wei2021macr}, adjust for it as a
confounder~\citep{zhang2021pda}, or disentangle interest from
conformity~\citep{zheng2021dice}. In this journal, popularity debiasing has
been studied from the user and item side jointly~\citep{boratto2021connecting},
with a controllable accuracy trade-off~\citep{okamura2024flexibly}, through
invariant learning with biased imputation~\citep{bai2025invariant}, and by
causal deconfounding of multi-criteria ratings~\citep{guo2026confounding}.
These methods correct the loss or the output of a model. DRUP applies IPS and
DR estimation to the \emph{edges} of the propagated graph, and its
control-variate family interpolates between the two.

\paragraph{Debiasing inside graph propagation}
Closest to our work are methods that change neighbour aggregation.
NAVIP~\citep{kim2022navip} weights each edge by its inverse propensity inside
LightGCN. r-AdjNorm~\citep{zhao2022radjnorm} shows that the normalisation
exponent of the adjacency controls how strongly popularity is amplified, and
tunes it. APDA~\citep{zhou2023apda} learns an adaptive inverse-popularity
weight per edge, CAGED~\citep{que2025caged} derives aggregation weights from a
backdoor adjustment, and DPAA~\citep{dpaa2026} learns interaction- and
layer-wise weights to counter the amplification of popularity by repeated
message passing. These methods change \emph{which} adjacency is propagated and
target popularity-related metrics. None of them analyses what happens to an
unbiased edge estimate when the re-weighted adjacency is multiplied with
itself over several hops. Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} shows that the product is
then biased, by a term of order $1/p$ that concentrates on rarely exposed
items, and DRUP removes this term exactly for any number of hops. Our linear
propagation baseline over the logged graph searches the normalisation exponent
and is therefore a training-free r-AdjNorm; we also train r-AdjNorm and NAVIP.

\paragraph{Unbiased estimation of polynomials under missingness}
The walk correction belongs to a classical idea: an unbiased estimator of a
polynomial in unknown quantities must avoid products of the same noisy
measurement, as in U-statistics~\citep{hoeffding1948}. For covariance matrices
estimated from Bernoulli-masked data, the off-diagonal entries of the masked
Gram matrix need a $1/p^2$ correction and the diagonal a $1/p$
correction~\citep{lounici2014}; our diagonal-corrected item operator $G$ is the
analogue for inverse-propensity and DR edges with heterogeneous propensities.
Unbiased subgraph counts from sampled edges~\citep{tsourakakis2009doulion},
and the passage from homomorphism counts to injective counts by Möbius
inversion on the partition lattice~\citep{rota1964}, are the combinatorial
counterparts of our $K$-hop construction. What is new here is the combination of these
tools with DR edge estimates and degree weights for recommendation, the
public-operator form, and the consequences for exposure invariance. Our
treatment of nuisances follows double machine learning, whose cross-fitting we
adopt~\citep{chernozhukov2018dml}, and our clipping and control-variate choices
mirror doubly robust off-policy evaluation~\citep{dudik2011doubly}.

\paragraph{Fairness in recommendation}
Fairness notions in recommendation cover users and items, groups and
individuals, and outcomes and exposure~\citep{wang2023fairness}; a unifying
account of their measurement is given by~\citet{amigo2023fairness}, and
provider fairness and its relation to data characteristics have been analysed
in~\citet{gomez2022provider,deldjoo2021explaining}. On the item side, exposure can be allocated
in proportion to merit~\citep{singh2018exposure}, amortised over
time~\citep{biega2018equity} or under uncertainty about
merit~\citep{singh2021uncertainty}, and two-sided allocations can be guaranteed
by constrained matching~\citep{patro2020fairrec}. Fairness can also be audited by counterfactual
reasoning~\citep{cornacchia2023auditing}. DRUP contributes a
causal item-side property, \emph{exposure invariance}: its expected scores do
not respond to interventions on an item's exposure (Theorem~\ref{thm:fair}).
Exposure invariance is a necessary condition for merit-based exposure
fairness, not a distributive criterion: it removes the dependence of scores on
exposure history but can increase the concentration of recommendations. The
exposure-capped allocation (Theorem~\ref{thm:alloc}) is the distributive
mechanism; like the matching view of~\citet{patro2020fairrec} it is a
$b$-matching.

\paragraph{Explanations, robustness and privacy}
Explainable recommendation is surveyed by~\citet{zhang2020explainable}. For graph models,
GNNExplainer~\citep{ying2019gnnexplainer} and CF-GNNExplainer~\citep{lucic2022cfgnn}
search for important or minimal sets of edges, PRINCE~\citep{ghazimatin2020prince}
finds minimal sets of a user's actions whose removal changes a random-walk
recommendation, and CountER~\citep{tan2021counter} optimises counterfactual
explanations of aspect-based models. Counterfactual explanations have a legal
motivation~\citep{wachter2018counterfactual} and hidden
assumptions~\citep{barocas2020hidden}, and Shapley attributions depend on their
baseline~\citep{kumar2020shapley}. Recommenders can be manipulated by injected
profiles~\citep{lam2004shilling,li2016poisoning,fang2018poisoning}, and shilling
groups can be detected with graph convolutions~\citep{wang2022shilling};
PORE~\citep{jia2023pore} certifies robustness by ensembling models trained on
sub-sampled data. Private recommenders perturb item--item
statistics~\citep{mcsherry2009netflix}, factorisation models~\citep{liu2024dpmf} or
matrix completion under joint differential privacy~\citep{jain2018dpmc}, and
federated approaches keep ratings on the device~\citep{chai2021fedmf}. Because a
DRUP score with fixed nuisances is linear in each user's own interactions, its
attributions are exact and its minimal counterfactual explanations are found by
sorting (Proposition~\ref{prop:affine}, Theorem~\ref{thm:cf}); the same linearity
gives closed-form certificates against injected profiles
(Theorem~\ref{thm:robust}) and a jointly private release of one item operator
with an analytically calibrated Gaussian mechanism~\citep{balle2018analytic,hsu2014matchings}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dp}). These properties hold for any linear propagation
operator.

\section{Problem setting}
%======================================================================

Users $u\in[m]$, items $i\in[n]$ and pairs $e=(u,i)$ carry a binary potential
outcome $Y_e\in\{0,1\}$ (whether $u$ would like $i$ if it were shown) and an
exposure indicator $O_e\in\{0,1\}$. The log reveals $O$ and $O\odot Y$.

\begin{assumption}[Conditionally independent exposure]\label{as:unconf}
Conditionally on $Y$, the exposures $O_e$ are independent Bernoulli variables
with propensities $p_e=\Pr(O_e=1\mid Y)>0$.
\end{assumption}
The propensity may depend on the pair's own outcome, so the log may be missing
not at random. All expectations below are over $O$ given $Y$.

\begin{assumption}[Fixed nuisances]\label{as:fixed}
The propensity estimates $\hat p$, the imputation $\hat Y\in[0,1]^{m\times n}$ and
the edge weights $C$ are fixed given $Y$, i.e., they do not depend on $O$ (for
instance, they are fitted on an independent log). We use
$C_{ui}=d_u^{-\alpha}d_i^{-(1-\alpha)}$ with degrees $d$ taken from $\hat Y$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[Cross-fitting]\label{rem:xfit}
In the experiments $\hat p$ and $\hat Y$ are cross-fitted over ten random folds
of pairs: the nuisances used at a pair of fold $k$ are fitted on the other
nine folds~\citep{chernozhukov2018dml}. A nuisance therefore never depends on
its own pair, which is the dependence that matters for one-hop terms. It
still depends weakly on other pairs, each nuisance parameter aggregating
hundreds of interactions. We measure the consequences directly by re-fitting
the nuisances after interventions and attacks
(Sections~\ref{sec:fair} and~\ref{sec:struct}).
\end{remark}

With $\tY=C\odot Y$, the target is propagation over the full-exposure graph,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
F^*=a\,\tY+b\,T^*,\qquad T^*=\tY\tY^\top\tY ,
\end{equation}
with fixed constants $a,b\ge0$. $F^*$ is the linear LightGCN score one would
compute if every item had been shown to every user. It is a normative choice:
it treats potential outcomes as fixed and ignores preferences that exposure
itself shapes~\citep{chaney2018confounding,perdomo2020performative}
(Section~\ref{sec:disc}).

\section{DRUP}

\paragraph{Edge estimates}
With clipped propensities $\bar p=\max(\hat p,\tau)$,
\begin{equation}
W^{\mathrm{IPS}}_e=\frac{O_eY_e}{\bar p_e},\qquad
W^{\mathrm{DR}}_e=\hat Y_e+\frac{O_e(Y_e-\hat Y_e)}{\bar p_e}.
\end{equation}
Both have mean $Y_e$ when $\bar p=p$.

\paragraph{Where the plug-in operator fails}
The plug-in three-hop operator $(\tW\tW^\top\tW)_{ui}$, with $\tW=C\odot W$, sums
the products $\tW_{uj}\tW_{vj}\tW_{vi}$ over all walks $u\!-\!j\!-\!v\!-\!i$.
If $v\neq u$ and $j\neq i$ the three edges are distinct and, by independence,
the product is unbiased. The other walks repeat an edge
(Figure~\ref{fig:walks}): for $v=u$ the edge $(u,j)$ is traversed twice, for
$j=i$ the edge $(v,i)$, and for $v=u,j=i$ the edge $(u,i)$ three times. A
repeated factor $\tW_e^k$ has mean $C_e^k\E[W_e^k]\ne C_e^kY_e$; for IPS,
$\E[W_e^2]=Y_e/p_e$. Because $Y_e^k=Y_e$ for binary outcomes, the unbiased
replacement of $\tW_e^k$ is simply $C_e^kW_e$.

\paragraph{Idempotent walk correction}
Let $\Delta=\tW\odot\tW-C\odot C\odot W$, with row sums $r$ and column sums
$\kappa$. The corrected three-hop operator subtracts the excess of each walk
type:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drup}
\hat T=\tW\tW^\top\tW-\tW\odot(r\mathbf 1^\top-\Delta)-\tW\odot(\mathbf 1\kappa^\top-\Delta)
-(\tW^{\odot3}-C^{\odot3}\odot W).
\end{equation}
The second term removes the excess of type (b) walks, the third that of type
(c), and the last that of type (d); the subtracted $\Delta$ inside the first
two avoids counting type (d) twice. The DRUP score is $s=a\,\tW+b\,\hat T$.
In the experiments $a=1/c_1$ and $b=\beta/c_3$, where $c_1$ and $c_3$ are the
mean absolute values of the one- and three-hop terms over all scored users and
$\beta$ is selected on validation data, so $s$ is a fixed linear combination
of the two terms.

\paragraph{Public-operator form}
Define the diagonal-corrected item Gram matrix
$G=\tW^\top\tW-\diag(\tW^\top\tW)+\diag(\mathbf 1^\top(C\odot C\odot W))$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
\hat T_{u\cdot}=\tilde w_uG-\tilde w_u\odot(r_u\mathbf 1^\top-2\delta_u)-(\tilde w_u^{\odot3}-c_u^{\odot3}\odot w_u),
\end{equation}
where $\delta_u=\tilde w_u^{\odot2}-c_u^{\odot2}\odot w_u$ and lower-case rows
belong to user $u$. Equation~\eqref{eq:local} matches~\eqref{eq:drup} to machine
precision. $G$ is the only object shared across users
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:drup}). Its off-diagonal entries are products of distinct
edges; its diagonal is corrected from $\sum_u\tW_{ui}^2$ to
$\sum_uC_{ui}^2W_{ui}$, the analogue of the diagonal correction of a Gram
matrix estimated from masked data~\citep{lounici2014}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{DRUP (training-free)}\label{alg:drup}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require log $(O,O\odot Y)$; hyper-parameters $\tau,\alpha,\lambda,\beta$
\State fit $\hat p$ (logistic exposure model) and $\hat Y$ (IPS-weighted additive ridge, optionally plus an IPS-weighted low-rank ALS residual), cross-fitted over ten folds of pairs
\State $\bar p\gets\max(\hat p,\tau)$; \ $W\gets\hat Y+O\odot(Y-\hat Y)/\bar p$
\State $C\gets d_u^{-\alpha}d_i^{-(1-\alpha)}$ with $d$ from $\hat Y$
\State $G\gets\tW^\top\tW-\diag(\tW^\top\tW)+\diag(\mathbf 1^\top(C\odot C\odot W))$ \Comment{public operator}
\For{each user $u$ (locally)}
\State $\hat T_{u\cdot}\gets$ Eq.~\eqref{eq:local}; \ $s_u\gets a\,\tilde w_u+b\,\hat T_{u\cdot}$; rank unexposed items by $s_u$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Control-variate family}
More generally, $W^\lambda_e=O_eY_e/\bar p_e+\lambda(\hat Y_e-O_e\hat Y_e/\bar p_e)$
is unbiased for every $\lambda\in[0,1]$ under correct propensities. It
interpolates between IPS ($\lambda=0$) and DR ($\lambda=1$, the only member that
is also robust to propensity errors), and it equals the DR estimate with the
shrunk imputation $\lambda\hat Y$. With $\bar p=p$ its variance is
$\frac{1-p}{p}(Y-\lambda\hat Y)^2$. All results below hold for every member, and
$\lambda$ is selected on validation data.

\paragraph{Imputation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased} holds for any fixed $\hat Y$, so the imputation is a
design choice; its accuracy enters the variance and the clipping bias
(Theorem~\ref{thm:var}). We use an additive model $\mu+b_u+b_i$ fitted by
IPS-weighted ridge regression, or that model plus a rank-32 residual $UV^\top$
fitted by IPS-weighted alternating least squares. The additive imputation gives
every user the same item order on unexposed candidates, which makes the one-hop
DR term non-personalised; the low-rank imputation removes this limitation.

\paragraph{Complexity and scale}
One $n\times n$ Gram product and $O(mn)$ reductions, i.e., the cost of the
uncorrected operator. On KuaiRec (7{,}176 users, 10{,}728 items) one
configuration takes under a minute on four CPU cores. The dense $W$ and $G$
need $O(mn+n^2)$ memory, which limits the present implementation to catalogues
of about $10^4$--$10^5$ items. Because the correction only modifies diagonals
and row and column sums, it carries over to sparse or low-rank
representations of $W$, but we have not evaluated catalogues of $10^6$ items.

\section{Theory}

Proofs are in \ref{app:proofs}. Let $\varepsilon_e=|Y_e-\hat Y_e|$.

\subsection{Unbiasedness and its limits}

\begin{theorem}[Edge-wise double robustness]\label{thm:unbiased}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:unconf}--\ref{as:fixed}, let
$\delta_e=(p_e/\bar p_e-1)(Y_e-\hat Y_e)$. Then $\E[\hat T_{ui}]$ equals $T^*_{ui}$
with every distinct edge contributing $Y_e+\delta_e$ once. In particular, if
every edge has a correct propensity or a correct imputation, then
$\E[s]=F^*$.
\end{theorem}

Recommendations rank the \emph{unexposed} candidates of a user, and there
the candidate's own edge estimate is deterministic ($W_{ui}=\hat Y_{ui}$ for DR,
$0$ for IPS). The following corollary states what DRUP estimates on the
candidate set.

\begin{corollary}[Unexposed candidates]\label{cor:cand}
Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased} with $\delta\equiv0$,
\[
\E[s_{ui}\mid O_{ui}=0]=F^*_{ui}-(Y_{ui}-\hat Y_{ui})\,g_{ui},\qquad
g_{ui}=\frac{\partial F^*_{ui}}{\partial Y_{ui}}
=C_{ui}\Big(a+b\big(\textstyle\sum_{v\neq u}C_{vi}^2Y_{vi}+\sum_{j\neq i}C_{uj}^2Y_{uj}+C_{ui}^2\big)\Big)\ge0 .
\]
For IPS edges the same holds with $\hat Y_{ui}=0$. The residual is the
imputation error at the candidate itself, which no estimator built from the
log can remove.
\end{corollary}

At three hops the walks through $(u,i)$ are exactly those counted in
$g_{ui}$. With IPS edges they vanish on unexposed candidates, so the uncorrected
IPS operator has no repeated-walk bias there; from five hops on, walks repeat
edges that are not incident to the candidate (for example
$u\!-\!j\!-\!v\!-\!j\!-\!v\!-\!i$) and the bias reappears
(Table~\ref{tab:mc}).

\begin{theorem}[Any odd number of hops]\label{thm:khop}
For $K=2r+1$, a walk $U_0\!-\!J_1\!-\!U_1\!-\cdots-\!U_r\!-\!J_{r+1}$ has user
variables $U_0,\dots,U_r$ and item variables $J_1,\dots,J_{r+1}$, with $U_0=u$
and $J_{r+1}=i$. The equality pattern of an assignment is a pair $\pi$ of set
partitions of the user and item variables, and $\pi$ fixes the multiplicity
$k_e$ of every distinct edge. Let $g_\pi(\sigma)$ be the unconstrained
contraction, over the blocks of a coarser pattern $\sigma\ge\pi$, of
$\prod_eC_e^{k_e}W_e$ (corrected) or of $\prod_e\tW_e^{k_e}$ (plug-in), the
product running over the distinct edges of $\pi$. Then
\[
\hat T^{(K)}=\tW(\tW^\top\tW)^r+\sum_{\pi:\,\exists k_e>1}\ \sum_{\sigma\ge\pi}\mu(\pi,\sigma)\big(g^{\mathrm{corr}}_\pi(\sigma)-g^{\mathrm{plug}}_\pi(\sigma)\big),
\]
with $\mu$ the Möbius function of the product of the two partition lattices,
satisfies $\E\hat T^{(K)}=(\tY\tY^\top)^r\tY$ under the conditions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased} with $\delta\equiv0$.
\end{theorem}
The number of pairs $(\pi,\sigma)$ is 5, 99 and 2{,}790 for $K=3,5,7$, and for
$K=3$ the construction reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:drup}. Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased},
Corollary~\ref{cor:cand} and Theorem~\ref{thm:fair} carry over to any odd $K$.

Assumption~\ref{as:unconf} excludes slates of fixed size, where exposures in a
user's history are negatively dependent (Coat, for instance, collects exactly
24 ratings per user). The next result bounds the resulting bias.

\begin{proposition}[Exposure dependence within users]\label{prop:dep}
Suppose that exposures are independent across users but may be dependent within
a user's row, with $|\mathrm{Cov}(O_e,O_f\mid Y)|\le\rho\,p_ep_f$ for distinct $e,f$ in
the same row. With correct propensities,
\[
\big|\E\hat T_{ui}-T^*_{ui}\big|\ \le\ \rho\sum_{j\neq i}C_{uj}\Big(\sum_{v\neq u}C_{vj}C_{vi}\,\varepsilon_{vj}\varepsilon_{vi}+C_{uj}C_{ui}\,\varepsilon_{uj}\varepsilon_{ui}\Big).
\]
For slates of $k$ items drawn uniformly without replacement, $\rho\le1/k$. For
DR edges the bias is of second order in the imputation error; for IPS edges
($\varepsilon=Y$) it is of first order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Repeated-walk bias of uncorrected propagation]\label{thm:lower}
Let the propensities be correct and let $(u,i)$ be a candidate with $O_{ui}=0$.
Relative to the conditional target of Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}, the
uncorrected DR operator $\tW\tW^\top\tW$ has conditional bias
\[
C_{ui}\hat Y_{ui}\big(R_u^{(i)}+K_i\big)-C_{ui}^3\hat Y_{ui}(1-\hat Y_{ui}^2),\quad
K_i=\sum_{v\neq u}C_{vi}^2V_{vi},\quad R_u^{(i)}=\sum_{j\neq i}C_{uj}^2V_{uj},
\]
where $V_e=\Var(W_e)=\frac{1-p_e}{p_e}\varepsilon_e^2$. Moreover:
(a) $K_i\ge(1/\max_vp_{vi}-1)\sum_{v\neq u}C_{vi}^2\varepsilon_{vi}^2$, which is unbounded
as the item's propensities vanish;
(b) with clipped propensities $\bar p=\max(p,\tau)$, $\tau\le1/2$, the excess
$C_e^2(\E[W_e^2]-\E[W_e])$ of a doubly traversed edge is at most
$C_e^2(1/\tau-1)\varepsilon_e^2$, with equality at $p_e=\tau$; the worst-case
repeated-walk bias is therefore $\Theta(1/\tau)$;
(c) for IPS edges the conditional bias vanishes at three hops.
DRUP has zero bias relative to the same target.
\end{theorem}
The within-user ranking is affected through $C_{ui}\hat Y_{ui}K_i$, which
up-weights items whose other raters were rarely exposed; $R_u^{(i)}$ depends on
$i$ only through one term.

\begin{theorem}[Variance, clipping and concentration]\label{thm:var}
Let $\Lambda_e(u,i)$ be the $C$-weighted mass of the walks from $u$ to $i$ through
edge $e$, and $B=\max(1,1/\tau)$. Then
(a) $\Var(W_e)\le\varepsilon_e^2/\tau$;
(b) $\Var(\hat T_{ui})\le\frac{(1+1/\tau)^2}{\tau}\sum_e\varepsilon_e^2\Lambda_e(u,i)^2$;
(c) if $\hat p=p$, so that clipping is the only source of bias,
$|\E\hat T_{ui}-T^*_{ui}|\le3\bar\delta(1+\bar\delta)^2\sum_{j,v}C_{uj}C_{vj}C_{vi}$ with
$\bar\delta=\max_e(1-p_e/\tau)_+\varepsilon_e$;
(d) $\Pr(|\hat T_{ui}-\E\hat T_{ui}|\ge t)\le2\exp(-2t^2/\sum_ec_e^2)$ with
$c_e=\varepsilon_eB^2\Lambda_e(u,i)/\tau$.
\end{theorem}

The variance scales with the imputation error $\varepsilon^2$, not with $Y^2$ as
for IPS, and $\tau$ trades variance against a clipping bias that vanishes when
$\hat Y$ is accurate. The constants are worst-case and loose; Section~\ref{sec:mc}
compares bound (b) with the Monte-Carlo variance.

\begin{definition}[Exposure elasticity]
For an intervention $do(p_{\cdot i}\leftarrow\pi p_{\cdot i})$ on item $i$'s
exposure mechanism, $\eta_i=\partial\log\E[s_{ui}]/\partial\log\pi$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exposure invariance]\label{thm:fair}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:unconf}--\ref{as:fixed} and $\pi p\ge\tau$, DRUP has
$\eta_i=0$, also conditionally on $O_{ui}=0$: items with equal potential
outcomes receive equal expected scores whatever their exposure history.
Logged-graph propagation has $\eta_i\ge1$ (popularity amplification).
Uncorrected DR propagation has $\eta_i<0$ whenever $\hat Y_{ui}K_i>0$
(over-correction).
\end{theorem}

Exposure invariance is a statement about expected scores. It is not a
distributive fairness criterion, and it is conditional: when many propensities
lie below the clip, $\pi p\ge\tau$ fails and invariance is lost
(Section~\ref{sec:fair}).

\subsection{Structural properties of linear propagation}\label{sec:structth}

The remaining results use only that, given the nuisances, $s$ is a fixed
linear function of the edge estimates. They hold for DRUP and equally for
the uncorrected operators and for linear LightGCN, and they describe the
recommender with its nuisances held fixed.

\begin{proposition}[Affine in the user's own data]\label{prop:affine}
Fix $u$ and a candidate $i$ with $O_{ui}=0$, and let $G^{(-u)}$ be $G$ without
$u$'s own contribution. With frozen nuisances,
$s_{ui}=\mathrm{const}+\sum_{j\neq i}\big(c_{uj}G^{(-u)}_{ji}+c_{ui}w_{ui}c_{uj}^2\big)w_{uj}$.
Hence un-logging interaction $j$, i.e., replacing $w_{uj}$ by its unlogged
value $\hat Y_{uj}$, changes $s_{ui}$ by exactly $\phi_j=a_j(w_{uj}-\hat Y_{uj})$,
removal effects add up over any subset, and completeness holds exactly.
\end{proposition}
Because the removal game is additive, the $\phi_j$ are also its Shapley values;
this is a consequence of linearity, and the attribution is relative to the
stated baseline~\citep{kumar2020shapley}.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal minimal counterfactual explanation]\label{thm:cf}
For candidates $i,k$ with $s_{ui}>s_{uk}$, sorting $g_j=\phi^{(i)}_j-\phi^{(k)}_j$ in
decreasing order and removing interactions until the cumulative sum exceeds the
margin yields a minimum-cardinality set whose removal makes $k$ outrank $i$.
If the positive $g_j$ do not sum above the margin, no such set exists. The
cost is $O(n+d_u\log d_u)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Certificates against injected profiles, frozen nuisances]\label{thm:robust}
With frozen nuisances, one injected user $v$ changes $u$'s three-hop score of item
$k$ by $\Delta_k=x_k(D+a_kc_{vk})-a_kx_k^2$, where $a=\tilde w_u$, $x=c_v\odot w_v$
and $D=\langle a,x\rangle$. If $v$ logs at most $L$ interactions with arbitrary
items, labels and propensities $\ge\tau$, then $D$ and $x_k$ lie in closed-form
intervals. $\Delta_k$ is linear in $D$ and quadratic in $x_k$, so its extremes
$[\underline\Delta_k,\overline\Delta_k]$ over this box are exact. Item $t$
provably stays out of $u$'s top-$K$ against any $F$ such users if
$s_{ut}+F\overline\Delta_t$ is below the $(K{+}1)$-th largest value of
$s_{uk}+F\underline\Delta_k$ over the other candidates. With unclipped IPS, the
influence of a single interaction is unbounded.
\end{theorem}
The same argument certifies the uncorrected operators (with
$\Delta_k=x_kD$). Degrees taken from $\hat Y$ make the fake user's weight $c_v$
known; degrees taken from $W$ would be attacker-controlled.

\begin{theorem}[Joint differential privacy with public nuisances]\label{thm:dp}
Let the population-level parts of the nuisances (the propensity model, the
item parameters of $\hat Y$, the item degrees in $C$ and the radius $R$) be
computed from public data, and every user-level part from the user's own row.
Scale each private row so that $\|\tilde w_v\|_2\le R$ and
$\|c_v^{\odot2}\odot w_v\|_2\le R^2$. Releasing $G$, computed over the private
rows, plus symmetric Gaussian noise of scale
$\sigma=\sigma_{\mathrm{aG}}(\epsilon,\delta;2R^2)$, the analytic Gaussian
calibration~\citep{balle2018analytic}, is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP under adding or
removing one user, for every $\epsilon>0$. Scoring by~\eqref{eq:local} uses only
the release and the user's own row, so the recommender is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-jointly
DP~\citep{hsu2014matchings}. Post-processing such as low-rank denoising is free.
\end{theorem}
Row scaling changes the estimate for users whose rows exceed $R$, so
Theorems~\ref{thm:unbiased} and~\ref{thm:fair} hold for the private variant only
up to this shrinkage.

\begin{theorem}[Exposure-capped allocation]\label{thm:alloc}
Let $N$ users each receive $K$ of their candidates and let item $i$ be
recommended to at most $\mathrm{cap}_i$ users. Let $\hat x$ maximise $\langle x,s\rangle$
over such allocations. The constraint matrix is totally unimodular, so the
linear relaxation has an integral optimum, and min-cost flow on the scores
rounded to a grid of $L$ levels finds $\hat x$ exactly up to the rounding gap
$\Gamma\le NK(\max s-\min s)/L$. All caps hold, at least $NK/\max_i\mathrm{cap}_i$
items are recommended, and for the full-exposure utility $U(x)=\langle x,F^*\rangle$,
\[
U(\hat x)\ \ge\ \max_{x}U(x)-\Gamma-2\max_x|\langle x,s-F^*\rangle| .
\]
If $\E s=F^*$ (DRUP with correct propensities), Theorem~\ref{thm:var}(d) and a
union bound give $\max_x|\langle x,s-F^*\rangle|\le NK\,t_\delta$ with probability
$1-\delta$, where $t_\delta=\max_{u,i}\big(\tfrac12\sum_ec_e(u,i)^2\ln(2|\mathcal C|/\delta)\big)^{1/2}$
and $\mathcal C$ is the candidate set. For logged-graph propagation,
$\E s=F^*(P\odot Y)$, and the last term does not vanish.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data}
\emph{Coat}~\citep{schnabel2016} has 290 users, 300 items and 6{,}960 self-selected
(MNAR) ratings, exactly 24 per user. The test data are 16 ratings per user on
uniformly random items (MAR); we rank only the items the user did not rate,
which leaves 14.7 candidates per user on average, and 225 users have at least
one positive candidate. The dataset ships propensities, which we use.
\emph{Yahoo!\,R3}~\citep{marlin2009collaborative} has 311{,}704 self-selected ratings of
15{,}400 users on 1{,}000 songs (2.0\% dense) and 54{,}000 ratings of 10 random
songs for 5{,}400 of the users, in the version distributed
with~\citet{chen2021autodebias}. A random 5\% of the MAR users is held out and used
only to fit an outcome-dependent Naive-Bayes propensity~\citep{schnabel2016}; in a
preliminary comparison on validation data it did not improve the propagation
operators, and all reported results use the logistic exposure model. The remaining 5{,}130 test users have 10 candidates each, and 2{,}315 of
them have a positive. \emph{KuaiRec}~\citep{gao2022kuairec} provides the big matrix
(7{,}176 users, 10{,}728 items, 10.3M logged views, 13.4\% dense) as the MNAR log and
a fully observed small matrix (1{,}411 users, 3{,}327 items) as the test set. We rank
all small-matrix items that the user never viewed in the log (3{,}314 candidates
per user, 154 of them positive on average). Positives are ratings $\ge4$ (Coat,
Yahoo!\,R3) and a watch ratio $\ge2$ (KuaiRec). A random ranking reaches nDCG@5
of 0.306 on Coat and 0.352 on Yahoo!\,R3 and nDCG@20 of 0.046 on KuaiRec. The
candidate sets of Coat and Yahoo!\,R3 are small, so their metric differences
are compressed; KuaiRec ranks thousands of candidates.

\paragraph{Protocol}
The unbiased data are split into a validation part (30\% of each user's
candidates on Coat, 30\% of the test users otherwise) and a test part, over 10
(Coat) or 5 random splits. Every method selects its configuration on the
validation part of each split, and trained models also select their epoch
there. All fitted nuisances are cross-fitted over ten folds of pairs
(Remark~\ref{rem:xfit}). We report the mean and standard deviation over splits
of the test metric. Because paired tests over re-sampled splits are anti-conservative when the
splits overlap~\citep{nadeau2003}, differences are tested at the level
of users, with a Holm correction over all comparisons of a dataset
(\ref{app:details}). Progress claims in top-$N$ recommendation have
repeatedly failed against well-tuned simple
baselines~\citep{dacrema2019progress,rendle2019baselines,anelli2023myth}; we therefore
tune every method on the same validation data and report how many
configurations each one searched.

\paragraph{Methods}
We compare 23 methods. \emph{Non-personalised}: popularity and the imputation
$\hat Y$ alone. \emph{Trained with a pointwise loss}: MF, IPS-MF~\citep{schnabel2016},
DR-MF~\citep{wang2019drjl}, LightGCN and DR-LightGCN (LightGCN with the DR loss).
\emph{Trained with a pairwise (BPR) loss}: MF, PDA~\citep{zhang2021pda},
LightGCN~\citep{he2020lightgcn}, r-AdjNorm~\citep{zhao2022radjnorm} and
NAVIP~\citep{kim2022navip}. \emph{Training-free on the logged graph}: linear LightGCN
(the one- plus three-hop form of Section~4 on $O\odot Y$; with the searched
normalisation exponent it is a linear r-AdjNorm), EASE~\citep{steck2019ease} and
GF-CF~\citep{shen2021gfcf}. \emph{Training-free on a debiased graph}: IPS adjacency
(NAVIP-style), IPS adjacency with the walk correction, EASE and GF-CF on the DR
graph $W$, DR adjacency, DRUP, and the five-hop versions of DR adjacency and
DRUP. Grids, the number of configurations per method, and training details are
in \ref{app:details}. The training-free methods search between 3 and
6{,}300 configurations and the trained ones between 4 and 36; each trained
configuration is trained once and evaluated after every epoch on every split,
so that it is also tuned per split.

\paragraph{The walk correction does not change accuracy} On every dataset DRUP
and the uncorrected DR adjacency with the same grid are statistically
indistinguishable (Coat $+0.0014$, KuaiRec $-0.0001$ with $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.45$), and

\paragraph{Propagation over the DR graph is what helps on KuaiRec} On KuaiRec,
whose test matrix is fully observed and whose candidate sets have 3{,}314 items,
training-free propagation over the DR graph reaches nDCG@20 of 0.638 and is
significantly better than every trained model (the best, LightGCN with a

DR graph and not from a particular filter. The selected DR configurations use
the low-rank imputation on all five splits.

\paragraph{Yahoo!\,R3 and Coat} On Yahoo!\,R3 the log is 2\% dense and the
imputation alone is below popularity (0.516 against 0.533), so almost every edge
of a DR graph would be imputed from a poor model; validation therefore selects
IPS edges ($\lambda=0$) with cross-fitted $W$-degrees on every split. With these
settings DRUP (0.657) is on par with linear propagation over the logged graph
(0.659) and GF-CF (0.657), and it is significantly behind the three graph models
trained with BPR: r-AdjNorm (0.671; $-0.015$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.004$), NAVIP and LightGCN
(0.669; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}\le0.04$). With degrees taken from the imputation instead, DRUP
drops to 0.62--0.63 on this dataset, so the degree source matters here. On Coat
(290 users, 15 candidates per user) no difference between DRUP and a trained or
training-free baseline survives the Holm correction except GF-CF on the DR
graph, which is better by 0.013 ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.03$); the best models are again the BPR
graph models (r-AdjNorm 0.575, LightGCN 0.570), but their advantage over DRUP is
not significant at the user level.

\paragraph{Baselines} Two changes of the baselines matter. First, pairwise
training is decisive on Yahoo!\,R3: pointwise MF variants stay near 0.51, below
popularity, whereas BPR-MF reaches 0.646 and BPR-trained LightGCN 0.669. On
KuaiRec the ordering reverses: pointwise LightGCN (0.628) and MF (0.623) beat
their BPR versions (0.609, 0.591). Second, per-split selection of configuration
and epoch benefits the trained models on the small Coat data, where re-training
the split-0 selection with other seeds lowers LightGCN from 0.581 to
$0.547\pm0.018$; on Yahoo!\,R3 and KuaiRec the seed standard deviation is below 0.005.
Across the three datasets, the best trained model is thus a BPR graph model on
Coat and Yahoo!\,R3 and a pointwise one on KuaiRec, while DR propagation without
training is the most accurate method on the largest and most complete test set.

%======================================================================
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{What the correction is for}
On the three benchmarks the walk correction does not change top-$K$
accuracy, and in simulation the uncorrected DR operator can even agree
slightly better with the full-exposure ranking, because its bias adds weight
to the imputation (Section~\ref{sec:mc}). The correction matters wherever the
\emph{value} of a propagated score is used rather than only its order within
one user's list: when scores are compared across users, as in the
exposure-capped allocation; when they are aggregated into an estimate of
full-exposure utility; and when an auditor wants to verify that expected
scores do not depend on exposure. In these uses the uncorrected estimate is
biased by a factor of 2--80 in our simulations, and the bias grows with the
number of hops and with small propensities. A practitioner who only needs
within-user rankings from a dense log loses nothing by using the uncorrected
DR adjacency; one who needs calibrated, auditable scores, or propagates over
five hops or more, should use the corrected operator. In both cases, most of
the accuracy comes from training-free propagation over a doubly robust graph.

\paragraph{Stakeholders}
For \emph{item providers}, exposure invariance means that an item is not
penalised in expectation for having been shown rarely in the past. It does not
mean equal exposure: when quality is concentrated, DRUP's lists can be more
concentrated than those of the logged graph (Table~\ref{tab:fair}), and the
scores of rarely exposed items have a larger variance
(Theorem~\ref{thm:var}(a)), so their realised rankings are noisier. Providers
who need exposure guarantees need an explicit mechanism such as the
exposure-capped allocation. For \emph{users}, the attributions show which of
their own interactions drive a recommendation, or certify that none can
overturn it; the population-level part of the score, which dominates on Coat,
remains unexplained, and explaining it through $G$ would conflict with the
privacy guarantee. For the \emph{platform}, DRUP is cheap and training-free
but needs propensities, and the clip $\tau$ and the control-variate weight
$\lambda$ trade exposure invariance against accuracy. This trade-off is a policy
choice rather than a tuning detail. One option is to select the most accurate
configuration subject to a bound on the elasticity measured in a thinning
audit such as ours. For \emph{auditors and regulators}, DRUP produces concrete
artefacts: a single item operator, a thinning audit with a known answer, and
certificates. Under the EU Digital Services Act~\citep{dsa2022}, platforms must
explain the main parameters of their recommender systems (Art.~27), assess and
mitigate systemic risks (Arts.~34--35), undergo independent audits (Art.~37)
and give vetted researchers access to data (Art.~40). Our artefacts fit the
last three, but the audit of exposure invariance needs the platform's
propensities, which an external auditor usually does not have.

\paragraph{Limitations}
The analysis is static. It assumes a single logging policy and fixed potential
outcomes, whereas recommenders are retrained on the feedback they
generate~\citep{chaney2018confounding,hazrati2022evolution} and can change the
preferences they predict~\citep{perdomo2020performative}; extending the
guarantees to adaptive and slate logging~\citep{swaminathan2017slate} is open.
Proposition~\ref{prop:dep} covers dependence within a user's history, not
dependence across users. The idempotent correction relies on binary outcomes;
graded relevance would need separate unbiased estimates of the powers of $Y$.
Items that were never exposed fall outside $\pi p\ge\tau$ and are scored by the
imputation only. The nuisances are cross-fitted rather than fitted on an
independent log, the joint-DP guarantee requires public nuisances, and on the
small Coat data the public-nuisance variant loses much of its accuracy. The
certificates are vacuous for catalogues of our KuaiRec size, and the variance
and concentration bounds are loose by several orders of magnitude
(Section~\ref{sec:mc}). The target $F^*$ is a linear LightGCN score of the
full-exposure world, a normative choice that other stakeholders may not
share. Like every method in our comparison, DRUP selects its hyper-parameters
on a small unbiased sample; without such a sample, selection would need an
off-policy estimate of the validation metric. Finally, our largest catalogue
has about $10^4$ items.

%======================================================================
\section{Implications}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{For research}
Propagation-level debiasing needs to consider the propagation operator itself,
not only the edges it propagates: multiplying unbiased edge estimates does not
give an unbiased multi-hop score. The repeated-walk bias is exactly
removable, and the tools that remove it (idempotent reduction and Möbius
inversion over walk-index coincidences) apply to any polynomial graph filter
built from inverse-propensity or DR estimates. Our evaluation also shows that
training-free propagation over a DR graph is a strong baseline against which
trained debiased graph recommenders should be compared, and that claims of
exposure invariance can be tested on real data by thinning exposures with a
known factor.

\paragraph{For practice}
DRUP runs at the cost of one item Gram matrix and needs no gradient training,
so it can serve as an auditable baseline or as a candidate scorer. The
choice between the corrected and the uncorrected operator depends on whether
score values or only within-user orders matter. On sparse logs, exact exposure
invariance requires small clips and costs accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:tau}). The
structural properties (exact attributions, certificates, a private item
operator and capped allocation) come with any linear propagation operator and
hold for the model with its nuisances held fixed; platforms should report them
together with that scope.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%======================================================================

Inverse-propensity and doubly robust graph propagation are biased beyond one
hop because walks that repeat an edge multiply its estimate with itself. We
gave the exact bias, whose worst case grows as the inverse of the propensity
clip, and removed it with an idempotent correction that is exact for any odd
number of hops and costs no more than the uncorrected operator. With fixed
nuisances the resulting estimator, DRUP, is edge-wise doubly robust for
propagation over the full-exposure graph, robust to within-user exposure
dependence to second order in the imputation error, and exposure-invariant in
expectation. On three benchmarks with unbiased test data, training-free DR
propagation is competitive with tuned trained and closed-form baselines, while
the correction itself leaves top-$K$ accuracy unchanged. Future work includes
private nuisance models, certificates that remain informative for large
catalogues, guarantees under adaptive logging, and combining the correction with
learned propagation weights.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

Throughout, expectations are conditional on $Y$, and ``walk'' means a
three-hop walk $u\!-\!j\!-\!v\!-\!i$ unless stated otherwise. Its edges are
$(u,j)$, $(v,j)$ and $(v,i)$.

\paragraph{Equations~\eqref{eq:drup} and~\eqref{eq:local}.}
Split the plug-in sum $(\tW\tW^\top\tW)_{ui}=\sum_{j,v}\tW_{uj}\tW_{vj}\tW_{vi}$ by walk
type (Figure~\ref{fig:walks}): (a) $v\ne u$, $j\ne i$; (b) $v=u$, $j\ne i$, giving
$\sum_{j\ne i}\tW_{uj}^2\tW_{ui}$; (c) $v\ne u$, $j=i$, giving
$\sum_{v\ne u}\tW_{ui}\tW_{vi}^2$; (d) $v=u$, $j=i$, giving $\tW_{ui}^3$. The
corrected estimator keeps (a) and replaces (b)--(d) by
$\sum_{j\ne i}C_{uj}^2W_{uj}\tW_{ui}$, $\sum_{v\ne u}\tW_{ui}C_{vi}^2W_{vi}$ and
$C_{ui}^3W_{ui}$. The difference between plug-in and corrected sums is
$\tW_{ui}\sum_{j\ne i}\Delta_{uj}+\tW_{ui}\sum_{v\ne u}\Delta_{vi}+(\tW_{ui}^3-C_{ui}^3W_{ui})
=\tW_{ui}(r_u-\Delta_{ui})+\tW_{ui}(\kappa_i-\Delta_{ui})+(\tW_{ui}^3-C_{ui}^3W_{ui})$,
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:drup}. For Eq.~\eqref{eq:local},
$(\tilde w_uG)_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\tW_{uj}(\tW^\top\tW)_{ji}+\tW_{ui}\sum_vC_{vi}^2W_{vi}
=(\tW\tW^\top\tW)_{ui}-\tW_{ui}\kappa_i+\tW_{ui}\sum_vC_{vi}^2W_{vi}$, and substituting
into Eq.~\eqref{eq:drup} cancels the $\kappa_i$ terms. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased}.}
By the decomposition above, the corrected estimator is a sum over walks of
$\prod_{e}C_e^{k_e}W_e$, the product running over the \emph{distinct} edges of the
walk with multiplicities $k_e$. Under Assumptions~\ref{as:unconf}--\ref{as:fixed}
the $W_e$ of distinct edges are independent, so the expectation factorises,
with $\E W_e=\hat Y_e+p_e(Y_e-\hat Y_e)/\bar p_e=Y_e+\delta_e$. The target walk
monomial is $\prod_eC_e^{k_e}Y_e^{k_e}=\prod_eC_e^{k_e}Y_e$ because
$Y_e\in\{0,1\}$. The one-hop term is unbiased edge by edge. \qed

\paragraph{Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}.}
Given $O_{ui}=0$, $W_{ui}=\hat Y_{ui}$ is deterministic and the other edge
estimates keep their distribution (Assumption~\ref{as:unconf}). The edge $(u,i)$
belongs to the one-hop term and to exactly the walks of types (b), (c) and (d).
In each of them it contributes $C_{ui}^{k}\hat Y_{ui}$ instead of $C_{ui}^kY_{ui}$,
and every other distinct edge contributes its mean $Y_e$. After the idempotent
reduction, $F^*_{ui}$ is affine in $Y_{ui}$, so
$\E[s_{ui}\mid O_{ui}=0]=F^*_{ui}|_{Y_{ui}\leftarrow\hat Y_{ui}}=F^*_{ui}-(Y_{ui}-\hat Y_{ui})\,\partial F^*_{ui}/\partial Y_{ui}$.
The derivative collects $aC_{ui}$ from the one-hop term,
$bC_{ui}\sum_{j\ne i}C_{uj}^2Y_{uj}$ from type (b),
$bC_{ui}\sum_{v\ne u}C_{vi}^2Y_{vi}$ from type (c) and $bC_{ui}^3$ from type (d).
For IPS edges, $W_{ui}=0$ given $O_{ui}=0$. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:khop}.}
Fix $u$ and $i$. For an assignment $\varphi$ of values to the free variables, let
$\pi(\varphi)$ be its equality pattern. Two walk edges $(U_s,J_t)$ and
$(U_{s'},J_{t'})$ coincide if and only if $U_s,U_{s'}$ share a user block and
$J_t,J_{t'}$ share an item block, so the distinct edges and their
multiplicities $k_e$ depend on $\pi(\varphi)$ only. For a pattern $\pi$, let
$F_\pi(\varphi)=\prod_\epsilon X^{(k_\epsilon)}_{\varphi(\epsilon)}$, the product over the
distinct edges $\epsilon$ of $\pi$, with $X^{(k)}=C^{\odot k}\odot W$ (corrected) or
$X^{(k)}=\tW^{\odot k}$ (plug-in). The plug-in $K$-hop product equals
$\sum_\pi S^{\mathrm{plug}}_\pi$ and the corrected estimator is
$\sum_\pi S^{\mathrm{corr}}_\pi$, where $S_\pi=\sum_{\varphi:\pi(\varphi)=\pi}F_\pi(\varphi)$. The two
coincide for patterns without a repeated edge, which gives the form of
$\hat T^{(K)}$ once each $S_\pi$ is written through unconstrained sums. For
$\sigma\ge\pi$ let $g_\pi(\sigma)=\sum_{\varphi:\pi(\varphi)\ge\sigma}F_\pi(\varphi)$: variables in the
same block of $\sigma$ are equal and values of different blocks are unrestricted,
so $g_\pi(\sigma)$ is a single tensor contraction over the blocks of $\sigma$ (with
the blocks of $U_0$ and $J_{r+1}$ fixed to $u$ and $i$). Grouping the terms of
$g_\pi(\sigma)$ by their exact pattern gives $g_\pi(\sigma)=\sum_{\tau\ge\sigma}f_\pi(\tau)$ with
$f_\pi(\tau)=\sum_{\varphi:\pi(\varphi)=\tau}F_\pi(\varphi)$. Möbius inversion on the interval
$[\pi,\hat1]$ of the product of the two partition lattices~\citep{rota1964} yields
$S_\pi=f_\pi(\pi)=\sum_{\sigma\ge\pi}\mu(\pi,\sigma)\,g_\pi(\sigma)$, where
$\mu(\pi,\sigma)=\prod_{B}(-1)^{b_B-1}(b_B-1)!$ over the blocks $B$ of $\sigma$ in both
lattices and $b_B$ is the number of blocks of $\pi$ merged into $B$. Finally, for
every $\varphi$ with $\pi(\varphi)=\pi$ the distinct edges are independent, so
$\E F^{\mathrm{corr}}_\pi(\varphi)=\prod_\epsilon C_{\varphi(\epsilon)}^{k_\epsilon}Y_{\varphi(\epsilon)}$, which is the
corresponding monomial of $(\tY\tY^\top)^r\tY$ since $Y$ is binary. The number of
contraction terms is the number of pairs $(\pi,\sigma)$ with $\sigma\ge\pi$ and $\pi$
containing a repeated edge, which is 5, 99 and 2{,}790 for $K=3,5,7$. \qed

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:dep}.}
With correct propensities $\E W_e=Y_e$, and for distinct $e,f$ in the same row
$\mathrm{Cov}(W_e,W_f)=(Y_e-\hat Y_e)(Y_f-\hat Y_f)\,\mathrm{Cov}(O_e,O_f)/(p_ep_f)$, so
$|\mathrm{Cov}(W_e,W_f)|\le\rho\,\varepsilon_e\varepsilon_f$. In a type (a) walk the edge $(u,j)$
lies in row $u$ and the edges $(v,j),(v,i)$ in row $v\ne u$; rows are independent,
so $\E[W_{uj}W_{vj}W_{vi}]=Y_{uj}(Y_{vj}Y_{vi}+\mathrm{Cov}(W_{vj},W_{vi}))$ and the bias of
the walk is at most $C_{uj}C_{vj}C_{vi}\rho\,\varepsilon_{vj}\varepsilon_{vi}$. A corrected type (b)
walk contributes $C_{uj}^2C_{ui}W_{uj}W_{ui}$ with two edges in row $u$, with bias
at most $C_{uj}^2C_{ui}\rho\,\varepsilon_{uj}\varepsilon_{ui}$. Type (c) walks have their two
distinct edges in different rows and type (d) walks a single edge, so they are
unbiased. Summing gives the bound. For $k$ of $n$ items drawn uniformly,
$\Pr(O_e=O_f=1)=k(k-1)/(n(n-1))$, so
$|\mathrm{Cov}(O_e,O_f)|/(p_ep_f)=(n-k)/(k(n-1))\le1/k$. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}.}
By the decomposition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:drup}, the plug-in minus the corrected
operator is $\tW_{ui}\sum_{j\ne i}\Delta_{uj}+\tW_{ui}\sum_{v\ne u}\Delta_{vi}+(\tW_{ui}^3-C_{ui}^3W_{ui})$.
Given $O_{ui}=0$, $\tW_{ui}=C_{ui}\hat Y_{ui}$ is deterministic and the other
$\Delta_e$ keep their distribution. With correct propensities and binary $Y$,
$\E\Delta_e=C_e^2(\E W_e^2-\E W_e)=C_e^2(\Var W_e+Y_e^2-Y_e)=C_e^2V_e$ with
$V_e=\frac{1-p_e}{p_e}\varepsilon_e^2$, which gives the stated bias, the last term being
$C_{ui}^3(\hat Y_{ui}^3-\hat Y_{ui})$. Part (a) uses $(1-p)/p\ge1/\max_vp_{vi}-1$. For
(b), with $\mu_e=\E W_e\in[0,1]$, $\E W_e^2-\E W_e=\Var W_e-\mu_e(1-\mu_e)\le\Var W_e
=p_e(1-p_e)\varepsilon_e^2/\bar p_e^2$. If $p_e\ge\tau$ this is
$\frac{1-p_e}{p_e}\varepsilon_e^2\le(1/\tau-1)\varepsilon_e^2$; if $p_e<\tau\le1/2$ it is
$p_e(1-p_e)\varepsilon_e^2/\tau^2<\tau(1-\tau)\varepsilon_e^2/\tau^2$ because $p(1-p)$ increases on
$[0,1/2]$. At $p_e=\tau$, $\mu_e=Y_e$ and the bound is attained; setting all
$p_{vi}=\tau$ gives a bias $C_{ui}\hat Y_{ui}(1/\tau-1)\sum_{v\ne u}C_{vi}^2\varepsilon_{vi}^2$, so the
worst case is $\Theta(1/\tau)$. For (c), IPS gives $\tW_{ui}=0$ and $C_{ui}^3W_{ui}=0$
given $O_{ui}=0$. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:var}.}
(a) $\Var(W_e)=p_e(1-p_e)\varepsilon_e^2/\bar p_e^2$; if $p_e\ge\tau$ it is at most
$(1/\tau-1)\varepsilon_e^2$, otherwise at most $p_e\varepsilon_e^2/\tau^2<\varepsilon_e^2/\tau$.
(b) $\hat T_{ui}$ is multilinear in the independent $W_e$ and hence affine in each
of them. The Efron--Stein inequality~\citep{efron1981jackknife} gives
$\Var(\hat T)\le\sum_e\E[(\partial_e\hat T)^2]\Var(W_e)$. The derivative $\partial_e\hat T$
is a sum over the walks through $e$ of $C$-weights times at most two other
independent $W_f$, and $\E W_f^2=\mu_f^2+\Var W_f\le1+1/\tau$. Minkowski's
inequality bounds $\|\partial_e\hat T\|_2\le(1+1/\tau)\Lambda_e$.
(c) By Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased}, the bias is at most
$\sum_{\text{walks}}|\kappa_{\text{walk}}|\,[\prod_f(Y_f+|\delta_f|)-\prod_fY_f]\le((1+\bar\delta)^3-1)\sum_{j,v}C_{uj}C_{vj}C_{vi}$,
and $(1+x)^3-1\le3x(1+x)^2$. With $\hat p=p$, $|\delta_e|=(1-p_e/\tau)_+\varepsilon_e$.
(d) Changing $O_e$ moves $W_e$ by at most $\varepsilon_e/\tau$. Every edge estimate
satisfies $|W_f|\le B$, so $|\partial_e\hat T|\le B^2\Lambda_e$, and McDiarmid's
inequality~\citep{mcdiarmid1989} applies. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:fair}.}
If $\pi p\ge\tau$ the clip does not bind after the intervention, so the
propensities remain correct. DRUP: by Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased}, $\E[s]=F^*$,
and by Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}, $\E[s\mid O_{ui}=0]$ is $F^*$ with $Y_{ui}$ replaced by
the fixed $\hat Y_{ui}$; neither depends on $\pi$. Logged graph: $\E[O_eY_e]=p_eY_e$,
and every walk that ends at $i$ contains $k\ge1$ edges incident to $i$, so its
expectation scales as $\pi^k$ and $\eta_i$ is an average of such $k$. Uncorrected
DR: by Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}, $\E[s_{ui}\mid O_{ui}=0]$ equals the DRUP target plus
$C_{ui}\hat Y_{ui}(R_u^{(i)}+K_i(\pi))$ minus a term free of $\pi$; $R_u^{(i)}$ involves
no edge of item $i$, and $K_i(\pi)=\sum_{v\ne u}C_{vi}^2\frac{1-\pi p_{vi}}{\pi p_{vi}}\varepsilon_{vi}^2$ is
strictly decreasing in $\pi$. \qed

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:affine} and Theorem~\ref{thm:cf}.}
With frozen nuisances, substitute
$G=G^{(-u)}+\tilde w_u^\top\tilde w_u-\diag(\tilde w_u^2)+\diag(c_u^2w_u)$
into~\eqref{eq:local}. The terms $\tilde w_{ui}\sum_j\tilde w_{uj}^2$ cancel against the
local correction, leaving $\tilde w_{ui}\sum_jc_{uj}^2w_{uj}$, and every remaining term
involving $w_{uj}$ ($j\neq i$) is linear. Un-logging $j$ replaces $w_{uj}$ by
$\hat Y_{uj}$, which changes $s_{ui}$ by $a_j(w_{uj}-\hat Y_{uj})$; the game is additive, so
its Shapley values are the individual effects. For Theorem~\ref{thm:cf}, the
margin after removing $S$ is $s_{ui}-s_{uk}-\sum_{j\in S}g_j$, so minimising $|S|$
under $\sum_Sg_j>$ margin selects the largest $g_j$. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:robust}.}
Adding row $v$ adds $\mathrm{contrib}_v$ to $G$ and leaves $u$'s local terms
unchanged, so $\Delta=\tilde w_u\,\mathrm{contrib}_v=(a\cdot x)x-a\odot x^2+a\odot c_v\odot x$.
A logged DR entry satisfies $w\in[\hat Y-\hat Y/\tau,\hat Y+(1-\hat Y)/\tau]$ for any label
and any $p\ge\tau$, and an unlogged one equals $\hat Y$. $D$ is maximised
(minimised) by assigning the $L$ slots to the largest positive (negative)
deviations. For fixed $x_k$ the effect is linear in $D$, so its extremes are at
the ends of the $D$ interval; for fixed $D$ it is quadratic in $x_k$, with
extremes at the ends or at the clamped vertex. Effects add over fake users. For
unclipped IPS, $|W_e|=1/p_e$ is unbounded. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:dp}.}
Because the population-level nuisances are public and every user-level part
depends only on the user's own row, adding or removing user $v$ changes $G$ by
$\mathrm{contrib}_v$ alone. With the row scaling,
$\|\mathrm{contrib}_v\|_F\le\|\tilde w_v\tilde w_v^\top-\diag(\tilde w_v^2)\|_F+\|c_v^2w_v\|_2\le R^2+R^2$,
so the $\ell_2$-sensitivity of the upper triangle is at most $2R^2$. The analytic
Gaussian mechanism is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP for every $\epsilon>0$ exactly when
$\Phi(\frac{\Delta}{2\sigma}-\frac{\epsilon\sigma}{\Delta})-e^\epsilon\Phi(-\frac{\Delta}{2\sigma}-\frac{\epsilon\sigma}{\Delta})\le\delta$
(Theorem~8 of~\citet{balle2018analytic}); we use the smallest such $\sigma$. The
classical calibration $\sigma=\Delta\sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}/\epsilon$~\citep{dwork2014algorithmic}
is only valid for $\epsilon<1$. Each user's output is a function of the release and
the user's own data, which gives joint DP by the billboard
lemma~\citep{hsu2014matchings}. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:alloc}.}
The allocation polytope is defined by the incidence matrix of the bipartite
user--candidate graph with degree bounds, which is totally unimodular, so the
linear relaxation has an integral optimal vertex and min-cost flow finds it.
Rounding changes each score by at most $h/2$ with $h=(\max s-\min s)/L$. If
$x^\star$ is optimal for $s$ and $\hat x$ for the rounded scores $\tilde s$, then
$\langle x^\star,s\rangle\le\langle x^\star,\tilde s\rangle+NKh/2\le\langle\hat x,\tilde s\rangle+NKh/2\le\langle\hat x,s\rangle+NKh$, so
$\Gamma\le NKh$. Each item is recommended at most $\max_i\mathrm{cap}_i$ times and $NK$
slots are filled, which gives the coverage bound. With $x_F$ optimal for $U$,
$U(\hat x)\ge\langle\hat x,s\rangle-|\langle\hat x,s-F^*\rangle|\ge\langle x_F,s\rangle-\Gamma-|\langle\hat x,s-F^*\rangle|
\ge U(x_F)-\Gamma-2\max_x|\langle x,s-F^*\rangle|$. If $\E s=F^*$, Theorem~\ref{thm:var}(d)
applied to every candidate pair and a union bound give
$|s_{ui}-F^*_{ui}|\le t_\delta$ for all candidate pairs with probability $1-\delta$, and an
allocation sums $NK$ such terms. \qed

\section{Experimental details}\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Training-free operators}
All operators share the score $s=s_1/c_1+\beta s_3/c_3$ (Section~4) with
$\beta\in\{0,0.03,0.1,0.3,1,3,10\}$ or the three-hop term alone; the five-hop
variants add $\gamma s_5/c_5$ with $\gamma\in\{0.03,0.1,0.3,1,3\}$. The grids are:
normalisation exponent $\alpha\in\{0.3,0.5,0.7\}$ (Coat), $\{0.5,0.7\}$
(Yahoo!\,R3) and $\{0.3,0.5\}$ (KuaiRec); clip
$\tau\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ (Coat), $\{0.1,0.2\}$ (Yahoo!\,R3) and
$\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ (KuaiRec); imputation ridge $\lambda\in\{1,5,20\}$ (Coat),
$\{20\}$ (Yahoo!\,R3) and $\{5,20\}$ (KuaiRec); additive or low-rank
(rank 32) imputation; control-variate weight $\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1\}$ on Yahoo!\,R3
and $1$ elsewhere; and degree source $\in\{\hat Y,\ \text{cross-fitted }W\}$ for
the debiased operators. The degree source is a searched hyper-parameter; both
options satisfy Remark~\ref{rem:xfit}, and only $\hat Y$-degrees make the
certificates of Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} informative. EASE uses
$\lambda\in\{10,100,1000\}$; GF-CF uses the ideal low-pass weight
$\in\{0,0.3,1\}$ with $64$ or $256$ singular vectors. On the DR graph, EASE and
GF-CF search the same nuisance grid as DR adjacency. The propensity model is
the one shipped with Coat and a logistic exposure model on user and item
exposure rates elsewhere; on Yahoo!\,R3 it was selected on validation data
against an outcome-dependent Naive-Bayes model. All nuisances are cross-fitted
over ten random folds of pairs.

\paragraph{Trained models}
Embeddings have 64 dimensions (Coat: 32 or 64) and LightGCN-type models two
layers. The grid is learning rate $\{10^{-2},3\cdot10^{-3},10^{-3}\}$ $\times$ weight
decay $\{10^{-5},10^{-4},10^{-3}\}$ on Coat and Yahoo!\,R3 and
$\{10^{-2},3\cdot10^{-3}\}\times\{10^{-5},10^{-4}\}$ on KuaiRec, plus
$\gamma\in\{0.1,0.2\}$ for PDA and $r\in\{0.3,0.7\}$ for r-AdjNorm. BPR models draw
one uniformly sampled negative per positive. Batch sizes are 256 (Coat), 1{,}024
(Yahoo!\,R3) and 32{,}768 (KuaiRec, with 2M sampled pairs per epoch for the
pointwise losses); training runs for at most 100, 60 and 30 epochs with
patience 5, 5 and 3 on the mean validation score. Because the training log is
the same for every split, each configuration is trained once and evaluated
after every epoch on the validation part of every split; each split then
selects its own configuration and epoch. The split-0 selection is re-trained
with three (KuaiRec: two) further seeds to measure seed variance.

selected from.

\paragraph{Evaluation and tests}
Coat splits each user's candidates (30\% validation), Yahoo!\,R3 and KuaiRec
split the test users (30\% validation), over 10, 5 and 5 random splits.
Users without a positive candidate are skipped. For a pair of methods, each
test user's metric difference is averaged over the splits in which the user
is in the test part, and we report the mean difference over users with a
95\% $t$-interval and the paired $t$-test, Holm-adjusted over all comparisons
of a dataset. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives the same conclusions. The
corrected resampled $t$-test over split means~\citep{nadeau2003} is far more
conservative with five or ten splits and is reported in the result files.

\paragraph{Hardware and code}
All experiments ran on four CPU cores with 16\,GB of memory, without a GPU.
Code, configurations and result files are available at the repository given
on the title page.
