\section{Structural properties of linear propagation}\label{sec:structth}

The results of this section use only that, given the nuisances, $s$ is a
fixed linear function of the edge estimates. They hold for DRUP and equally for
the uncorrected operators and for linear LightGCN, and they describe the
recommender with its nuisances held fixed.

\begin{proposition}[Affine in the user's own data]\label{prop:affine}
Fix $u$ and a candidate $i$ with $O_{ui}=0$, and let $G^{(-u)}$ be $G$ without
$u$'s own contribution. With frozen nuisances,
$s_{ui}=\mathrm{const}+\sum_{j\neq i}\omega_jw_{uj}$ with
$\omega_j=b\big(c_{uj}G^{(-u)}_{ji}+c_{ui}w_{ui}c_{uj}^2\big)$.
Hence un-logging interaction $j$, i.e., replacing $w_{uj}$ by its unlogged
value $\hat Y_{uj}$, changes $s_{ui}$ by exactly $\phi_j=\omega_j(w_{uj}-\hat Y_{uj})$,
removal effects add up over any subset, and completeness holds exactly.
\end{proposition}
Because the removal game is additive, the $\phi_j$ are also its Shapley values;
this is a consequence of linearity, and the attribution is relative to the
stated baseline~\citep{kumar2020shapley}.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal minimal counterfactual explanation]\label{thm:cf}
For candidates $i,k$ with $s_{ui}>s_{uk}$, sorting $g_j=\phi^{(i)}_j-\phi^{(k)}_j$ in
decreasing order and removing interactions until the cumulative sum exceeds the
margin yields a minimum-cardinality set whose removal makes $k$ outrank $i$.
If the positive $g_j$ do not sum above the margin, no such set exists. The
cost is $O(n+d_u\log d_u)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Certificates against injected profiles, frozen nuisances]\label{thm:robust}
With frozen nuisances, one injected user $v$ changes $u$'s three-hop score of item
$k$ by $\Delta_k=x_k(D+a_kc_{vk})-a_kx_k^2$, where $a=\tilde w_u$, $x=c_v\odot w_v$
and $D=\langle a,x\rangle$. If $v$ logs at most $L$ interactions with arbitrary
items, labels and propensities $\ge\tau$, then $D$ and $x_k$ lie in closed-form
intervals. $\Delta_k$ is linear in $D$ and quadratic in $x_k$, so its extremes
$[\underline\Delta_k,\overline\Delta_k]$ over the box formed by the two intervals
are attained at its edges or at the vertex of the quadratic. The box contains
every feasible $(D,x_k)$ but ignores that both are determined by the same $L$
interactions, so $[\underline\Delta_k,\overline\Delta_k]$ contains the feasible
range of $\Delta_k$ and is conservative rather than exact. Item $t$
provably stays out of $u$'s top-$K$ against any $F$ such users if
$s_{ut}+F\overline\Delta_t$ is below the $(K{+}1)$-th largest value of
$s_{uk}+F\underline\Delta_k$ over the other candidates. With unclipped IPS, the
influence of a single interaction is unbounded.
\end{theorem}
The same argument certifies the uncorrected operators (with
$\Delta_k=x_kD$). Degrees taken from $\hat Y$ make the fake user's weight $c_v$
known; degrees taken from $W$ would be attacker-controlled.

\begin{theorem}[Joint differential privacy with public nuisances]\label{thm:dp}
Let the population-level parts of the nuisances (the propensity model, the
item parameters of $\hat Y$, the item degrees in $C$ and the radius $R$), the
hyper-parameters and the constants $a,b$ of the score be fixed a priori or
computed from public data and from the release, and every user-level part
from the user's own row.
Scale each private row so that $\|\tilde w_v\|_2\le R$ and
$\|c_v^{\odot2}\odot w_v\|_2\le R^2$. Releasing $G$, computed over the private
rows, plus symmetric Gaussian noise of scale
$\sigma=\sigma_{\mathrm{aG}}(\epsilon,\delta;2R^2)$, the analytic Gaussian
calibration~\citep{balle2018analytic}, is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP under adding or
removing one user, for every $\epsilon>0$. Scoring by~\eqref{eq:local} uses only
the release and the user's own row, so the recommender is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-jointly
DP~\citep{hsu2014matchings}. Post-processing such as low-rank denoising is free.
\end{theorem}
Row scaling changes the estimate for users whose rows exceed $R$, so
Theorems~\ref{thm:unbiased} and~\ref{thm:fair} hold for the private variant only
up to this shrinkage.

\begin{theorem}[Exposure-capped allocation]\label{thm:alloc}
Let $N$ users each receive $K$ of their candidates and let item $i$ be
recommended to at most $\mathrm{cap}_i$ users. Let $\hat x$ maximise $\langle x,s\rangle$
over such allocations. The constraint matrix is totally unimodular, so the
linear relaxation has an integral optimum, and min-cost flow on the scores
rounded to a grid of $L$ levels finds $\hat x$ exactly up to the rounding gap
$\Gamma\le NK(\max s-\min s)/L$. All caps hold, at least $NK/\max_i\mathrm{cap}_i$
items are recommended, and for the full-exposure utility $U(x)=\langle x,F^*\rangle$,
\[
U(\hat x)\ \ge\ \max_{x}U(x)-\Gamma-2\max_x|\langle x,s-F^*\rangle| .
\]
If $\E s=F^*$ (DRUP under Assumption~\ref{as:fixed} with $\xi\equiv0$),
McDiarmid's inequality and a union bound over all $mn$ pairs give
$\max_x|\langle x,s-F^*\rangle|\le NK\,t_\vartheta$ with probability $1-\vartheta$, where
\begin{gather*}
t_\vartheta=\max_{u,i}\Big(\tfrac12\textstyle\sum_e\bar c_e(u,i)^2\ln(2mn/\vartheta)\Big)^{1/2},\\
\bar c_e(u,i)=b\,c_e(u,i)+a\,C_{ui}\,\varepsilon_{ui}\,\mathbb 1[e=(u,i)]/\tau ,
\end{gather*}
$c_e(u,i)$ is the constant of Theorem~\ref{thm:var}(d) for the three-hop term
and the second part is the sensitivity of the one-hop term. The union runs over
all pairs because the candidate set depends on the log. For logged-graph propagation,
$\E s=F^*(P\odot Y)$, and the last term does not vanish.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Proofs}\label{sec:structproofs}

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:affine} and Theorem~\ref{thm:cf}.}
With frozen nuisances, substitute
$G=G^{(-u)}+\tilde w_u^\top\tilde w_u-\diag(\tilde w_u^2)+\diag(c_u^2w_u)$
into~\eqref{eq:local}. The terms $\tilde w_{ui}\sum_j\tilde w_{uj}^2$ cancel against the
local correction, leaving $\tilde w_{ui}\sum_jc_{uj}^2w_{uj}$, and every remaining term
involving $w_{uj}$ ($j\neq i$) is linear, with coefficient
$c_{uj}G^{(-u)}_{ji}+c_{ui}w_{ui}c_{uj}^2$ in $\hat T_{ui}$. Since $O_{ui}=0$, the one-hop
term $a\tW_{ui}$ does not involve $w_{uj}$, so the coefficient in $s_{ui}=a\tW_{ui}+b\hat T_{ui}$
is $\omega_j$. Un-logging $j$ replaces $w_{uj}$ by
$\hat Y_{uj}$, which changes $s_{ui}$ by $\omega_j(w_{uj}-\hat Y_{uj})$; the game is additive, so
its Shapley values are the individual effects. For Theorem~\ref{thm:cf}, the
margin after removing $S$ is $s_{ui}-s_{uk}-\sum_{j\in S}g_j$, so minimising $|S|$
under $\sum_Sg_j>$ margin selects the largest $g_j$. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:robust}.}
Adding row $v$ adds $\mathrm{contrib}_v$ to $G$ and leaves $u$'s local terms
unchanged, so $\Delta=\tilde w_u\,\mathrm{contrib}_v=(a\cdot x)x-a\odot x^2+a\odot c_v\odot x$.
A logged DR entry satisfies $w\in[\hat Y-\hat Y/\tau,\hat Y+(1-\hat Y)/\tau]$ for any label
and any $p\ge\tau$, and an unlogged one equals $\hat Y$. $D$ is maximised
(minimised) by assigning the $L$ slots to the largest positive (negative)
deviations, and $x_k$ ranges over the values of a logged or unlogged entry.
Every feasible profile gives a point $(D,x_k)$ of the product of the two
intervals, so the extremes of $\Delta_k$ over this box bound its feasible
extremes; they are not attained in general, because the box treats $D$ and
$x_k$ as free although both depend on the same at most $L$ entries. For fixed
$x_k$ the effect is linear in $D$, so its extremes over the box are at the ends
of the $D$ interval; for fixed $D$ it is quadratic in $x_k$, with extremes at the
ends or at the clamped vertex. With frozen nuisances $G$ is a sum of
per-user contributions, so effects add over fake users. For
unclipped IPS, $|W_e|=1/p_e$ is unbounded. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:dp}.}
Because the population-level nuisances are public and every user-level part
depends only on the user's own row, adding or removing user $v$ changes $G$ by
$\mathrm{contrib}_v$ alone. With the row scaling,
$\|\mathrm{contrib}_v\|_F\le\|\tilde w_v\tilde w_v^\top-\diag(\tilde w_v^2)\|_F+\|c_v^2w_v\|_2\le R^2+R^2$,
so the $\ell_2$-sensitivity of the upper triangle is at most $2R^2$. The analytic
Gaussian mechanism is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP for every $\epsilon>0$ exactly when
$\Phi(\frac{\Delta}{2\sigma}-\frac{\epsilon\sigma}{\Delta})-e^\epsilon\Phi(-\frac{\Delta}{2\sigma}-\frac{\epsilon\sigma}{\Delta})\le\delta$
(Theorem~8 of~\citet{balle2018analytic}); we use the smallest such $\sigma$. The
classical calibration $\sigma=\Delta\sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}/\epsilon$~\citep{dwork2014algorithmic}
is only valid for $\epsilon<1$. Each user's output is a function of the release and
the user's own data, which gives joint DP by the billboard
lemma~\citep{hsu2014matchings}. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:alloc}.}
The allocation polytope is defined by the incidence matrix of the bipartite
user--candidate graph with degree bounds, which is totally unimodular, so the
linear relaxation has an integral optimal vertex and min-cost flow finds it.
Rounding changes each score by at most $h/2$ with $h=(\max s-\min s)/L$. If
$x^\star$ is optimal for $s$ and $\hat x$ for the rounded scores $\tilde s$, then
$\langle x^\star,s\rangle\le\langle x^\star,\tilde s\rangle+NKh/2\le\langle\hat x,\tilde s\rangle+NKh/2\le\langle\hat x,s\rangle+NKh$, so
$\Gamma\le NKh$. Each item is recommended at most $\max_i\mathrm{cap}_i$ times and $NK$
slots are filled, which gives the coverage bound. With $x_F$ optimal for $U$,
$U(\hat x)\ge\langle\hat x,s\rangle-|\langle\hat x,s-F^*\rangle|\ge\langle x_F,s\rangle-\Gamma-|\langle\hat x,s-F^*\rangle|
\ge U(x_F)-\Gamma-2\max_x|\langle x,s-F^*\rangle|$. For the concentration step,
$s_{ui}=a\tW_{ui}+b\hat T_{ui}$ is a function of the independent exposures.
Changing $O_e$ moves $b\hat T_{ui}$ by at most $b\,c_e(u,i)$ (proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:var}(d)) and, for $e=(u,i)$, moves $a\tW_{ui}$ by at most
$aC_{ui}\varepsilon_{ui}/\tau$, so $s_{ui}$ has bounded differences $\bar c_e(u,i)$. If
$\E s=F^*$, McDiarmid's inequality gives
$\Pr(|s_{ui}-F^*_{ui}|\ge t)\le2\exp(-2t^2/\sum_e\bar c_e(u,i)^2)$. The candidate set
depends on $O$, so we take a union bound over all $mn$ pairs: with probability
$1-\delta$, $|s_{ui}-F^*_{ui}|\le t_\delta$ for every pair, in particular for every
candidate, and an allocation sums $NK$ such terms. \qed

\section{Structural properties on real data}\label{sec:struct}

The properties of Section~\ref{sec:structth} hold for the model with frozen
(explanations, certificates) or public (privacy) nuisances, and for any linear
operator; we check them for DRUP and report where they are informative.

\paragraph{Explanations} Attributions recomputed against full re-propagation
are exact to floating-point precision (relative error $10^{-16}$ in float64 on
Coat and Yahoo!\,R3, $3\cdot10^{-6}$ in float32 on KuaiRec; Table~\ref{tab:explain}), and removing the
interactions with the largest attribution moves the top item further down than
removing random or similar ones, so the attributions are faithful. The
counterfactual analysis mostly certifies that no explanation from the user's
own data exists: on Coat 94\% of the top recommendations cannot be overturned by removing any subset of the user's own interactions, on KuaiRec 52\%, and on Yahoo!\,R3 10\%; the minimal explanation otherwise has a median size of 2, 7 and 1 interactions. Such recommendations rest on population-level
evidence (through $G$), which the attribution to a user's own interactions does
not explain.

\paragraph{Robustness} No certificate was violated in any run
(Table~\ref{tab:attack}). With imputation-based degrees, DR adjacency and DRUP
certify every Coat user against one or two fake profiles and 93--95\% of users on
Coat and KuaiRec against five, and realised attacks with up to 100 fake users
placed the target in a KuaiRec top-20 for at most 0.3\% of users under IPS, DR adjacency and
DRUP (2.7\% for the logged graph). Certificates are informative only
for small budgets: at $F=20$ they cover 10--26\% of users, and at 200 fake users
the target reaches 39\% of KuaiRec users under DR and DRUP, more than under IPS
(10\%) and the logged graph (29\%). On Yahoo!\,R3, where DRUP selects IPS edges with
cross-fitted $W$-degrees, the fake users' degrees are attacker-controlled and no
user is certified. When the nuisances are re-fitted on the poisoned log, which
the certificates do not cover, none of the certified user--target pairs of DRUP (5{,}196 on Coat, 2{,}821 on KuaiRec)
was violated, and the realised hit rates were lower than with frozen nuisances
(Table~\ref{tab:refit} in the additional results section), because the fake users' own
imputation raises their degree and lowers their weight.

\paragraph{Privacy} Table~\ref{tab:privacy} reports the jointly private variant of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dp}, in which only public users inform the population-level
nuisances and the score constants $c_1,c_3$. The configuration is fixed a
priori (clip 0.1, ridge 20, additive imputation, $\alpha=0.5$, control-variate
weight 1, $\beta=1$), not selected on the test data. Useful privacy needs a large
user base and a weak guarantee, and the public-nuisance model can itself be the
limit. On KuaiRec, with 717 public users, private DRUP reaches nDCG@20 of 0.618
at $\epsilon=16$ and 0.598 at $\epsilon=8$, against 0.634 without noise, 0.637
with all nuisances and 0.577 for popularity; it falls below popularity for
$\epsilon\le4$. On Coat (29 public users) the noise-free model reaches 0.548,
only just above popularity (0.527), and it is below popularity at every finite
$\epsilon$ (0.438 at $\epsilon=16$). On Yahoo!\,R3 the noise-free model with
public nuisances reaches 0.492 and so is below popularity (0.525) at every
$\epsilon$, although the non-private model with all nuisances reaches 0.652:
1{,}540 public users do not estimate the item parameters of a 2\%-dense log. The
privacy variant is therefore useful only on the largest log. Privatising the
nuisance models with a composed budget is left open.

\subsection{Exposure-capped allocation}\label{sec:rerank}

Each item may be recommended to at most $c$ times its fair share $NK/n$ of users.
Cells: nDCG@$K$ (Gini@$K$). Every cap held; the full range of caps and the
accuracy--concentration frontier are in the additional results section.}\label{tab:rerank}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\textwidth}
\input{../tables/rerank_compact.tex}
\end{adjustbox}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:rerank} applies the exposure-capped allocation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:alloc} to every operator. The min-cost-flow solver returns the
optimum up to its rounding gap (a relative gap below $4\cdot10^{-5}$ in every run), all
caps hold, and coverage never falls below the guaranteed $NK/\max\mathrm{cap}$. On KuaiRec, where exposure is extremely concentrated,
DR adjacency and DRUP are the most accurate operators under every cap, for
example 0.199 against 0.173 (logged graph) and 0.178 (IPS) at $c=5$, and 0.140
against 0.115 and 0.121 at $c=2$; the two agree to the third decimal under
every cap, so the correction does not change the allocation either. Under the same cap, however, their lists
remain more concentrated (Gini 0.80 against 0.72 at $c=5$), and at equal Gini
the frontier over each operator's grid (Table~\ref{tab:frontier}) favours the
correlation-based operators on Coat. On Coat, whose random test items are already
diverse, capping costs DRUP more accuracy than it costs the logged graph; on
Yahoo!\,R3 exposure is not concentrated (Gini 0.27) and caps down to twice the
fair share cost every operator at most 0.002. The allocation is thus the
mechanism that controls concentration, and the DR graph is the better input to
it when exposure in the log is very uneven.
