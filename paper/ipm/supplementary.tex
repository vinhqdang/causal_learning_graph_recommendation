\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

Throughout, expectations are conditional on $Y$, and ``walk'' means a
three-hop walk $u\!-\!j\!-\!v\!-\!i$ unless stated otherwise. Its edges are
$(u,j)$, $(v,j)$ and $(v,i)$.

\paragraph{Equations~\eqref{eq:drup} and~\eqref{eq:local}.}
Split the plug-in sum $(\tW\tW^\top\tW)_{ui}=\sum_{j,v}\tW_{uj}\tW_{vj}\tW_{vi}$ by walk
type (Figure~\ref{fig:walks}): (a) $v\ne u$, $j\ne i$; (b) $v=u$, $j\ne i$, giving
$\sum_{j\ne i}\tW_{uj}^2\tW_{ui}$; (c) $v\ne u$, $j=i$, giving
$\sum_{v\ne u}\tW_{ui}\tW_{vi}^2$; (d) $v=u$, $j=i$, giving $\tW_{ui}^3$. The
corrected estimator keeps (a) and replaces (b)--(d) by
$\sum_{j\ne i}C_{uj}^2W_{uj}\tW_{ui}$, $\sum_{v\ne u}\tW_{ui}C_{vi}^2W_{vi}$ and
$C_{ui}^3W_{ui}$. The difference between plug-in and corrected sums is
$\tW_{ui}\sum_{j\ne i}\Delta_{uj}+\tW_{ui}\sum_{v\ne u}\Delta_{vi}+(\tW_{ui}^3-C_{ui}^3W_{ui})
=\tW_{ui}(r_u-\Delta_{ui})+\tW_{ui}(\kappa_i-\Delta_{ui})+(\tW_{ui}^3-C_{ui}^3W_{ui})$,
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:drup}. For Eq.~\eqref{eq:local},
$(\tilde w_uG)_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\tW_{uj}(\tW^\top\tW)_{ji}+\tW_{ui}\sum_vC_{vi}^2W_{vi}
=(\tW\tW^\top\tW)_{ui}-\tW_{ui}\kappa_i+\tW_{ui}\sum_vC_{vi}^2W_{vi}$, and substituting
into Eq.~\eqref{eq:drup} cancels the $\kappa_i$ terms. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased}.}
By the decomposition above, the corrected estimator is a sum over walks of
$\prod_{e}C_e^{k_e}W_e$, the product running over the \emph{distinct} edges of the
walk with multiplicities $k_e$. Under Assumptions~\ref{as:unconf}--\ref{as:fixed}
the $W_e$ of distinct edges are independent, so the expectation factorises,
with $\E W_e=\hat Y_e+p_e(Y_e-\hat Y_e)/\bar p_e=Y_e+\xi_e$. The target walk
monomial is $\prod_eC_e^{k_e}Y_e^{k_e}=\prod_eC_e^{k_e}Y_e$ because
$Y_e\in\{0,1\}$. The one-hop term is unbiased edge by edge. \qed

\paragraph{Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}.}
Given $O_{ui}=0$, $W_{ui}=\hat Y_{ui}$ is deterministic and the other edge
estimates keep their distribution (Assumption~\ref{as:unconf}). The edge $(u,i)$
belongs to the one-hop term and to exactly the walks of types (b), (c) and (d).
In each of them it contributes $C_{ui}^{k}\hat Y_{ui}$ instead of $C_{ui}^kY_{ui}$,
and every other distinct edge contributes its mean $Y_e$. After the idempotent
reduction, $F^*_{ui}$ is affine in $Y_{ui}$, so
$\E[s_{ui}\mid O_{ui}=0]=F^*_{ui}|_{Y_{ui}\leftarrow\hat Y_{ui}}=F^*_{ui}-(Y_{ui}-\hat Y_{ui})\,\partial F^*_{ui}/\partial Y_{ui}$.
The derivative collects $aC_{ui}$ from the one-hop term,
$bC_{ui}\sum_{j\ne i}C_{uj}^2Y_{uj}$ from type (b),
$bC_{ui}\sum_{v\ne u}C_{vi}^2Y_{vi}$ from type (c) and $bC_{ui}^3$ from type (d).
For IPS edges, $W_{ui}=0$ given $O_{ui}=0$. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:khop}.}
Fix $u$ and $i$. For an assignment $\varphi$ of values to the free variables, let
$\pi(\varphi)$ be its equality pattern. Two walk edges $(U_s,J_t)$ and
$(U_{s'},J_{t'})$ coincide if and only if $U_s,U_{s'}$ share a user block and
$J_t,J_{t'}$ share an item block, so the distinct edges and their
multiplicities $k_e$ depend on $\pi(\varphi)$ only. For a pattern $\pi$, let
$F_\pi(\varphi)=\prod_\epsilon X^{(k_\epsilon)}_{\varphi(\epsilon)}$, the product over the
distinct edges $\epsilon$ of $\pi$, with $X^{(k)}=C^{\odot k}\odot W$ (corrected) or
$X^{(k)}=\tW^{\odot k}$ (plug-in). The plug-in $K$-hop product equals
$\sum_\pi S^{\mathrm{plug}}_\pi$ and the corrected estimator is
$\sum_\pi S^{\mathrm{corr}}_\pi$, where $S_\pi=\sum_{\varphi:\pi(\varphi)=\pi}F_\pi(\varphi)$. The two
coincide for patterns without a repeated edge, which gives the form of
$\hat T^{(K)}$ once each $S_\pi$ is written through unconstrained sums. For
$\sigma\ge\pi$ let $g_\pi(\sigma)=\sum_{\varphi:\pi(\varphi)\ge\sigma}F_\pi(\varphi)$: variables in the
same block of $\sigma$ are equal and values of different blocks are unrestricted,
so $g_\pi(\sigma)$ is a single tensor contraction over the blocks of $\sigma$ (with
the blocks of $U_0$ and $J_{r+1}$ fixed to $u$ and $i$). Grouping the terms of
$g_\pi(\sigma)$ by their exact pattern gives $g_\pi(\sigma)=\sum_{\tau\ge\sigma}f_\pi(\tau)$ with
$f_\pi(\tau)=\sum_{\varphi:\pi(\varphi)=\tau}F_\pi(\varphi)$. Möbius inversion on the interval
$[\pi,\hat1]$ of the product of the two partition lattices~\citep{rota1964} yields
$S_\pi=f_\pi(\pi)=\sum_{\sigma\ge\pi}\mu(\pi,\sigma)\,g_\pi(\sigma)$, where
$\mu(\pi,\sigma)=\prod_{B}(-1)^{b_B-1}(b_B-1)!$ over the blocks $B$ of $\sigma$ in both
lattices and $b_B$ is the number of blocks of $\pi$ merged into $B$. Finally, for
every $\varphi$ with $\pi(\varphi)=\pi$ the distinct edges are independent, so
$\E F^{\mathrm{corr}}_\pi(\varphi)=\prod_\epsilon C_{\varphi(\epsilon)}^{k_\epsilon}Y_{\varphi(\epsilon)}$, which is the
corresponding monomial of $(\tY\tY^\top)^r\tY$ since $Y$ is binary. The number of
contraction terms is the number of pairs $(\pi,\sigma)$ with $\sigma\ge\pi$ and $\pi$
containing a repeated edge, which is 5, 99 and 2{,}790 for $K=3,5,7$. \qed

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:dep}.}
With correct propensities $\E W_e=Y_e$, and for distinct $e,f$ in the same row
$\mathrm{Cov}(W_e,W_f)=(Y_e-\hat Y_e)(Y_f-\hat Y_f)\,\mathrm{Cov}(O_e,O_f)/(p_ep_f)$, so
$|\mathrm{Cov}(W_e,W_f)|\le\rho\,\varepsilon_e\varepsilon_f$. In a type (a) walk the edge $(u,j)$
lies in row $u$ and the edges $(v,j),(v,i)$ in row $v\ne u$; rows are independent,
so $\E[W_{uj}W_{vj}W_{vi}]=Y_{uj}(Y_{vj}Y_{vi}+\mathrm{Cov}(W_{vj},W_{vi}))$ and the bias of
the walk is at most $C_{uj}C_{vj}C_{vi}\rho\,\varepsilon_{vj}\varepsilon_{vi}$. A corrected type (b)
walk contributes $C_{uj}^2C_{ui}W_{uj}W_{ui}$ with two edges in row $u$, with bias
at most $C_{uj}^2C_{ui}\rho\,\varepsilon_{uj}\varepsilon_{ui}$. Type (c) walks have their two
distinct edges in different rows and type (d) walks a single edge, so they are
unbiased. Summing gives the bound. For $k$ of $n$ items drawn uniformly,
$\Pr(O_e=O_f=1)=k(k-1)/(n(n-1))$, so
$|\mathrm{Cov}(O_e,O_f)|/(p_ep_f)=(n-k)/(k(n-1))\le1/k$. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}.}
By the decomposition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:drup}, the plug-in minus the corrected
operator is $\tW_{ui}\sum_{j\ne i}\Delta_{uj}+\tW_{ui}\sum_{v\ne u}\Delta_{vi}+(\tW_{ui}^3-C_{ui}^3W_{ui})$.
Given $O_{ui}=0$, $\tW_{ui}=C_{ui}\hat Y_{ui}$ is deterministic and the other
$\Delta_e$ keep their distribution. With correct propensities and binary $Y$,
$\E\Delta_e=C_e^2(\E W_e^2-\E W_e)=C_e^2(\Var W_e+Y_e^2-Y_e)=C_e^2V_e$ with
$V_e=\frac{1-p_e}{p_e}\varepsilon_e^2$, which gives the stated bias, the last term being
$C_{ui}^3(\hat Y_{ui}^3-\hat Y_{ui})$. Part (a) uses $(1-p)/p\ge1/\max_vp_{vi}-1$. For
(b), with $\mu_e=\E W_e\in[0,1]$, $\E W_e^2-\E W_e=\Var W_e-\mu_e(1-\mu_e)\le\Var W_e
=p_e(1-p_e)\varepsilon_e^2/\bar p_e^2$. If $p_e\ge\tau$ this is
$\frac{1-p_e}{p_e}\varepsilon_e^2\le(1/\tau-1)\varepsilon_e^2$; if $p_e<\tau\le1/2$ it is
$p_e(1-p_e)\varepsilon_e^2/\tau^2<\tau(1-\tau)\varepsilon_e^2/\tau^2$ because $p(1-p)$ increases on
$[0,1/2]$. At $p_e=\tau$, $\mu_e=Y_e$ and the bound is attained; setting all
$p_{vi}=\tau$ gives a bias $C_{ui}\hat Y_{ui}(1/\tau-1)\sum_{v\ne u}C_{vi}^2\varepsilon_{vi}^2$, so the
worst case is $\Theta(1/\tau)$. For (c), IPS gives $\tW_{ui}=0$ and $C_{ui}^3W_{ui}=0$
given $O_{ui}=0$. \qed

\subsection*{Statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:var}}
\begin{theorem}[Variance, clipping and concentration]\label{thm:var}
Let $\Lambda_e(u,i)$ be the $C$-weighted mass of the walks from $u$ to $i$ through
edge $e$, and $B=\max(1,1/\tau)$. Then
(a) $\Var(W_e)\le\varepsilon_e^2/(4\tau^2)$, and $\Var(W_e)\le\varepsilon_e^2/\tau$ if $\hat p=p$;
(b) if $\hat p=p$, $\Var(\hat T_{ui})\le\frac{(1+1/\tau)^2}{\tau}\sum_e\varepsilon_e^2\Lambda_e(u,i)^2$;
(c) if $\hat p=p$, so that clipping is the only source of bias,
$|\E\hat T_{ui}-T^*_{ui}|\le3\bar\xi(1+\bar\xi)^2\sum_{j,v}C_{uj}C_{vj}C_{vi}$ with
$\bar\xi=\max_e(1-p_e/\tau)_+\varepsilon_e$;
(d) for any $\hat p$, $\Pr(|\hat T_{ui}-\E\hat T_{ui}|\ge t)\le2\exp(-2t^2/\sum_ec_e^2)$ with
$c_e=\varepsilon_eB^2\Lambda_e(u,i)/\tau$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:var}.}
(a) $\Var(W_e)=p_e(1-p_e)\varepsilon_e^2/\bar p_e^2\le\varepsilon_e^2/(4\tau^2)$ for any $\hat p$,
since $p(1-p)\le1/4$ and $\bar p_e\ge\tau$. If $\hat p=p$, then $\bar p=\max(p,\tau)$: if
$p_e\ge\tau$ the variance is at most $(1/\tau-1)\varepsilon_e^2$, otherwise at most
$p_e\varepsilon_e^2/\tau^2<\varepsilon_e^2/\tau$. Without $\hat p=p$ the bound $\varepsilon_e^2/\tau$ can
fail (e.g., $p=0.5$, $\hat p=0.01$, $\tau=0.05$ gives $100\,\varepsilon_e^2>20\,\varepsilon_e^2$).
(b) $\hat T_{ui}$ is multilinear in the independent $W_e$ and hence affine in each
of them. The Efron--Stein inequality~\citep{efron1981jackknife} gives
$\Var(\hat T)\le\sum_e\E[(\partial_e\hat T)^2]\Var(W_e)$. The derivative $\partial_e\hat T$
is a sum over the walks through $e$ of $C$-weights times at most two other
independent $W_f$, and with $\hat p=p$, $\mu_f=Y_f\in[0,1]$ and
$\E W_f^2=\mu_f^2+\Var W_f\le1+1/\tau$ by (a). Minkowski's
inequality bounds $\|\partial_e\hat T\|_2\le(1+1/\tau)\Lambda_e$.
(c) By Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased}, the bias is at most
$\sum_{\text{walks}}|\kappa_{\text{walk}}|\,[\prod_f(Y_f+|\xi_f|)-\prod_fY_f]\le((1+\bar\xi)^3-1)\sum_{j,v}C_{uj}C_{vj}C_{vi}$,
and $(1+x)^3-1\le3x(1+x)^2$. With $\hat p=p$, $|\xi_e|=(1-p_e/\tau)_+\varepsilon_e$.
(d) Changing $O_e$ moves $W_e$ by at most $\varepsilon_e/\tau$. Every edge estimate
satisfies $|W_f|\le B$, so $|\partial_e\hat T|\le B^2\Lambda_e$, and McDiarmid's
inequality~\citep{mcdiarmid1989} applies. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:fair}.}
By Definition~\ref{def:elast} the estimator uses the post-intervention
propensities, and if $\pi p\ge\tau$ the clip does not bind, so they remain
correct. DRUP: by Theorem~\ref{thm:unbiased}, $\E[s]=F^*$,
and by Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}, $\E[s\mid O_{ui}=0]$ is $F^*$ with $Y_{ui}$ replaced by
the fixed $\hat Y_{ui}$; neither depends on $\pi$. Logged graph with fixed $C$: $\E[O_eY_e]=p_eY_e$,
and every walk that ends at $i$ contains $k\ge1$ edges incident to $i$, so its
expectation scales as $\pi^k$ and $\eta_i$ is an average of such $k$. Uncorrected
DR: by Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}, $\E[s_{ui}\mid O_{ui}=0]$ equals the DRUP target plus
$C_{ui}\hat Y_{ui}(R_u^{(i)}+K_i(\pi))$ minus a term free of $\pi$; $R_u^{(i)}$ involves
no edge of item $i$, and $K_i(\pi)=\sum_{v\ne u}C_{vi}^2\frac{1-\pi p_{vi}}{\pi p_{vi}}\varepsilon_{vi}^2$ is
strictly decreasing in $\pi$. \qed

\section{Experimental details}\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Training-free operators}
All operators share the score $s=s_1/c_1+\beta s_3/c_3$ (Section~\ref{sec:drup}) with
$\beta\in\{0,0.03,0.1,0.3,1,3,10\}$ or the three-hop term alone; the five-hop
variants add $\gamma s_5/c_5$ with $\gamma\in\{0.03,0.1,0.3,1,3\}$. The grids are:
normalisation exponent $\alpha\in\{0.3,0.5,0.7\}$ (Coat), $\{0.5,0.7\}$
(Yahoo!\,R3, KuaiRand) and $\{0.3,0.5\}$ (KuaiRec); clip
$\tau\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ (Coat), $\{0.1,0.2\}$ (Yahoo!\,R3),
$\{0.02,0.05,0.1\}$ (KuaiRand) and $\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ (KuaiRec); imputation ridge
$\lambda\in\{1,5,20\}$ (Coat), $\{20\}$ (Yahoo!\,R3, KuaiRand) and $\{5,20\}$
(KuaiRec); additive or low-rank (rank 32) imputation; control-variate weight
$\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1\}$ on Yahoo!\,R3, $\{0,0.5,1\}$ on KuaiRand and $1$ elsewhere; and degree source $\in\{\hat Y,\ \text{cross-fitted }W\}$ for
the debiased operators. The degree source is a searched hyper-parameter.
$\hat Y$-degrees satisfy Assumption~\ref{as:fixed} whenever $\hat Y$ does;
cross-fitted $W$-degrees do not, since the weight at a pair then depends on
the exposures of other pairs, so configurations with $W$-degrees (selected on
Yahoo!\,R3) lie outside the theorems. Only $\hat Y$-degrees make the
certificates of Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} informative. EASE uses
$\lambda\in\{10,100,1000\}$; GF-CF uses four settings of (ideal low-pass weight, singular vectors):
$(0,0)$, $(0.3,64)$, $(0.3,256)$ and $(1,256)$. BSPM~\citep{choi2023bspm} uses blurring time
$\in\{1,2\}$, sharpening time $\in\{0,0.1,0.3\}$ and ideal low-pass weight
$\in\{0,0.3\}$ with one Euler step each. On the DR graph, EASE, GF-CF and BSPM
search the same nuisance grid as DR adjacency. The sample-split variants are
run for five draws of the split-off pairs; each draw is tuned separately, and
the tables report the mean over draws. The propensity model is
the one shipped with Coat and a logistic exposure model on user and item
exposure rates elsewhere; on Yahoo!\,R3 it was selected on validation data
against an outcome-dependent Naive-Bayes model. All nuisances are cross-fitted
over ten random folds of pairs, except for the sample-split variants, whose
imputation (and propensity model, where it is estimated) is fitted on a random
20\% of the pairs.

\paragraph{Trained models}
The grid combines the learning rates $10^{-2}$, $3\cdot10^{-3}$ and $10^{-3}$ with the
weight decays $10^{-5}$, $10^{-4}$ and (Coat, and the MF models on Yahoo!\,R3)
$10^{-3}$. Embeddings have 64 dimensions (Coat: 32 or 64). The graph models with
the widest search (LightGCN and r-AdjNorm on Yahoo!\,R3, the pointwise
and BPR LightGCN on KuaiRec, all graph models on Coat) also search one to three
layers and 64 or 128 dimensions (Coat: 32 or 64); the others use two layers.
PDA searches $\gamma\in\{0.1,0.2\}$, r-AdjNorm $r\in\{0.3,0.7\}$, SimGCL the
contrastive weight $\in\{0.1,0.5\}$ (noise $0.1$, temperature $0.2$, InfoNCE over at
most 2{,}048 users and items of a batch) and MACR $c\in\{0.1,0.3\}$; iALS searches
the confidence $\alpha\in\{3,10,30\}$ and the ridge $\in\{1,10,100,1000\}$. DR-JL and MRDR
learn an MF imputation model jointly with the prediction
model~\citep{wang2019drjl,guo2021mrdr}. BPR models draw one uniformly sampled
negative per positive. Batch sizes are 256 (Coat), 1{,}024 (Yahoo!\,R3), 8{,}192
(KuaiRand) and 32{,}768 (KuaiRec, with 2M sampled pairs per epoch for the
pointwise losses); training runs for at most 100, 60, 40 and 30 epochs. Because
the training log is the same for every split, each configuration is trained
once and evaluated after every epoch on the validation part of every split.
Each split stops on its own validation score (patience 5 on Coat and
Yahoo!\,R3, 3 otherwise) and selects its own configuration and epoch; training
continues until every split has stopped, and a split never uses epochs after
its own stopping point, so no split's selection depends on another split's
validation users, who may be its test users. The split-0 selection is
re-trained with three (KuaiRec, KuaiRand: two) further seeds to measure seed
variance. We did not run DICE, StableDR and methods that train on the uniform
data (CausE, AutoDebias): in our protocol the uniform data serve only for
selection and testing, for every method. Table~\ref{tab:configs} lists the
number of configurations each method was selected from.

\paragraph{Evaluation and tests}
Coat splits each user's candidates (30\% validation), Yahoo!\,R3, KuaiRand and
KuaiRec split the test users (30\% validation), over 10, 5, 5 and 5 random splits.
Users without a positive candidate are skipped. For a pair of methods, each
test user's metric difference is averaged over the splits in which the user
is in the test part, and we report the mean difference over users with a
95\% $t$-interval and the paired $t$-test. The Holm family is every comparison
run for a dataset, with all references together (DRUP, DR adjacency, five-hop
DRUP and DRUP-split against every other method); the Wilcoxon signed-rank
$p$-values are adjusted over the same family. Within-reference adjustments are
in the result files. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives the same conclusions except for the
five-hop DRUP against DR comparison on KuaiRec ($p=0.66$ against 0.011) and for
DRUP-split against pointwise LightGCN on KuaiRec ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.48$ against $3\cdot10^{-4}$). The
corrected resampled $t$-test over split means~\citep{nadeau2003} is far more
conservative with five or ten splits and is reported in the result files.
All tests condition on the training log and the fitted nuisances.

\paragraph{Hardware and code}
All experiments ran on four CPU cores with 16\,GB of memory, except the KuaiRand
debiased and sample-split operators, which ran on a Google Colab T4 GPU (same code and
seeds; per-user ranking metrics are computed once per score matrix and match the
per-user routine to $10^{-15}$). GF-CF uses a randomised SVD, so its scores vary by
about 0.002 between runs.
Code, configurations and result files are available at the repository given

\section{Structural properties of linear propagation}\label{sec:structth}

The results of this section use only that, given the nuisances, $s$ is a
fixed linear function of the edge estimates. They hold for DRUP and equally for
the uncorrected operators and for linear LightGCN, and they describe the
recommender with its nuisances held fixed.

\begin{proposition}[Affine in the user's own data]\label{prop:affine}
Fix $u$ and a candidate $i$ with $O_{ui}=0$, and let $G^{(-u)}$ be $G$ without
$u$'s own contribution. With frozen nuisances,
$s_{ui}=\mathrm{const}+\sum_{j\neq i}\omega_jw_{uj}$ with
$\omega_j=b\big(c_{uj}G^{(-u)}_{ji}+c_{ui}w_{ui}c_{uj}^2\big)$.
Hence un-logging interaction $j$, i.e., replacing $w_{uj}$ by its unlogged
value $\hat Y_{uj}$, changes $s_{ui}$ by exactly $\phi_j=\omega_j(w_{uj}-\hat Y_{uj})$,
removal effects add up over any subset, and completeness holds exactly.
\end{proposition}
Because the removal game is additive, the $\phi_j$ are also its Shapley values;
this is a consequence of linearity, and the attribution is relative to the
stated baseline~\citep{kumar2020shapley}.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal minimal counterfactual explanation]\label{thm:cf}
For candidates $i,k$ with $s_{ui}>s_{uk}$, sorting $g_j=\phi^{(i)}_j-\phi^{(k)}_j$ in
decreasing order and removing interactions until the cumulative sum exceeds the
margin yields a minimum-cardinality set whose removal makes $k$ outrank $i$.
If the positive $g_j$ do not sum above the margin, no such set exists. The
cost is $O(n+d_u\log d_u)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Certificates against injected profiles, frozen nuisances]\label{thm:robust}
With frozen nuisances, one injected user $v$ changes $u$'s three-hop score of item
$k$ by $\Delta_k=x_k(D+a_kc_{vk})-a_kx_k^2$, where $a=\tilde w_u$, $x=c_v\odot w_v$
and $D=\langle a,x\rangle$. If $v$ logs at most $L$ interactions with arbitrary
items, labels and propensities $\ge\tau$, then $D$ and $x_k$ lie in closed-form
intervals. $\Delta_k$ is linear in $D$ and quadratic in $x_k$, so its extremes
$[\underline\Delta_k,\overline\Delta_k]$ over the box formed by the two intervals
are attained at its edges or at the vertex of the quadratic. The box contains
every feasible $(D,x_k)$ but ignores that both are determined by the same $L$
interactions, so $[\underline\Delta_k,\overline\Delta_k]$ contains the feasible
range of $\Delta_k$ and is conservative rather than exact. Item $t$
provably stays out of $u$'s top-$K$ against any $F$ such users if
$s_{ut}+F\overline\Delta_t$ is below the $(K{+}1)$-th largest value of
$s_{uk}+F\underline\Delta_k$ over the other candidates. With unclipped IPS, the
influence of a single interaction is unbounded.
\end{theorem}
The same argument certifies the uncorrected operators (with
$\Delta_k=x_kD$). Degrees taken from $\hat Y$ make the fake user's weight $c_v$
known; degrees taken from $W$ would be attacker-controlled.

\begin{theorem}[Joint differential privacy with public nuisances]\label{thm:dp}
Let the population-level parts of the nuisances (the propensity model, the
item parameters of $\hat Y$, the item degrees in $C$ and the radius $R$), the
hyper-parameters and the constants $a,b$ of the score be fixed a priori or
computed from public data and from the release, and every user-level part
from the user's own row.
Scale each private row so that $\|\tilde w_v\|_2\le R$ and
$\|c_v^{\odot2}\odot w_v\|_2\le R^2$. Releasing $G$, computed over the private
rows, plus symmetric Gaussian noise of scale
$\sigma=\sigma_{\mathrm{aG}}(\epsilon,\delta;2R^2)$, the analytic Gaussian
calibration~\citep{balle2018analytic}, is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP under adding or
removing one user, for every $\epsilon>0$. Scoring by~\eqref{eq:local} uses only
the release and the user's own row, so the recommender is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-jointly
DP~\citep{hsu2014matchings}. Post-processing such as low-rank denoising is free.
\end{theorem}
Row scaling changes the estimate for users whose rows exceed $R$, so
Theorems~\ref{thm:unbiased} and~\ref{thm:fair} hold for the private variant only
up to this shrinkage.

\begin{theorem}[Exposure-capped allocation]\label{thm:alloc}
Let $N$ users each receive $K$ of their candidates and let item $i$ be
recommended to at most $\mathrm{cap}_i$ users. Let $\hat x$ maximise $\langle x,s\rangle$
over such allocations. The constraint matrix is totally unimodular, so the
linear relaxation has an integral optimum, and min-cost flow on the scores
rounded to a grid of $L$ levels finds $\hat x$ exactly up to the rounding gap
$\Gamma\le NK(\max s-\min s)/L$. All caps hold, at least $NK/\max_i\mathrm{cap}_i$
items are recommended, and for the full-exposure utility $U(x)=\langle x,F^*\rangle$,
\[
U(\hat x)\ \ge\ \max_{x}U(x)-\Gamma-2\max_x|\langle x,s-F^*\rangle| .
\]
If $\E s=F^*$ (DRUP under Assumption~\ref{as:fixed} with $\xi\equiv0$),
McDiarmid's inequality and a union bound over all $mn$ pairs give
$\max_x|\langle x,s-F^*\rangle|\le NK\,t_\vartheta$ with probability $1-\vartheta$, where
\begin{gather*}
t_\vartheta=\max_{u,i}\Big(\tfrac12\textstyle\sum_e\bar c_e(u,i)^2\ln(2mn/\vartheta)\Big)^{1/2},\\
\bar c_e(u,i)=b\,c_e(u,i)+a\,C_{ui}\,\varepsilon_{ui}\,\mathbb 1[e=(u,i)]/\tau ,
\end{gather*}
$c_e(u,i)$ is the constant of Theorem~\ref{thm:var}(d) for the three-hop term
and the second part is the sensitivity of the one-hop term. The union runs over
all pairs because the candidate set depends on the log. For logged-graph propagation,
$\E s=F^*(P\odot Y)$, and the last term does not vanish.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Proofs}\label{sec:structproofs}

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:affine} and Theorem~\ref{thm:cf}.}
With frozen nuisances, substitute
$G=G^{(-u)}+\tilde w_u^\top\tilde w_u-\diag(\tilde w_u^2)+\diag(c_u^2w_u)$
into~\eqref{eq:local}. The terms $\tilde w_{ui}\sum_j\tilde w_{uj}^2$ cancel against the
local correction, leaving $\tilde w_{ui}\sum_jc_{uj}^2w_{uj}$, and every remaining term
involving $w_{uj}$ ($j\neq i$) is linear, with coefficient
$c_{uj}G^{(-u)}_{ji}+c_{ui}w_{ui}c_{uj}^2$ in $\hat T_{ui}$. Since $O_{ui}=0$, the one-hop
term $a\tW_{ui}$ does not involve $w_{uj}$, so the coefficient in $s_{ui}=a\tW_{ui}+b\hat T_{ui}$
is $\omega_j$. Un-logging $j$ replaces $w_{uj}$ by
$\hat Y_{uj}$, which changes $s_{ui}$ by $\omega_j(w_{uj}-\hat Y_{uj})$; the game is additive, so
its Shapley values are the individual effects. For Theorem~\ref{thm:cf}, the
margin after removing $S$ is $s_{ui}-s_{uk}-\sum_{j\in S}g_j$, so minimising $|S|$
under $\sum_Sg_j>$ margin selects the largest $g_j$. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:robust}.}
Adding row $v$ adds $\mathrm{contrib}_v$ to $G$ and leaves $u$'s local terms
unchanged, so $\Delta=\tilde w_u\,\mathrm{contrib}_v=(a\cdot x)x-a\odot x^2+a\odot c_v\odot x$.
A logged DR entry satisfies $w\in[\hat Y-\hat Y/\tau,\hat Y+(1-\hat Y)/\tau]$ for any label
and any $p\ge\tau$, and an unlogged one equals $\hat Y$. $D$ is maximised
(minimised) by assigning the $L$ slots to the largest positive (negative)
deviations, and $x_k$ ranges over the values of a logged or unlogged entry.
Every feasible profile gives a point $(D,x_k)$ of the product of the two
intervals, so the extremes of $\Delta_k$ over this box bound its feasible
extremes; they are not attained in general, because the box treats $D$ and
$x_k$ as free although both depend on the same at most $L$ entries. For fixed
$x_k$ the effect is linear in $D$, so its extremes over the box are at the ends
of the $D$ interval; for fixed $D$ it is quadratic in $x_k$, with extremes at the
ends or at the clamped vertex. With frozen nuisances $G$ is a sum of
per-user contributions, so effects add over fake users. For
unclipped IPS, $|W_e|=1/p_e$ is unbounded. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:dp}.}
Because the population-level nuisances are public and every user-level part
depends only on the user's own row, adding or removing user $v$ changes $G$ by
$\mathrm{contrib}_v$ alone. With the row scaling,
$\|\mathrm{contrib}_v\|_F\le\|\tilde w_v\tilde w_v^\top-\diag(\tilde w_v^2)\|_F+\|c_v^2w_v\|_2\le R^2+R^2$,
so the $\ell_2$-sensitivity of the upper triangle is at most $2R^2$. The analytic
Gaussian mechanism is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP for every $\epsilon>0$ exactly when
$\Phi(\frac{\Delta}{2\sigma}-\frac{\epsilon\sigma}{\Delta})-e^\epsilon\Phi(-\frac{\Delta}{2\sigma}-\frac{\epsilon\sigma}{\Delta})\le\delta$
(Theorem~8 of~\citet{balle2018analytic}); we use the smallest such $\sigma$. The
classical calibration $\sigma=\Delta\sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}/\epsilon$~\citep{dwork2014algorithmic}
is only valid for $\epsilon<1$. Each user's output is a function of the release and
the user's own data, which gives joint DP by the billboard
lemma~\citep{hsu2014matchings}. \qed

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:alloc}.}
The allocation polytope is defined by the incidence matrix of the bipartite
user--candidate graph with degree bounds, which is totally unimodular, so the
linear relaxation has an integral optimal vertex and min-cost flow finds it.
Rounding changes each score by at most $h/2$ with $h=(\max s-\min s)/L$. If
$x^\star$ is optimal for $s$ and $\hat x$ for the rounded scores $\tilde s$, then
$\langle x^\star,s\rangle\le\langle x^\star,\tilde s\rangle+NKh/2\le\langle\hat x,\tilde s\rangle+NKh/2\le\langle\hat x,s\rangle+NKh$, so
$\Gamma\le NKh$. Each item is recommended at most $\max_i\mathrm{cap}_i$ times and $NK$
slots are filled, which gives the coverage bound. With $x_F$ optimal for $U$,
$U(\hat x)\ge\langle\hat x,s\rangle-|\langle\hat x,s-F^*\rangle|\ge\langle x_F,s\rangle-\Gamma-|\langle\hat x,s-F^*\rangle|
\ge U(x_F)-\Gamma-2\max_x|\langle x,s-F^*\rangle|$. For the concentration step,
$s_{ui}=a\tW_{ui}+b\hat T_{ui}$ is a function of the independent exposures.
Changing $O_e$ moves $b\hat T_{ui}$ by at most $b\,c_e(u,i)$ (proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:var}(d)) and, for $e=(u,i)$, moves $a\tW_{ui}$ by at most
$aC_{ui}\varepsilon_{ui}/\tau$, so $s_{ui}$ has bounded differences $\bar c_e(u,i)$. If
$\E s=F^*$, McDiarmid's inequality gives
$\Pr(|s_{ui}-F^*_{ui}|\ge t)\le2\exp(-2t^2/\sum_e\bar c_e(u,i)^2)$. The candidate set
depends on $O$, so we take a union bound over all $mn$ pairs: with probability
$1-\delta$, $|s_{ui}-F^*_{ui}|\le t_\delta$ for every pair, in particular for every
candidate, and an allocation sums $NK$ such terms. \qed

\subsection{Stakeholders and regulation}\label{sec:stake}
\emph{Item providers.} Exposure invariance means that an item's expected score does not
depend on how often it was observed; it is not equal exposure, and it comes with more
concentrated lists (Table~\ref{tab:fair}) that a retrained system could entrench.
Deployments should report concentration next to invariance and use an explicit allocation
mechanism. \emph{Users.} The removal attributions describe the model with its nuisances
frozen and are not an account of data deletion. \emph{Platforms and auditors.} The clip
$\tau$ and the control-variate weight $\lambda$ trade invariance against accuracy; one
option is to select the most accurate configuration subject to a bound on the elasticity
measured in a thinning audit, whose power is limited. The audit is a first-party
instrument: it needs the log, the propensities, the deployed hyper-parameters and the
ability to re-run the pipeline. Article~40 of the Digital Services
Act~\citep{dsa2022} gives vetted researchers access to data of very large online platforms
and search engines; whether it extends to re-running a proprietary pipeline is not settled
by the text, and we do not claim that it does. The Act's risk-assessment and audit
obligations (Articles~34--37, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/436~\citep{eu2024audit})
concern compliance of very large providers, not properties of estimators, and our results
can at most inform a risk assessment.
