\begin{letter}{Editor-in-Chief\\Information Processing \& Management}
\opening{Dear Editor,}

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Repeated-walk bias in debiased graph propagation for recommendation: an exact correction and its limits'', for publication in
\emph{Information Processing \& Management}.

Graph-based recommenders propagate preferences over interaction graphs that the
platform's own exposure policy has filtered. The manuscript shows that the
inverse-propensity and doubly robust corrections used inside graph propagation
are themselves biased once propagation spans more than one hop, gives the exact
bias, and removes it with a training-free operator (DRUP) that is exact for any
odd number of hops and, at three hops, costs no more than the uncorrected
operator. It states the guarantees that follow, with their assumptions: edge-wise double robustness, robustness to
within-user exposure dependence, and exposure invariance in expectation, plus
the properties that any linear operator with fixed nuisances shares (exact
attributions, certificates against injected profiles, joint differential
privacy with public nuisances, capped allocation). It also shows, by
simulation, that these guarantees require nuisances fitted independently of
the log: they hold for an independent log or a sample split, but not for
nuisances cross-fitted on the same log, and a sample-split variant is
evaluated alongside.

The evaluation is deliberately conservative. A semi-synthetic test on KuaiRec
with known exposure shows that the correction removes the 18--29\% overestimate
of the full-exposure utility of fixed allocations that uncorrected operators
produce. On Coat, Yahoo!\ R3, KuaiRand and KuaiRec, whose test data are random
or fully observed, DRUP is compared with up to 26 tuned baselines under one
protocol (per-split tuning and early stopping of every method, user-level tests
with Holm correction over all comparisons). The manuscript reports that the walk
correction does not change top-$K$ accuracy or rankings, so that its
contribution is at the level of scores; that the most accurate method differs
by dataset (iALS on Coat, BPR-trained graph models on Yahoo!\ R3 and KuaiRand,
training-free propagation over the doubly robust graph on KuaiRec); that the
sample split that the guarantees require costs accuracy on sparse logs; and that
a real-data thinning audit of exposure invariance has limited power, and the
selected configurations are not invariant where they use clipped inverse-propensity edges.

We believe the work fits the journal's interest in trustworthy information
access and recommender systems. The manuscript is original, has not been
published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. Code and
results are publicly available. In line with the double-anonymised review
policy, the manuscript file contains no identifying information; author details
and declarations are on the separate title page.
