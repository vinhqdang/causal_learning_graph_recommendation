\opening{Dear Editor,}

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Walk-unbiased doubly robust graph
propagation for recommendation from exposure-biased logs'', for publication in
\emph{Information Processing \& Management}.

Graph-based recommenders propagate preferences over interaction graphs that the
platform's own exposure policy has filtered. The manuscript shows that the
inverse-propensity and doubly robust corrections used inside graph propagation
are themselves biased once propagation spans more than one hop, gives the exact
bias, and removes it with an operator (DRUP) that is exact for any odd number
of hops at no extra cost and without training. It states the guarantees that
follow, with their assumptions: edge-wise double robustness, robustness to
within-user exposure dependence, and exposure invariance in expectation, plus
the properties that any linear operator with fixed nuisances shares (exact
attributions, certificates against injected profiles, joint differential
privacy with public nuisances, capped allocation).

The evaluation is deliberately conservative. On Coat, Yahoo!\ R3 and KuaiRec,
whose test data are random or fully observed, DRUP is compared with 22 methods
under one protocol (cross-fitted nuisances, per-split tuning of every method,
user-level tests with Holm correction). The manuscript reports that the walk
correction does not change top-$K$ accuracy, that training-free propagation over
the doubly robust graph is the most accurate method on KuaiRec while trained
graph models are better on Yahoo!\ R3, and that exposure invariance holds on
Coat and KuaiRec but not at the selected configuration on Yahoo!\ R3.

We believe the work fits the journal's interest in trustworthy information
access and recommender systems. The manuscript is original, has not been
published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. Code and
results are publicly available. In line with the double-anonymised review
policy, the manuscript file contains no identifying information; author details
and declarations are on the separate title page.

\closing{Sincerely,}
